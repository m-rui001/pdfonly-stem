Abstract (<125 words) The cycle life and energy density of rechargeable metal batteries have been limited by mossy/dendritic growth of their metal anodes (lithium, sodium or zinc). Using threedimensional cross-linked polyethylenimine lithium-ion-affinity sponge as host, we show that electrokinetic phenomena, including electrokinetic surface conduction and electroosmosis, lead to lithium-ion self-concentrating/pumping features within the highzeta-potential host that change concentration and current density profiles, enabling dendritic-free plating/stripping of lithium with high Coulombic efficiency at high deposition capacities and current densities, even at low temperature. The use of lithiumhosting sponge leads to a significantly improved cycling stability of lithium batteries using a limited amount of lithium at commercial-level areal capacity. We also observed improved morphology in sodium and zinc anodes, indicating a broader promise of this approach.

Page 2

Using metals as anodes in metal batteries has been realized in commercial low-cost batteries, such as zinc (Zn) metal batteries and lead acid batteries, and considered the most promising approach to achieve high energy density in next-generation batteries, such as lithium (Li) metal batteries (1). However, the highly reversible plating/stripping of metal, so pivotal for long cycle life, is compromised by mossy/dendritic growth that reduces Coulombic efficiency (CE) and thus leads to poor cycling performance. The alkaline metal anodes, such as Li and Na metal anodes, pose a particular challenge because deposition is associated with severe electrolyte decomposition due to their high reactivities, with ensuing non-uniform nucleation and severe growth of mossy/dendritic metal (2, 3). To date, considerable progress to address these issues, especially for Li metal anode, has emphasized the development of protective or interfacial layer (4-7), electrolyte additives to promote durable solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) (8, 9), alternative electrolytes (10, 11), and polymeric/lithiophilic anodic host (12-16). Although these methods assist in mitigating dendritic Li growth, it is still challenging to achieve dendritefree plating/stripping of Li with high CE at both high current density and high deposition capacity which is required to implement high energy and long cycling life of Li metal batteries. High current density induces very fast depletion of Li ions and in turn, leads to severe concentration polarization because of diffusion- and advection-limited ion transport in bulk electrolyte. This promotes fast growth of Li dendrite occuring by focusing the ionic flux on rapidly growing dendrite tips (17, 18). High Li deposition capacity accompanies vast volume change of Li metal, which leads to a continous cracking of SEI layers and formation of mossy/dendritic Li. At both conditions of high current density and high deposition capacity, morphology of depostied Li is further deteriorated, accompanied by

Page 3

increased surface area of deposited Li, propagated SEI formation and reduced CE of Li plating/stripping. Dendrite growth of metal deposition has been simulated by theoretical models, e.g., classical diffusion model of Sand’s equation (19), governed by key parameters of initial metal-ion concentration, local current density, and transfer nature of metal ions. Guided by Sand’s model, reduced local current densities have been experimentally verified in three-dimensional (3D) electronically conductive porous host for Li deposition, that can alleviate growth of dendritic Li (20-23), although achieving high CE in Li plating/stripping remains challenging. Having noticed from Sand’s model that changing Li-ion transfer can effectively alter Li deposition behavior, we have considered to develop an approach to overcome diffusion- and advection-limited ion transport so as to achieve dendrite-free Li plating/stripping with high CE under both conditions. Inspired by a variety of electrokinetic phenomena in porous media under electric field that enable enhanced mass transport, such as electrokinetic surface conduction, electroosmosis, and electrophoresis (24-27), we have developed a 3D polymeric porous sponge capable of providing Li-ion affinity and promoting electrokinetic phenomena, that works as a plausible Li-ion electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping host on the current collector, to achieve dendrite-free Li plating/stripping with high CE. Strong affinity of Li ions from the 3D cross-linked porous polyethylenimine sponge (PPS) not only selfconcentrates Li ions in the sponge, leading to the higher local concentration of Li ions than in bulk solution, but also drives the formation of interfacial electric double layer (EDL) to provide an electrokinetic pumping feature by electrokinetic surface conduction and electroosmosis during Li plating/stripping (Fig. 1A). Together, the Li-ion electrokinetic

Page 4

self-concentrating/pumping features of the 3D PPS can reduce the concentration polarization, overcome diffusion limited current and therefore regulate the Li-ion concentration difference in deionization area to enable a uniform Li-ion distribution. This in turn leads to dendrite-free and conformal plating/stripping of Li metal of high CE within the 3D PPS host at both high current density and high deposition capacity.

3D PPS was prepared directly on Cu foil by an ice-templating method through crosslinking of branched polyethylenimine (PEI) by 1, 4-butanediol diglycidyl ether (BGE) (Fig. 1B, Fig. S1 and S2) (28, 29). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) investigation of the 3D PPS host on Cu foil (3D PPS@Cu) along top and cross-section views (Fig. 1C and 1D, respectively) reveals sponge-like interconnected porous structure containing irregularly shaped pores with pore diameters ranging from 5 µm to 500 µm and the pore wall thickness of around 550 nm. The 3D PPS has a pore volume of ~5.5 cm3 g-1 and a porosity of 87% (Note S1), and therefore can accommodate deposited Li as high as 74.6 wt% within the sponge host. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) of 3D PPS (Fig. S3) presents the vibration peaks at ~1102 and 1288 cm-1 ascribed to the C-O and CN bonds, respectively. The polar groups (amine and ether) have strong affinity with Li ions

The existence of EDL promotes electrokinetic phenomena, such as electrokinetic surface conduction and electroosmosis, within the sponge under an electric field. To demonstrate the presence of surface conduction of 3D PPS, we measured ion conductivity of solid Li-ion-adsorbed 3D PPS that can reach 9.62 × 10-5 S cm-1 at 25 oC, remarkably higher than the bulk Li-salt-containing cross-linked branched PEI solid electrolyte (2.73 × 10-7 S cm-1 at 25 oC) (Fig. S4). This result indicates surface-adsorbed Li ions, even in solid form, have faster transport along the surfaces of the 3D PPS pore walls than Li ions in the bulk polymer. In liquid electrolyte, owing to the EDL of 3D PPS, the higher Li-ion concentration in the EDL than in bulk liquid electrolyte leads to higher Li-ion conductivity of this EDL layer, providing an additional conductivity of an electrolyte in the vicinity of 3D PPS surfaces, as further demonstrated in kinetic modeling (Fig. 1F). To present evidence of electroosmosis enabled by the 3D PPS, we coated a layer of the cross-linked branched PEI inside a plastic microtube (inner diameter is 850 µm) and successfully observed electroosmosis of the electrolyte within the microtube under an applied electric field of 5 V cm-1 (Movie S1 and S2). The electroosmosis can induce enhanced mass transport within the porous structure even in a closed system (30). Collectively, electrokinetic surface conduction and

Page 7

electroosmosis, along with the Li-ion-affinity property, endow the 3D PPS with the electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping properties of Li ions. We use linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) to experimentally manifest these features of 3D PPS that can increase local Li-ion concentration and enhance Li-ion transport so as to overcome diffusion limited current for Li deposition. LSV of 3D PPS@Cu (Fig. 1E) shows a plateau with higher diffusion limited current over bare Cu electrodes at a voltage range between 0.4 V and 0.8 V, owing to higher local Li-ion concentration within 3D PPS@Cu. Above a voltage of 0.8 V in the LSV, significantly increased current density of 3D PPS@Cu over bare Cu electrodes can be attributed to enhanced Li-ion transport originating from electrokinetic surface conduction and electroosmosis within 3D PPS. To more clearly illustrate the Li-ion electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping features, we carried out a simulation of kinetic equilibrium of Li-ion concentration profiles with constant Li consumption sources (mimicking Li deposition at a constant current density) on both 3D PPS@Cu (Fig. 1G1) and bare Cu electrodes (Fig. 1G2), by solving the ionic diffusion and electrostatic potential field using finite element approach. In contrast to typical concentration polarization of bare Cu electrode (Fig. 1F2 and 1H2), the concentration polarization region is suppressed and Li ions are self-concentrated within 3D PPS@Cu (Fig. 1F1 and 1H1). We found that, at the scale of sponge-pore (Fig. 1F), electrokinetic surface conduction becomes the dominating regime under a high consumption rate of Li sources (corresponding to a current density of 10 mA cm-2) for 3D PPS@Cu (Fig. 1F1). At the scale of entire half-cell system (Fig. 1H), the Li-ion concentration in 3D PPS region (within dash lines) is increased uniformly due to the Liion-affinity of randomly-interconnected porous structure (Fig. 1H1). Similarly, a cross-

sectional 1D concentration profile along normal Y direction of the electrode surface (Fig. 1I) vividly exhibit increased Li-ion concentration within the sponge over bulk electrolyte, higher Li-ion concentration than on bare Cu electrode, and reduced concentration polarization region. Morphological evolution of Li plating within the 3D PPS was investigated by SEM along top and cross-section views (Fig. 2A1-F1 and 2A2-F2, respectively) with increasing deposition amounts (Fig. 2G). The SEM images show the compact and dendrite-free Li grows on the Cu surface upward and conformally fills porous structures of 3D PPS. Phasefield modelling of Li plating, coupling with mass and charge conservation, was performed to study its temporal evolution in 3D PPS and ascertain the Li-ion electrokinetic selfconcentrating/pumping effect. Consistent with the experimentally observed morphology, dense and dendrite-free Li is conformally deposited within the sponge pores as shown from the simulated morphology of Li deposition (Fig. 2H1). Profiling of Li-ion concentration and current density within the 3D PPS was also conducted. As illustrated in Fig. 2I1 and 2K, the concentration polarization is significantly reduced, and Li-ion concentration is enriched and homogeneously distributed within 3D PPS during Li plating/stripping. In addition, the current density is evenly distributed on the Li surface within 3D PPS (Fig. 2J1). In contrast, the dendritic Li deposition with large concentration polarization and uneven current density distribution are observed on a bare Cu electrode (Fig. 2H2-J2 and 2K). Overall, the electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping effect raises the average Li-ion concentration level at the surface of plated Li, efficiently compensates Li-ion depletion, and mitigates the surface concentration difference, leading to a uniform local current density distribution and thus a more uniform Li plating morphology. To demonstrate the

Page 9

important roles of functional groups and/or zeta potential on generating electrokinetic selfconcentrating/pumping effect, we also synthesized 3D porous sponge made from poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) free of Li-ion-affinity property, with a pore volume of 6.4 cm3 g-1 and a porosity of 91.8%, but with a measured zeta potential of around only -7 mV in the same electrolyte. Contrastingly, mossy and fluffy Li still grows within the 3D porous PVDF sponge structure (Fig. S5). These results imply that the Li-ion electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping effect that accounts for remarkable Li plating behavior within the 3D PPS, originates from properties of Li-ion affinity, electrokinetic surface conduction and electroosmosis within the high-zeta-potential cross-linked 3D PPS materials under an electric field.

Benefiting from the Li-ion electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping effect of 3D PPS, dendrite-free Li plating/stripping on 3D PPS@Cu at both high deposition capacity and high current density is achieved in a variety of electrolyte. Compared with bare Cu electrode typical of mossy/dendritic structure of Li (Fig. 3A-B, Fig. S6), 3D PPS@Cu exhibits a uniform, compact and dendrite-free Li conformally deposited within 3D PPS at various deposition capacities and current densities in ether-based electrolyte (2 mA h cm-2 and 2 mA cm-2 in Fig. 3C-F; 2 mA h cm-2 and 4 mA cm-2 in Fig. S7A-D; 4 mA h cm-2 and 4 mA cm-2 in Fig. S7E-H; 4 mA h cm-2 and 6 mA cm-2 in Fig. S7I-L). After 50 cycles of Li plating/stripping, growth of mossy/dendritic Li continues to be effectively suppressed (Fig. S8). Remarkably, at extremely high deposition capacities and current densities (6 mA h cm-2 and 6 mA cm-2 in Fig. 3I-L and Fig. S9; 4 mA h cm-2 and 12 mA cm-2 in Fig. S10AD; 4 mA h cm-2 and 20 mA cm-2 in Fig. S10E-H), the deposited Li still shows a uniform, compact morphology and a conformal structure within 3D PPS, in contrast to aggressive growth of mossy/dendritic Li on bare Cu electrodes (Fig. 3G-H, Fig. S11). Strikingly, even at low temperatures, prone to the growth of Li dendrite due to decreased Li-ion mobility eluted from Sand’s equation, uniform and dendrite-free Li deposition is still observed

Page 12

within 3D PPS at a high current density of 20 mA cm-2 and a deposition capacity of 4 mA h cm-2 (0 oC and -10 oC as shown in Fig. S12 and S13, respectively). Interestingly, the 3D PPS maintains its structure integrity as a host, free of any crack and collapse after the repeated Li plating/stripping (Fig. S14), likely due to its good elastic properties. Moreover, 3D PPS also exhibits superior dendrite-free morphology of Li plating in carbonate-based or highly concentrated electrolyte (Supplementary Materials, Materials and Methods) than bare Cu electrodes at both high deposition capacities and current densities (Fig. S15-18).

The uniform and dendrite-free of Li within 3D PPS@Cu leads to significantly improved CE and cycling stability of Li plating/stripping. We used coin-type asymmetric 3D PPS@Cu foil|Li cells to examine the cycling stability of the 3D PPS@Cu electrode for Li plating/stripping. We adopt highly concentrated electrolyte in the investigation because of high CE of Li plating/stripping achieved at low deposition capacity as reported (10). The 3D PPS@Cu electrodes exhibit greatly improved CE and cycling stability as compared with bare Cu electrodes. When measured at a deposition capacity of 2 mA h cm-2 and a current density of 2 mA cm-2, the cells with 3D PPS@Cu electrodes exhibit a long cycle life over 800 cycles with an average CE as high as 99.1% (Fig. 4A). Note that the electric field strength in the cell calculated based on the voltage profiles of Li plating/stripping (Fig. S19) can reach 5.6 V cm-1, high enough to induce the electroosmosis as observed in the microtube electroosmotic flow experiment (Movie S1 and S2). When elevating the deposition capacity to 4 mA h cm-2 and the current density to 4 mA cm-2, a high average CE of 99% over 450 cycles is achieved (Fig. 4B). More notably, under extremely high deposition capacity and current density (6 mA h cm-2 and 6 mA cm-2), the 3D PPS@Cu electrodes show a peerless Li plating/stripping performance undemonstrated to date (Table S2) with an average CE of 98.9% for more than 300 cycles (Fig. 4C). Even at a high rate of 5 C (a current density of 20 mA cm-2 and a deposition capacity of 4 mA h cm-2), the 3D PPS@Cu electrodes still deliver a high average CE of 97.8% for 180 cycles (Fig. S20). Similarly, the 3D PPS@Cu electrodes also exhibit significantly enhanced CE and cycling

Page 14

life of Li plating/stripping in both ether-based electrolyte (Fig. S21-24) and carbonatebased electrolyte (Fig. S25). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) demonstrates the chemical components of SEI layers formed on 3D PPS@Cu are similar to those formed on bare Cu electrodes in these electrolytes (Fig. S26-28). Therefore, the improved cycling performance of Li plating/stripping is mainly attributed to the 3D PPS which not only enables dendrite-free Li deposition with high CE, but also accommodates volume change during Li plating/stripping so as to maintain stable electrode structures, as evidenced by a stable voltage hysteresis of 3D PPS@Cu electrodes during Li plating/stripping process (Fig. S19, Fig. S21).

The high CE of Li plating/stripping within 3D PPS@Cu allow us to fabricate 3D PPS@Cu with limited amount of Li (designated as Li@3D PPS@Cu) as anode, potentially achieving a high-energy-density Li metal battery with excellent cycling life. We demonstrated this advantage in full cells assembled using Li@3D PPS@Cu anodes and LiFePO4 cathodes. At a low N/P ratio of 0.6 (areal Li amount ratio of negative to positive electrodes, termed N/P ratio), the full cell exhibits an initial capacity of 146.7 mA h g-1 (areal capacity of 2.5 mA h cm-2) and maintains a stable cycling performance over 200 cycles with an average CE of ~99.7% (Fig. 4D). In contrast, the control cell with the same N/P ratio shows a very fast capacity decay with a poor average CE of ~98.3%. We attributed the excellent cycling performance of full cells to stable Li@3D PPS@Cu anodes confirmed by their dendrite-free morphology after 50 cycles, as shown in SEM images (Fig. 4E-H). To examine the stability of Li@3D PPS@Cu anodes at high deposition capacity in full cell system, we paired high-areal-capacity LiFePO4 cathodes (3.8 mA h cm-2) with Li@3D PPS@Cu anodes. The full cells deliver an initial capacity of 149 mA h g−1 and show excellent cyclability, 300 cycles with average CE of ~99.8% at a N/P ratio of 1.2 (Fig.

Page 16

S30) and 400 cycles with average CE of ~99.78% at a N/P ratio of 2 (Fig. 4I), confirming excellent cycling stability of Li@3D PPS@Cu anodes in a practical cycling condition. It is noteworthy that gravimetric energy density of the Li metal battery was not adversely affected by use of lightweight 3D PPS. The Li-ion electrokinetic self-concentrating/pumping effect of 3D PPS alters the Li deposition behavior and enables a dendrite-free growth of Li metal at both high deposition capacities and current densities, and in turn, extends the cycle life of high-areal-capacity Li metal batteries. This approach is shown to be applicable to various liquid electrolytes and can also resolve dendritic issues of Li metal anodes at low temperatures. Moreover, we further demonstrated that uniform and dendrite-free deposition of Na (Fig. S31 and S32) and Zn (Fig. S33 and S34) metals at high deposition capacities and current densities can be achieved using this strategy.
