Keywords: biomass, bifunctional catalysts, single-atom electrocatalysts, heteroatom doping, rechargeable Zn-air batteries

Abstract: Iron–nitrogen–carbon materials (Fe-N-C) are known for their excellent ORR performance. Unfortunately, they generally show a laggard OER activity, which results in a lethargic charging performance in rechargeable Zn-air batteries. Here we innovatively synthesized hierarchical porous S-doped Fe-N-C graphene-like nanosheets utilizing a scalable FeCl3-encapsulated-porphyra precursor pyrolysis strategy. The obtained electrocatalyst exhibited ultrahigh ORR activity (E1/2 = 0.84 V vs. RHE) and impressive OER performance (Ej = 10 = 1.64V). The potential gap (ΔE = Ej = 10 − E1/2) was 0.80 V, outperforming that of most highly active bifunctional electrocatalysts reported to date. Furthermore, we, for the first time, expound the key role of S involved in the atomically dispersed Fe-Nx species on the enhanced both ORR and OER activity by ultrasound-assisted extracting the exclusive S source (taurine) from porphyra. Moreover, the assembled rechargeable Zn-air battery 1

comprising this bifunctional electrocatalyst exhibited higher power density (225.1 mW cm-2) and lower charging–discharging overpotential (1.00 V, 100 mA cm-2) compared to Pt/C + RuO2 catalyst. The design strategy can expand the utilization of earth-abundant biomaterialderived catalyst, and the mechanism investigations of S doping on the structure-activity relationship will inspire the progress of other functional electrocatalysts. 1. Introduction The increasing global energy crisis and the serious environmental issues have impelled extensive research on rechargeable Zn-air battery which is driven by ORR and OER as discharging-charging processes.[1,

2]

However, exploring highly active and durable

bifunctional electrocatalysts with reduced overpotential for OER and ORR remains a challenge.[3, 4] Noble-metal based catalysts (Pt-based and Ru/Ir oxides catalysts) are generally believed to have prominent electrocatalytic activities for ORR and OER respectively, but scarcity and instability limit their commercialization.[5] Therefore, it is highly important to explore non-precious metal catalysts (NPMCs) to replace noble-metal based catalysts.[6] In recent decades, considerable efforts have committed to exploiting catalysts based on FeN-C with remarkable ORR activity, which are comparable with commercial Pt/C.[7] For instance, Wang et al. reported the high-density Fe@N-C material with a positive E1/2 (0.83 V, vs. RHE) than Pt/C (0.82 V, vs. RHE) and relatively high limiting current density.[2] Su et al. developed FeCo nanoparticles encapsulated by N-doped graphitic carbon nanotubes with predominant catalytic activity and stability, preferable to that of noble metal benchmarks.[3] Very recently, Chen et al. prepared an isolated single-atom Fe/N-doped porous carbon with the positive E1/2 of 0.900 V (vs. RHE) and excellent stability, which surpassed that of commercial Pt/C at 0.842 V( vs. RHE) and most non-precious metal catalysts reported to date.[8] Moreover, a mass of experiments and density functional theory (DFT) calculations have demonstrated that the structure of Fe-coordinated N (Fe-Nx) can effectively optimize the 2

Page 3

ORR intermediate adsorption, results in a superior ORR activity.[9, 10, 11]. However, Fe-N-C materials frequently display an unenlightened OER activity, which will bring about a lethargic charging performance in rechargeable Zn-air batteries. The primary factors for the stagnant OER performance of Fe-N-C materials derive from the too strong adsorption of intermediate and invalid desorption of the final product (O2) during OER.[12] Accordingly, a viable approach to enhance the OER performance of Fe-N-C catalysts is heteroatom doping, in which S with a lower electronegativity than N (N (3.04) > S (2.58) ≈ C (2.55)) is considered as an imperative dopant.[13] Abundant studies have revealed that S doping can change electron structure of the doped carbon materials, which benefits catalytic process.[1, 12, 14, 15] However, the acute insight for the synergistic effect of S involved Fe-N-C materials on the ORR and OER activity remains elusive. Therefore, to investigate the catalytic theory of S-doped Fe-NC materials, exploring a legitimate strategy is essentially important. Currently, the traditional precursor of Fe-N-C materials such as 2, 2-bipyridine[1], dicyandiamide,[2] polypyrrole,[16] the metal-amino acid,[17] graphene and CNTs,[18] has disadvantages of being cumbersome and pricey, which significantly hampers its pursuable commercialization. In addition, a large number of studies have focused on abundant and cheap carbon sources derived from biological materials, among which laver has been proved to be an excellent precursor material for electrocatalysis and lithium ion batteries due to its inherent heteroatom doping advantages. In this study, we innovatively introduced low-cost and earth-abundant porphyra as a precursor of Fe-N-C material. The dry weight percentages of vegetable proteins and taurine in porphyra can reach up to 30% and 1.4% which can introduce high N and S supply, respectively.[19] Meanwhile, the inherent advantages of organics homogeneously dispersed in the biomass cells can be beneficial to generate the atomic dispersed active sites.[20] In addition, different from the traditional Fe-N-C catalysts which are heterogeneous, taurine as the mainly S source can be ultrasound-assisted extracted from the porphyra easily, making it possible to verify the role of S species.[21] 3

Page 4

Herein, one-step pyrolysis of FeCl3-encapsulated-porphyra was proposed to generate N and S co-doped Fe-N-C species (Fe-NSDC). Benefiting from the plentiful atomically dispersed active sites coordinated to engender high ORR/OER activities, 3D conductive stripped flake structure to facilitate mass/electron transfer, and large specific surface area to increase active site exposure, the ultima sample showed admirable bifunctional electrocatalytic activity and much enhanced durability. Prominently, a homemade rechargeable Zn-air battery integrating this hybrid electrocatalyst showed lower charge-discharge voltage gap of 1.00 V at a high current density of 100 mA cm-2, which was superior to Pt/C and Pt/C + RuO2 catalyst. All above results revealed that S doped Fe-N-biocarbon source was effective for realizing an improvement in oxygen catalysis for rechargeable Zn-Air battery. 2. Results and Discussion 2.1. Synthesis and structure characterization The bifunctional Fe-NSDC catalyst was prepared by a scalable pyrolysis process (Figure 1a). In brief, the dry porphyra with monolayer cell structure was grinded together with FeCl3•6H2O to obtain Fe3+ uniformly encapsulated porphyra precursor materials. Considering the homogeneous organics in the biomass cells, N and S atoms were supposed to uniformly disperse in the porphyra precursor materials. Then the FeCl3-encapsulated-porphyra precursor (FeCl3@porphyra) was annealed at 900 °C in Ar atmosphere for 2 h. As the heating temperature increased, porphyra rich in taurine and bioactive peptide as the main N and S sources were converted into N, S co-doped carbon, along with the generation of Fe-based active sites. Meanwhile, the biocarbon was stripped into thin sheets with porous structure accompanied the pyrolysis of organics in porphyra by gas production (CO2, NO2, and so on), and the active metallic Fe might react with nearby atoms (such as C, N and S). Finally, the pyrolyzed product was etched in 2 M HCl solution at 85 °C for 3 h to remove inactive Fe species. For comparision, the only N-self-doped Fe-N-C species (Fe-NDC) were also 4

prepared by using ultrasound-assisted extraction porphyra as precursor and heated with the addition of FeCl3•6H2O (mass ratio of porphyra: FeCl3•6H2O = 1: 4) under Ar atmosphere at 900 °C for 2 h. The delicate morphologies of N, S co-doped carbon derived from porphyra (NSDC), FeNDC and Fe-NSDC samples were investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). For NSDC, due to gas production during pyrolysis of porphyra, interconnected porous structures (20-50 nm) could be observed on the surface of the biocarbon (thickness ~ 15-20 μm) derived from the carbonization of single layer porphyra cell (Figure 1b-c). In contrast, the Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC samples exhibited three-dimensional (3D) hierarchical stripped flake structure due to the catalytic procedure of Fe (Figure 1d,e). Moreover, compared with the Fe-NDC, thinner stripped flake with porous and wave-like ripples structure was observed in Fe-NSDC, which provided more exposure of active sites and facilitated the rapid electron transport. Meanwhile, none Fe particles were found in both Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC after acid etching. The N2 adsorption–desorption curve of NSDC, Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC appertained to type IV isotherms (ground on IUPAC classification), indicating the existence of mesopores. Furthermore, Fe-NSDC displayed a normative hysteresis loop of type H3, which was deemed to be the slit mesopores created by the stacking of flake structures consistent with SEM image. Interestingly, the pore size distributions of the three samples implied that the mesopores were in a narrow pore size range of 3-6 nm (Figure 2a). However, Fe-NSDC displayed approximate triple BET surface area (1533.7 m2 g-1) than NSDC (554 m2 g-1) and Fe-NDC (552.9 m2 g-1), which resulted from more micropore structure volume (0.39 cm3 g-1) than NSDC (0.09 cm3 g-1) and Fe-NDC (0.07 cm3 g-1) (Table S1). These results indicated that the coexistence of taurine and Fe in Fe-NSDC generated 3D conductive stripped porous flake structure, which was advantageous for mass/electron transfer and maximum effective contact of active sites.[22] 5

The XRD pattern of NSDC showed two broad carbon peaks at 25°(002) and 45° (101) (JPCDS card no.41-1487), indicating a poor graphitic structure in NSDC.[23] By contrast with NSDC, Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC appeared a strong and sharp diffraction peak at 26.3°that corresponded to the (002) plane of graphitic carbon (JCPDS No. 41-1487), indicating a good graphitization resulting from the catalysis of Fe during heat treatment,[24] which could be also confirmed by Raman spectra. As shown in Figure S1a, the unified intensity graphitic/carbon ratio (IG/ID) of the two samples increased from 0.62 (NSDC) to 0.69 (Fe-NDC) and 0.75 (FeNSDC), respectively. In addition, the XRD diffraction peaks of Fe-NSDC observed at 44.67° was indexed to (110) planes of Fe (JPCDS card no. 06-0696), and the diffraction peaks at 42.87°, 43.73°, 44.56°, 45.00°, 45.85°, 54.42°, and 78.58° were assigned to (211), (102), (220), (031), (112), (230) and (133) planes of Fe3C (JPCDS card no. 77-0255), respectively.[25] These metallic Fe crystalline phases were reduced from Fe3+ by carbothermal reduction during pyrolysis, while Fe3C phases were ascribed to the reaction of accumulated excessive Fe and neighboring carbon atoms.[10] In sharp contrast, Fe-NSDC showed weak peaks of Fe or Fe3C phases, indicating that the coexistence of taurine in Fe-NSDC benefited to remove inactive Fe species. The LSV curves of Pt/C, Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC were obtained on RDE with a series of rotating electrode speeds. The diffusion current density of Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC improved with increasing rotation speed, similar with the Pt/C, implying the same ORR mass transfer controlled process (Figure 3b-d).[35] Meanwhile, the homologous Koutecky-Levich (K-L) plots exhibited a distinguished linearity between J-1 and ω-1/2, implying the first-order reaction kinetics for ORR related to dissolved oxygen in solution.[36] To quantitatively evaluate the intermediate peroxide product, the rotating ringdisk electrode (RRDE) measurements were also performed. OER catalytic activity The OER catalytic activities of the prepared electrocatalysts were assessed in an O2saturation 0.1 M KOH solution. As shown in Figure 5a-b, the LSV curve of the Fe-NSDC catalyst in 0.1 M KOH solution exhibited a small overpotential of 410 mV at a current density of 10 mA cm-2 (Ej = 10) and a low Tafel slope of 59 mV dec-1, suggesting the most remarkable performance among previously reported Fe-N-C electrocatalysts for OER, and even comparable to that of RuO2 (Ej = 10 = 390 mV, Tafel slope = 85 mV dec-1). In contrast, the OER performance of Fe-NDC was sluggish under the same condition. Namely, the increase of S content in Fe-NSDC triggered the enhanced OER performance, indicating that the S doping played a crucial role in catalyzing oxygen evolution process. Then the high stability of Fe-NSDC was also proved by chronoamperometric analysis for 30000 s, the relative current density then became stable after experiencing initial increase, possibly due to the generation of additional active substances and the piecemeal penetration of the electrolyte into the stomatal structure. In contrast, the RuO2 catalyst showed a significant attenuation in current density, maintaining a current density of approximately 92.5% after 11

Thermodynamically, smaller ΔE value amounts to less

efficiency loss and better catalyst performance during a reversible oxygen catalysis.[40] So the potential gap (ΔE = Ej = 10 − E1/2) between OER (Ej = 10) and ORR (E1/2) was a key parameter of overall ORR/OER bifunctional activities and was evaluated here (Figure 5d). Remarkably, the Fe-NSDC exhibited a ΔE of 0.80 V, which suggested the best catalyst performance among previously reported high-performance bifunctional catalysts, including highly active non-metallic materials, even highly active transition-metal nitride/oxide bifunctional catalysts (Figure 5e and Table S2). These results reassured its highly intrinsic bifunctionality for ORR and OER, meanwhile enabled its use as air cathode material for rechargeable Zn-air battery applications. In addition, in order to further identify the role of Fe and S species in OER, 15 mM KCN was added to KOH solution (Figure 5f). As expected, the OER activity of CN--poisoned Fe-NSDC significantly decreased, confirming that Fe-Nx was an indispensable part of the catalytic activity. Thus we deduced that the high OER performance of Fe-NSDC catalyst was directly related to the synergistic Fe-N3|S active sites. Moreover, the OER process in alkaline medium is generally acknowledged to proceed through four different electron/proton transfer step (Scheme S1) and a mass of experiments and DFT calculations have manifested that the Fe-N-C materials can effectively intensify the intermediate adsorption (O2, OH-, OOH-, etc.).[8, 11] However, immoderately strong adsorption of these intermediates on the Fe-NDC hindered the evolution of the final product and ultimately reduced the holistic OER activity. So when N atoms were replaced by the less electronegative S atom (N (0.7) > S (0.5)) in Fe-NSDC, its oxygen desorption was much facile and eventually resulted in the prominent OER activity. Therefore, S doping optimized charge and spin distributions of Fe-N-C materials which were actively favorable for adsorption of intermediates and O2 desorption on the active sites during OER. Rechargeable Zn-air battery and all-solid Zn-air battery test Considering the excellent bifunctional activity of Fe-NSDC, we constructed Zn-air battery in two-electrode device to demonstrate its viability in actual energy devices. A self-regulating rechargeable Zn-air battery was fabricated with Fe-NSDC loaded-nickel foam as the air cathode, zinc foil as the metal anode, and 6.0 M KOH with the adding of zinc acetate (0.2 M) as the electrolyte,[41] the schematic of basic configuration was shown in Figure 6a. As shown in Figure S5a-b, in comparison with other groups, the Fe-NSDC-driven Zn-air battery showed a ultrahigh OCP of 1.53 V and outstanding discharge behaviors with a high peak power density of 225.1 mW cm-2, implying its enormous feasibility to act as efficient air cathodes catalyst. In order to evaluate battery stability and capacity, the galvanostatic discharge curves of the different catalyst-based battery were also measured. As shown in Figure S5c-e, both Fe-NDC and Fe-NSDC-based battery showed significantly higher voltage plateau at the different current densities than Pt/C-based battery, which further revealed both Fe-NSDC and Fe-NDC-based battery exhibited the ultrahigh discharge performance derived from their excellent ORR activity. Furthermore, only a negligible voltage drop was observed for FeNSDC and Fe-NDC-based battery after a chronic galvanostatic discharge, suggesting their excellent durability. When normalized to the mass of attrited zinc, the battery using the FeNSDC cathode exhibited approximate twice specific capacities (740.8, 732.0 and 714.8 mAh g-1Zn) than Pt/C cathode battery (413.2, 398.7, 384.1 mAh g-1Zn) at current densities of 4, 20 and 200 mA cm-2 respectively, indicating a satisfactory rate capability. A commercial red light-emitting diodes (LED, 1.5 V) light could be continuously lit by two series batteries for

about 20h until the zinc foil was exhausted (Figure 6b), manifesting its practical application potential. Compared with other groups, the charge-discharge polarization curves of Fe-NSDC showed a lower overpotential (1.00 V) at a high current density of 100 mA cm-2 than Fe-NDC (1.64 V), and even better than the precious metal cathode (Pt/C + RuO2) (1.09 V) (Figure 6c), suggesting its excellent rechargeability. The stability of the rechargeable Zn-air battery was further verified by galvanostatic charge-discharge cycling tests at 4 mA cm-2 with each cycle being 10 min. As shown in Figure 6b, the Fe-NSDC-driven Zn-air battery showed a smaller overpotential (0.45 V) than Pt/C + RuO2-driven battery (1.16 V) at the 72nd cycle. The FeNSDC-driven Zn-air battery also showed a long deep cycle life over the last 100 cycles with the charge-discharge overpotential changed from 0.70 V at the 300 th cycle to 0.71 V at the 400 th cycle, corresponding to only 1.4% decrease of voltaic efficiency. It is more preferable than that of Pt/C + RuO2-driven battery with the charge-discharge overpotential increased from 0.95 V at the 300th cycle to 1.26 V at the 400th cycle, corresponding to 30.5% decrease of voltaic efficiency. The noticeable inactivation behavior of Pt/C + RuO2-driven battery could be ascribed to the particle agglomeration and the subsequent breakaway of noble metal nanoparticles from the carbon support during operation.[42] The unique mesoporous structure of Fe-NSDC could not only help to hinder the catalysts from aggregation but also maintain continuous electron/mass-transport channels for ORR/OER, resulting in much enhanced voltaic efficiency. The high-performance rechargeable Zn-air battery was comparable to or even outperforming those of devices involving various bifunctional electrocatalysts reported previously (Table S3). Conclusion In summary, we developed a porphyra-derived S doped Fe-N-C effective bifunctional electrocatalyst. The resultant Fe-NSDC electrocatalyst exhibited the state-of-the-art ORR performance, impressive OER performance and lower ΔE ascribed to the large quantity of monoatomic dispersed active sites and the enhanced mass/electron adsorption-transport properties. Intensive investigation disclosed that S-doping could optimize charge and spin distributions of Fe-N-C materials to produce superior ORR and OER activities, which were mainly ascribed to the Fe-Nx and Fe-N3|S sites, respectively. Both Fe-NSDC driven liquid Znair battery and ﬂexible all-solid-state Zn-air battery exhibited excellent battery parameters in the charge and discharge process, further qualified them as potential candidates for ﬂexible devices. The novel design of biomaterial-derived S doped Fe-N-C bifunctional catalyst could inspire the design and progress of other functional electrocatalysts applied in next-generation reversible energy conversion systems. 4. Experimental methods The synthesis of catalysts: The preparation procedure of hierarchical carbon from porphyra was summarized in Figure 1a. First, the porphyra were carefully rubbed into small pieces 15

Page 16

(about 1 cm long) in a self-styled bag, washed with deionized water, and dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 12 h. Then the dried samples (1g) were ground into a powder in a mortar, and X g FeCl3•6H2O (mass ratio, porphyra: FeCl3•6H2O = 1: X) was added to the mortar to grind together. Next, the uniformly mixed powder was heated to 900 °C at a heating rate of 5 °C min-1 under a flowing Ar atmosphere for 2 h. Finally, the pyrolyzed product was etched in 2 M HCl solution at 85 °C for 3 h to remove inactive Fe species. The leached sample was further filtered several times with deionized water until the solution became neutral and dried in vacuum at 60 °C overnight. The obtained N, S co-doped Fe-N-C species derived from porphyra were labeled as Fe-NSDC-X. Moreover, the porphyra without taurine was prepared in a ultrasound-assisted extracted and purified method.[21] The only N-self-doped Fe-N-C species (Fe-NDC) were prepared by using ultrasound-assisted extraction porphyra as precursor and heated with the addition of FeCl3•6H2O (mass ratio of porphyra: FeCl3•6H2O = 1: 4) under Ar atmosphere at 900 °C for 2 h. Then another control sample was synthesized through pyrolysis of porphyra, and the final samples were named as NSDC.
