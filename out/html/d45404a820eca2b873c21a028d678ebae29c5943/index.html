11 The degradation of perovskite solar cells in the presence of trace water and oxygen 12 poses one challenge for their commercial impact given the appreciable permeability of cost- 13 effective encapsulants. Point defects were recently shown to be a major source of 14 decomposition due to their high affinity to water and oxygen molecules. Here we report 15 that in single cation/halide perovskites, local lattice strain facilitates the formation of 16 vacancies and that cation/halide mixing suppresses their formation via strain relaxation. 17 We then show that judiciously selected dopants can maximize the formation energy of 18 defects responsible for degradation. Cd-containing cells show an order of magnitude 19 enhanced unencapsulated stability compared to state-of-art mixed perovskite solar cells, 20 for both shelf storage and maximum power point operation in air ambient at relative 21 humidity of 50%. We conclude by testing the generalizability of the defect engineering 22 concept, demonstrating both vacancy-formation suppressors (such as Zn) and -promoters 23 (such as Hg).

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of MAPbI3-based solar cells (MA=CH3NH3 +) has 25 improved rapidly in the last five years1–7. However, instability in MAPbI3 limits this active 26 material’s applicability in solar cell (PSC) technology8. Notable progress toward increasing the 27 stability of PSCs was recently made via the engineering of interfaces9–11 and of carrier selective 28 layers.12,13 Developing even more stable perovskite active layers is now a topic that requires 29 further effort. The incorporation of low-dimensional structures is an attractive approach to 30 improve the stability of perovskites,14,15 but, at present, compromises PCE. 31 Replacing MA with FA (HC(NH2)2 +) has been found to produce perovskites relatively stable 32 against decomposition at high temperatures16; however, at room temperature, FAPbI3 is 33 structurally unstable owing to the large size of the FA cation: it spontaneously transforms into a 34 yellow, nonfunctional phase. The structural instability of FAPbI3 was recently addressed by 35 engineering the Goldschmidt tolerance factor in perovskites via the partial substitution of FA 36 with MA and/or Cs, and of I with Br17–23. Cation/anion-mixed perovskite solar cells were shown 37 to operate stably at maximum power point (MPP) conditions under continuous illumination and 38 nitrogen flow for hundreds of hours24,25. 39 The structural stability of mixed perovskites can be understood in terms of steric effects26,27 40 and energy gain from entropy of mixing28–31; nevertheless, the mechanisms that prevent the 41 decomposition of these perovskites remain incompletely understood. Understanding the factors 42 governing stability against decomposition will enable fabrication of PSCs that are more tolerant 43 to air ambient. The need for air-tolerant solar cells arises from the high water vapor and oxygen 44 transmission rates (WVTR and OTR, respectively) of cost-effective photovoltaic encapsulation 45 materials, which until now have been insufficient for protection of PSCs32; in contrast, the best 46 alternative atmospheric barriers work well with perovskites, but add cost8,33 (Supplementary 47

Developing air-ambient-tolerant perovskite active layers will ensure that practical 48 encapsulation technologies can be applied. 49 Here we report that in single cation/halide FAPbI3, local lattice strain induces the formation 50 of point defects, recently shown to be a major source of degradation in PSCs. We then show that 51 incorporation of Cs/MA/Br ions in the state-of-art CsMAFA perovskite 52 (Cs0.05MA0.15FA0.8PbI2.55Br0.45)21,25 increases the formation energy of vacancies, consistent with 53 this material’s impressive initial performance. However, we also show that such defects, even if 54 rare, have high affinity to water and oxygen molecules; and that even a small density of them is 55 highly detrimental. Further increasing Cs/Br content blueshifts the bandgap, which works against 56 PSC power conversion efficiency. We therefore sought new strategies to suppress vacancy 57 formation, and report herein the incorporation of judiciously-selected B-site dopants into the 58 lattice of mixed perovskite crystals. We incorporate cadmium (Cd) into a mixed perovskite 59 lattice, releasing remaining lattice strain and further increasing the energetic cost associated with 60 the formation of vacancies. The resultant unencapsulated PSCs show significantly extended 61 stability: they maintain >90% of their initial PCE after 30 days of storage in air ambient at a 62 relative humidity (RH) of 50%. They also show an order of magnitude longer operating MPP 63 lifetime under these same air ambient RH conditions compared to state-of-art CsMAFA 64 perovskite solar cells. 65

We found an 71 elemental composition in the crystal similar to that in the feed solution (Fig. 1b and 1c, 72 Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 2). 79 Lattice strain relaxation mechanisms in perovskites 80 To gain insight into the origins of improved stability of CsMAFA21,25, we calculated 81 formation energies of FAPbI3 and CsMAFA as well as antisites and Schottky vacancies 82 (stoichiometric amount of anion and cation vacancies) for large supercells (Fig. 2a) using density 83 functional theory (DFT, see Methods and Supplementary Data 1). We did not find a notable 84 difference in the formation energies of the compounds (Supplementary Table 3). Similarly, the 85 formation of Pb-I antisites, the most probable deep electronic traps34, are equally unlikely given 86 their high formation energies (Fig. 2b). 87 In contrast, we found a significant difference in the formation energies associated with lead 88 iodide vacancies (Fig. 2b). In FAPbI3, this energy is remarkably low (~0.25 eV), corresponding 89 to an equilibrium PbI2 vacancy density of ~3×1017 cm-3 (Supplementary Table 4). This suggests that incorporation of small ions prevents 102 formation of defects by opening a new lattice strain relaxation pathway in CsMAFA (Fig. 3a), 103 which is otherwise reached by vacancy formation in single cation/anion perovskites. 104

105 The role of vacancies in degradation of perovskites 106 Although perovskite vacancies are shallow electronic traps34, they hold potential to cause the 107 formation of deep traps if they react with the ambient environment. The absorption of water and 108 oxygen molecules at vacancy sites is significantly more favorable than at pristine surfaces36,37 109 accelerating perovskite degradation via the vacancy-assisted decomposition mechanism. We 110 explored experimentally the results of our computational studies by measuring the rate of 111 superoxide generation, an indicator of the density of vacancies in perovskites, using a 112

Page 7

6

hydroethidine fluorescent probe:37,38 we found the superoxide yield to be significantly reduced in 113 CsMAFA films compared to MAPbI3 and FAPbI3 films (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Fig. 4). 114 The vacancy-assisted mechanism of perovskite decomposition suggests a means to increase 115 further the stability of perovskites in air ambient. Our concept is to relax the remaining lattice 116 strain on B/X sites (Fig. 3d) to maximize further the formation energy of the most abundant 117 defect without introducing electronic traps. We therefore chose isovalent dopants as candidates 118 (Fig. 3e, Supplementary Table 5), noting that heterovalent dopants may introduce more defects.39 119

120 Suppression of vacancies via the incorporation of Cl 121 We start with incorporation of Cl- as a promising test case for this purpose, as it is 122 isoelectronic with I- and may further decrease the lattice strain due to its small ionic radius. 123 Previous reports found that Cl blended into MAPbI3 solution enhances carrier transport in the 124 ultimate perovskite film40,41. Recent studies established that there is no Cl in the final crystal 125 lattice42 and that Cl may only reside at the interface43–46, thus its role was attributed to the 126 morphological evolution of MAPbI3 47–49. Here we explore instead Cl incorporation within the 127 CsMAFA lattice with the goal of preventing the formation of vacancies, identifying a new role 128 for Cl that enables air-ambient-tolerant perovskites. 129 We first carried out DFT calculations to investigate the role of Cl in relaxing the lattice strain 130 and reducing the vacancy density. We found that when I was partially replaced by Cl, the Pb-X 131 bond lengths decreased, as did the Pb-X-Pb distortion angles50 (Supplementary Fig. 3), and the I- 132 vacancy formation is suppressed by ≥0.3 eV (Supplementary Fig. 5). The formation of a charged 133

Page 8

7

defect favors the formation of a counter-charged defect in perovskites, which subsequently form 134 charge-neutral Schottky defect pairs (Supplementary Fig. 6).51 135 We then fabricated Cl-containing CsMAFA films using PbCl2 as a Cl source mixed in 136 solution (see Methods). The reduced superoxide generation indicates that Cl suppresses the 137 density of defects (Fig. 4a). In particular, in air ambient with RH of 50%, CsMAFA films 138 degraded within fewer than 7 days, while the films with Cl remained dark (Supplementary Fig. 139 7). We also observed improved stability of Cl-containing CsMAFA films over control CsMAFA 140 films when subjected to thermal stress (85°C) in air ambient (Supplementary Fig. 8). 141 To ascertain whether Cl is incorporated within the crystal structure, we grew single crystals 142 in the presence of Cl, and performed XPS of the cleaved interior of the crystal. The results 143 showed the successful incorporation of Cl within CsMAFA perovskite (Supplementary Fig. 9). 144 Thus, in contrast with MAPbI3, CsMAFA is able to host Cl: we explain this by noting that the 145 ~1/6 occupancy of CsMAFA halide-site with Br (Br-based perovskites can form solid solutions 146 with both I and Cl)52 and the presence of Cs (CsPbI3 can host Cl at levels near the solubility of Cl 147 in CsPbI3)53. 148

149 Suppression of vacancies via the incorporation of Cd 150 The mechanism of lattice strain relaxation via the incorporation of small monovalent halogen 151 anions (Cl) suppressed the formation of vacancies and, consequently, enhanced stability. 152 However, incorporation of additional Cl will increase the bandgap, undesirable for solar cells 153 (Supplementary Fig. 10). We therefore expanded our concept of lattice strain relaxation to 154 include B-site dopants (Figure 3c). We chose Cd, which is isovalent to Pb but has a smaller ionic 155

We also observed successful incorporation of Cd within the perovskite crystal 162 lattice (Supplementary Fig. 9), in agreement with previous findings.54 We note that Rb- 163 containing CsMAFA showed no reduction in the rate of superoxide generation (Fig. 4a). This 164 may arise due to a failure of Rb to incorporate within the crystal lattice.55 165 Incorporation of Cl and Cd increased the PL intensity of films five- and ten-fold, 166 respectively. PL lifetime measurements revealed that Cl and Cd incorporation 167 decreases twofold the integrated contribution of the short recombination term and notably 168 increases the integrated counts of the long recombination term (Fig. 4c, Supplementary Fig. 11). 169 Identical XRD diffraction patterns and SEM images (Fig. 4d,e, Supplementary Fig. 12) indicate 170 that the lattice parameters and morphology are preserved at the Cl and Cd concentrations used 171 herein. 172

173 Perovskite solar cell performance and stability 174 With the goal of improving stability in Cl and Cd films, we sought to quantify the impact of 175 Cl and Cd integration on PSC devices. We fabricated PSCs in planar architecture following the 176

recently reported CsMAFA PSCs on Cl-capped TiO2 with 20.1% certified PCE25. The latter also 177 served as a control device. 178 Freshly fabricated CsMAFA, Cl- and Cd-PSCs showed similar average PCEs of ~20.5%, Voc 179 of 1.16 V, FF of ~80% and Jsc of ~22 mA/cm2 (Fig. 5a, Supplementary Fig. 13): the latter agrees 180 with the external quantum yield (EQE) measurements (Supplementary Fig. 14). 181 The devices showed a large difference in stability when stored in air ambient at RH of 50% 182 (Fig. 5b). Unencapsulated Cl- and Cd-PSCs retained >95% of their initial PCE after storage in 183 the dark in air ambient for 30 days. In contrast, CsMAFA PSCs retained only 60% of their initial 184 PCE and exhibited bleaching (Supplementary Fig. 7). 185 We also investigated the photostability of unencapsulated PSCs under MPP operation and 186 continuous illumination in air ambient. CsMAFA cells showed a rapid loss of PCE with relative 187 -1.5%/min of linear slope,56 while Cl and Cd cells extended the lifetime by a factor of 6 and 15, 188 respectively (Fig. 5c, Supplementary Fig. 15). Extended stability of Cd-containing PSCs agrees 189 with our computational results as well as our experimental measurements of superoxide 190 generation rate. In a nitrogen atmosphere, all PSCs retained >90% of their initial PCE after 191 continuous MPP for tens of hours (Fig. 5d), indicating that the moisture and oxygen in air play a 192 vital role in their decomposition. 193 Defects in perovskites assist segregation of halides under illumination, leading to 194 hysteresis57. In agreement with the finding herein, we found large hysteresis in MAPbI3 and 195 FAPbI3-based PSCs, whereas hysteresis for mixed PSCs was negligible (Supplementary Table 196 6). The CsMAFA treatment with MACl and vinyl benzyl chloride (VBCl) also enhanced PL 197 peaks as a result of surface passivation (Supplementary Fig. 16); however, devices showed poor 198

performance and large hysteresis (Supplementary Table 6, Supplementary Fig. 17). We attribute 199 this to off-stoichiometric surface composition after treatment. Engineering of defects in the bulk 200 and at the surface is likely crucial to the combination of performance and stability in PSCs. 201 In light of the proposed vacancy engineering mechanisms, we also explored the effect of 202 other divalent dopants such as hard Lewis acids (Group 2: Ba, Ca, Sr) and soft/intermediate 203 Lewis acids (Group 12: Hg and Zn) on the performance of PSCs (Supplementary Fig. 18). Hard 204 Lewis acids showed a notably decreased time-zero PCE, likely due to a lack of incorporation 205 and formation of traps: this agrees with the Pearson hard and soft acid and base (HSAB) theory 206 whereby soft I- base forms a stronger bond with soft acid Pb2+. Zn-doped PSCs showed 207 promising stability; however, the time-zero PCE of devices was slightly decreased, a subject for 208 future studies. Hg-containing PSCs exhibited an opposite trend: comparable initial PCE, but 209 substantial degradation within few days, likely due to formation of heterovalent Hg+ that 210 introduces more vacancies.39 These findings agree with our DFT findings (Supplementary Fig. 211 5), that Zn suppresses formation of vacancies, while Hg is a vacancy-formation promoter. 212

213 Conclusion 214 Because the standard photovoltaic encapsulation materials transmit water and oxygen at a 215 rate sufficient for complete degradation of perovskites within few days (Supplementary Table 216 1)32, it is desirable to realize PSCs that exhibit enhanced operating stability in air ambient to 217 ensure that realistic encapsulation technologies can be used. Mixed CsMAFA perovskite is less 218 prone to form vacancies compared to single cation/anion perovskites, and therefore is more 219 stable against decomposition. Suppressing atomic vacancies via incorporation of Cd and Cl 220

Page 12

11

within the CsMAFA lattice further enhances the air ambient MPP operational stability of state- 221 of-art PSCs for an order of magnitude. This in turn significantly relaxes the requirements for 222 encapsulation materials and their processing conditions. The proposed degradation mechanism 223 and vacancy engineering strategy via lattice strain relaxation open a new avenue to enable 224 continued progress toward PSCs with 25-year operating lifetime with practical encapsulants. 225
