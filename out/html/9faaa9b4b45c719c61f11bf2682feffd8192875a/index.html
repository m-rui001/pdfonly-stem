Abstract 1 Multi-junction all-perovskite tandem solar cells are a promising choice for next- 2 generation solar cells with high efficiency and low fabrication cost. However, the lack of 3 high-quality low-bandgap perovskite absorber layers seriously hampers the development 4 of efficient and stable two-terminal monolithic all-perovskite tandem solar cells. Here, we 5 report a bulk-passivation strategy via incorporation of chlorine, to enlarge grains and 6 reduce electronic disorder in mixed tin-lead low-bandgap (~1.25 eV) perovskite absorber 7 layers. This enables the fabrication of efficient low-bandgap perovskite solar cells using 8 thick absorber layers (~750 nm), which is a requisite for efficient tandem solar cells. Such 9 improvement enables the fabrication of two-terminal all-perovskite tandem solar cells 10 with a champion power conversion efficiency of 21% and steady-state efficiency of 20.7%. 11 The efficiency is retained to 85% of its initial performance after 80 hours of operation 12 under continuous illumination. 13

Organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells (PVSCs) have attracted tremendous 1 interest due to the rapid increase in record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction 2 devices from 3.8% to a certified 23.3%1-4. Low-temperature solution processability and 3 bandgap tunability make the metal halide perovskites an ideal candidate for fabricating all- 4 perovskite tandem solar cells, which can overcome the Shockley-Queisser (SQ) radiative 5 efficiency limits for single-junction cells. For two-terminal (2-T) monolithic tandem devices 6 consisting of a wide-bandgap (wide-Eg) top subcell (~1.7-1.9 eV)5 and a low-bandgap (low-Eg) 7 bottom subcell (~1.1-1.3 eV)6,7, the predicted maximum PCE is over 40%8,9. 8 Thus far, efficient 2-T tandem cells based on perovskite/Si, perovskite/copper indium 9 gallium diselenide (Cu(In,Ga)Se2), and perovskite/perovskite configurations have been 10 reported8,10-13. Among them, the all-perovskite tandem cells possess unique advantages, 11 including low-temperature processes for all subcells, compatibility with flexible and 12 lightweight applications, and potentially low fabrication costs9,14,15. Recently, McGehee, Snaith, 13 and co-workers reported a 2-T all-perovskite tandem cell with a PCE of 17%8. Bolink and co- 14 workers combined solution-processed wide-Eg perovskite top cells with vacuum-deposited 15 medium-bandgap (~1.5-1.6 eV) perovskite bottom cells to build 2-T tandem cells with a 16 champion PCE of 18.1%12. Jen and co-workers achieved a PCE of 18.5% for a 2-T all- 17 perovskite tandem cell using a 1.82 eV wide-Eg top cell and a 1.22 eV low-Eg bottom cell13. 18 For a 2-T tandem cell, current matching between the two subcells plays a crucial role in 19 the final performance of the device. The electronic quality of the low-Eg perovksite absorber 20 layer is particularly important since it requires a thick layer to fully absorb the infrared light 21 passing through the wide-Eg subcell16. A thick absorber layer without high electronic quality 22 would inevitably result in a solar cell with a relatively low fill factor (FF) and open-circuit 23 voltage (Voc) due to the non-radiative recombination losses occuring in the bulk perovskite layer 24 and at the perovskite/charge-selective layer interfaces17-21. Many efforts have been made to 25 reduce the non-radiative recombination losses in various-bandgap PVSCs via passivating the 26 Pursuant to the DOE Public Access Plan, this document represents the authors' peer-reviewed, accepted manuscript. Unlike in their wide and medium bandgap perovskite conterparts, efficiency 2 improvement in low-Eg PVSCs is rarely reported due to the difficulty and challenge to process 3 high-quality low-Eg perovskite absorber layers. However, such improvement is essential for 4 fabricating efficient 2-T all-perovskite tandem cells. 5 Here, we report on the increase in grain size and reduction of electronic disorder in low-Eg 6 mixed Sn-Pb perovskite layers via incorporating chlorine (Cl). Such improvements allow us to 7 successfully fabricate efficient 1.25 eV low-Eg PVSCs with thick absorber layers (~750 nm) 8 without sacrificing either FF or Voc. By combining these low-Eg perovskite bottom subcells 9 with 1.75 wide-Eg perovskite top subcells and ultra-flat and compact interconnecting layers 10 (ICLs), we fabricate 2-T all-perovskite tandem cells with PCEs approaching 21%. An 11 encapsulated tandem solar cell with an efficiency greater than 20% retains 85% of its initial 12 performance after 80 hours of continuous operation under 1 sun illumination in air as measured 13 by maximum power point tracking. 14

9 Characterization of low-Eg perovskites with Cl incorporation 10 To gain further insight into the efficiency improvement due to the incorporation of Cl, we 11 characterize perovskite film properties/quality, charge carrier mobility, and charge 12 recombination in solar cells without Cl and with 2.5% Cl. As shown in Fig. 2a and 2b, after 13 the incorporation of 2.5% Cl, the grain size becomes comparable with the film thickness (~750 14 nm thick). The full width at half maximum (FWHM) values of the (110) peak from XRD 15 patterns of Sn/Pb-0.0%Cl and Sn/Pb-2.5%Cl are 0.099o and 0.087o, respectively 16 (Supplementary Fig. 6), indicating an increase in crystallinity upon the incorporation of Cl. 17 The near-unity aspect ratio of the grains implies a low number of horizontal grain boundaries 18 (i.e., oriented perpendicular to the net charge transport direction) which could cause excessive 19 carrier scattering and recombination. Thereby, the resulting grain morphology from the 20 incorporation of Cl likely facilitates efficient charge transport inside the device, leading to 21 improved Voc and FF. Moreover, it has been demonstrated that the Cl ions segregate onto and 22 passivate grain boundaries in MAPbI3 perovskite films to enhance device performance30,31. 8 2-Terminal All-perovskite Tandem Solar Cells 9 We fabricate 2-T all-perovskite tandem cells with 1.75 eV perovskite top cells (~250 nm) 10 and 2.5% Cl-incorporated 1.25 eV perovskite bottom cells (~720 nm), as illustrated in Fig. 3a 11 and 3b. For 2-T all-perovskite monolithic tandem cells, the interconnecting layer (ICL) 12 provides optical and electrical connection as well as full protection of the underlying layers 13 during fabrication of the subsequent bottom subcells. Although several ICLs in 2-T all- 14 perovskite tandem cells have been employed such as SnO2/ITO8, TaTm:F6TCNNQ/C60:Phlm12, 15 and Bis-C60/ITO40 (Supplementary Table 3), it is still highly desired to explore a facile ICL 16 combination obtainable through easily accessible and processable component materials. We use 17 vacuum-processed multiple stack of ultra-thin Ag (1 nm), MoOx (3 nm), and ITO (~120 nm) as 18 the ICL. Ag and MoOx layers were thermally evaporated, and ITO was deposited by sputtering. 19 The MoOx layer prevents damage to the BCP/Ag layers caused by the sputtering of ITO and 20 ensures good contact between Ag and ITO. These multiple layers smoothen the 1.75 eV 21 perovskite layer (Supplementary Fig. 9) and enable an extremely flat and smooth ICL without 22 any pinholes (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Fig. 10), which well protects the underlying 1.75 23 eV perovskite subcell from solvent damage8,40, especially when processing water-containing 24 PEDOT:PSS. 1 Conclusion 2 Overall, we found that incorporation of 2.5% Cl enlarges grains, increases crystallinity and 3 carrier mobility, reduces electronic disorder, and suppresses trap-assisted recombination in 1.25 4 eV low-Eg perovskite films, leading to improved performance of devices using thick absorber 5 layers. Combining the optimised bottom subcells with 1.75 eV wide-Eg top subcell and effective 6 ICLs, we successfully fabricated 2-T all-perovskite tandem cell with efficiencies up to ~21% 7 and promising operational stability. Because of the low-temperature solution processing of 8 absorber layers and full vacuum processing of ICLs, our approach is suitable for the fabrication 9 of high-efficiency 2-T all-perovskite tandem cells on flexible plastic substrates thus offering 10 opportunities for cost reduction.
