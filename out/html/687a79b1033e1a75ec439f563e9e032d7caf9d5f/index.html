Abstract

Transition metal sulfide with a multi-elemental nature represents a class of promising catalysts for oxygen evolution reaction (OER) owing to its good catalytic activity. However, its synthesis remains a grand challenge due to the thermodynamic immiscibility of the constituent multimetallic elements in a sulfide structure. Herein, we for the first time report a high-entropy metal sulfide (HEMS, i.e., (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx) solid solution nanoparticles. Computational and Xray photoelectron spectroscopy analysis suggest that the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx exhibits a synergistic effect among metal atoms that leads to desired electronic states to enhance OER activity. The (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles showed one of the best activities (low overpotential 295 mV at 100 mA cm-2 in 1 M KOH solution) and good durability (only slight polarization after 10 hours by chronopotentiometry) compared with the unary, binary, ternary, and quaternary sulfide counterparts. This work opens up a new synthesis paradigm for high-entropy compound nanoparticles for highly efficient electrocatalysis applications.

Introduction Water splitting by electrochemical means has proven a cost-effective, clean, and efficient energy conversion technology, which has received significant attention in the past decades.[1-6] Further fruition of this technology is currently hampered by the sluggish kinetics of the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) because of its complex four-electron transfer process.[7-10] As such, developing highly efficient, durable, and low-cost electrocatalysts is critical to reduce overpotentials and improve the overall OER performance. Transition metal sulfide (MxSy) catalysts have recently emerged as promising candidates to fill this role as they feature good electrical conductivity and potentially good activity.[11-13] However, MxSy often suffers from poor stability,

including

thermodynamically

instability,[14]

structural

and

morphological

deformation,[15] and catalyst detachment from the substrate[16] under highly oxidative electrochemical conditions. In addition, the unary, binary, and ternary MxSy catalysts with only a few metal elements lack broad compositional tunability. In contrast, the high-entropy metal sulfides (HEMS) with multiply metal elements can achieve great compositional tenability toward the optimal adsorption of reaction intermediates according to the Sabatier principle[17-19] to further boost OER activity.[20] HEMS feature homogeneously mixed multi-metallic elements (≥5) in a sulfide structure. Benefiting from the high-entropy nature to stabilize its phase, HEMS are expected for good structure stability,[21-24] which can potentially improve the OER stability. Tuning the catalystadsorbate interaction via multiple-elemental synergy to adjust the charge state in metal sulfides

3

Page 6

holds good promise for enhancing the catalytic activity. However, synthesizing HEMS in an efficient and high-quality manner remains a daunting task to the research community. To date, only a number of binary and limited ternary MxSy catalysts have been reported due to the immiscibility limit to incorporate additional elements. Figure S1 showcases the bulk phase diagrams of various MxSy systems, where bimetallic (NiFe)Sx, (FeCo)Sx, and (NiCo)Sx are immiscible at most compositional and temperature regimes. The complex elemental compositions with different physiochemical properties are especially difficult to work with using traditional methods (see the distinctive physio-chemical properties of the elemental metals in Table S1). In addition, the synthesis of MxSy heavily relies on the energy-intensive and timeconsuming multi-step process (e.g., pretreatment and post sulfurization treatment),[14, 25-28] which does not permit fast screening and optimization. Moreover, stabilizing the solid solution in a relatively reactive sulfide structure (as compared to oxide and carbide, etc.) posts additional challenge that is beyond the capability of conventional methods.[29-31] In this study, we for the first time synthesized MxSy nanoparticles through a pulse thermal decomposition method[32] to overcome the immiscibility of multiple metallic constituents. In comparison with previous MxSy materials that are limited to 3 metal elements, we achieved an unprecedented step forward to synthesize quinary HEMS nanoparticles (i.e., (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx) (Figure 1a). The resulted (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles were used as an electrocatalyst for OER in 1 M KOH solution, which exhibited an exceptionally low overpotential of 295 mV at a current density of 100 mA cm−2, along with excellent operation stability over 10 h at 100 mA

cm−2, without severe overpotential increase. The performance of the HEMS nanoparticles outperforms most MxSy materials reported in the literature (Table S2), as well as its unary, binary, ternary, and quaternary MxSy counterparts. The overpotentials of unary, binary, ternary, and quaternary MxSy nanoparticles exhibit an inverse relationship with the number of metal elements (Figure 1b), indicating a synergistic effect among metal elements, which we confirmed with computational analysis. The remarkable OER activity and good durability of our HEMS demonstrate its good potential as electrocatalyst for efficient water splitting, meanwhile our pulse thermal decomposition method offers a general approach toward multi-elemental sulfide synthesis for a range of applications.

To prepare the HEMS nanoparticles, metal salt precursors (any metal salt can be used as the metal precursor, e.g., nitride, chloride, acetate, or phosphate, etc) as the metal source and thiourea as the sulfur source were dissolved together in ethanol and loaded on a carbon substrate (i.e., carbonized wood, Figure S2) for pulse thermal decomposition synthesis (Figure S3a). The carbon substrate was applied with a transient current, which ramped the temperature rapidly to ~1650 K and maintained for 55 ms followed by fast quenching (Figure S3b), upon which the HEMS nanoparticles were obtained on the carbon substrate. The color image of the emitted light spectrum captured by a high-speed camera indicates uniform spatial temperature distribution throughout the carbon substrate (insert in Figure S3b). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) suggests that the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles synthesized via pulse thermal decomposition are uniformly dispersed, homogeneous in size (i.e., 11.9 nm), and are of a narrow size distribution ( 2.1 nm) (Figure 2a and b). The (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticle size is tunable by controlling the pulse thermal decomposition time. (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles with particle sizes of 21.8 nm, 34.0 nm, and 40.9 nm can be obtained with pulse thermal decomposition time of 1 s, 5 s, and 10 s, respectively (Figure S4). The lattice fringe spacing of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx is 0.36 nm (Figure 2c), which corresponds to the spacing of (220) plane of a cubic crystal structure. The spacing of (220) plane of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx is slightly larger than that of (FeCoNi)9S8 (i.e., 0.35 nm), which is presumably due to the incorporated larger atoms (e.g., Cr and Mn) in the lattice. The high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) image and energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) elemental maps

(Figure 2d) clearly reveal the homogeneous distribution of Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and S elements throughout the entire nanoparticle without elemental segregation. The successful HEMS synthesis with a composition of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx by overcoming immiscibility issue and stabilizing multi-metallic elements in a sulfide structure is confirmed.

Major diffraction peaks in X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles could be well indexed to (311), (222), (331), and (440) planes of the Fm-3m cubic structure, which in line with (CrMnFeCoNi)9S8 stoichiometry (Figure 3a). Quaternary and ternary nanoparticles featuring (MnFeCoNi)9S8 and (FeCoNi)9S8 synthesized by the same method exhibit similar crystal structures (e.g., (FeCoNi)9S8, JCPDS No.12-0723) (Figure S5). Further, based on the EDS characterization (Figure S6), the content of sulfur and metal elements in nanoparticle is approximately in line with (CrMnFeCoNi)9S8 stoichiometry. The ratio of metal elements Cr/Mn/Fe/Co/Ni is 11.5: 7.3: 12.0: 10.4: 12.9. Mn has a much higher vapor pressure compared to Cr, Fe, Co, and Ni, and evaporates more than Cr, Fe, Co, and Ni. (Figure S7). To disclose the valence states of metallic elements, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed to compare HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx with binary (NiFe)9S8 (Figure S8, Figure 3b and c). As shown in Figure 3b, Fe 2p3/2 peaks of the HEMS shift to higher binding energies compared to those of binary (NiFe)9S8, which indicates an electron-deficient state of Fe in the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx. On the contrary, compared with (FeCoNi)9S8, the Co 2p peaks of the HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx shift to lower binding energies, implying an electron-rich state of Co (Figure 3c). The peak shifts in Fe, Co, Ni, and Mn spectra (Figure S8) imply that Fe and Mn would serve

8

Page 11

as electron donors to Co and Ni, indicating a synergy effect among the metal atoms, which would be beneficial to the oxygen electrocatalysis such as OER.

To understand the crucial role of multi-elemental mixing in activating HEMS with synergistic effect, we performed first-principle density functional theory (DFT) calculation on the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx catalyst. We constructed the HEMS model with a metal atom ratio of 2:1:2:2:2 for Cr/Mn/Fe/Co/Ni, and denoted the HEMS model as (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8. The constructed HEMS (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8 model is shown in Figure 3d, where the Co atoms are surrounded by five S atoms on (100) surface and are chosen as OER active sites, which is consistent with the common understanding in literature[33-37]. For investigating the thermodynamics of OER on multi-elemental transition sulfides, we calculated the free energy evolution process, where OER initiated from H2O adsorption and followed by multiple deprotonation steps and O-O coupling to form *OH, *O, *OOH, and O2 sequentially (Figure 3e). We found that the limiting potential of OER on the HEMS is 1.55 V, which is much lower than that of the unary sulfides (i.e., Cr9S8 (2.74 V), Mn9S8 (2.27 V), Fe9S8 (2.15 V), Co9S8 (1.60 V), and Ni9S8 (1.77 V), Figure S9). It is noteworthy that the second or third proton-electron transfer step as the rate-determining step[38] exhibits the same free energy value as the limiting potential of 1.55 V (Figure 3e), indicating its good potential for OER catalysis (see detail explanation in Supporting Information, Figure S10). A series of DFT calculations were performed to plot the theoretically limiting potential as a function of adsorption energy Eo for a wide range of

9

Page 12

catalytic materials (i.e., a volcano relationship) as a quantitative illustration of the Sabatier principle (Figure 3f). According to the calculation, the adsorption energy Eo is weak for Mn, Fe, and especially Cr, while being too strong for Ni. In contrast, the HEMS (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8 with multi-elemental synergistic effect sits close to the peak of the volcano with a near-neutral adsorption energy, which represents potentially the best catalyst choice for OER. It should be noted that the DFT calculations predicted qualitatively the correct trend of the OER activity between Co9S8 and HEMS. Owing to the simplified computation models, our DFT calculations did not predict quantitatively the performance difference between them as observed by experiment measurements. The multi-metallic synergy effect on adsorption energy Eo can be further explained by the migration in the charge state among multiple metallic atoms. The charge density difference map between (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8 and Co9S8 near Co atom depicts that more electrons were accumulated (yellow iso-surface) around the Co atom (Figure 3g), which is consistent with Bader charge analysis. Bader analysis[39] (Table S3) demonstrates apparent interfacial electron transfer from Fe and Mn atoms to Co atoms in (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8, resulting in more positive charge on Fe (0.19 e−) and Mn (0.34 e−) sites and more negative charge on Co (0.31 e−) site (Figure 3h, Table S3), qualitatively in good agreement with our XPS analysis. The accumulated charge near Co site could enhance the interaction between cationic center and OER intermediates, which agrees well with free energy evolution results.[34-36, 40] We further carried out density of state (DOS) analysis to obtain the d-band center of the metal active site, where the

higher value would suggest stronger catalyst-adsorbate interaction[41] (Figure 3i, Figure S11). In good agreement with the catalytic volcano plot, the d-band center of (Cr2MnFe2Co2Ni2)S8 (Figure 3i) also presents its balanced adsorption energy of OER intermediates that is neither too strong nor too weak, as compared with other unary metal sulfides. As such, the DFT analysis offered a fundamental understanding of our HEMS as a high-performing OER catalyst.

To evaluate the OER performance, the HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles were applied as electrocatalysts in 1 M KOH solution using a typical three-electrode configuration. Binary (NiFe)9S8, ternary (FeCoNi)9S8, and quaternary (MnFeCoNi)9S8 metal sulfides were tested as control groups. The content of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles on carbon substrate was 11.2 wt% (Figure S12). Note that the catalyst mass loading can be readily controlled by tuning the precursor loading on carbon substrate (Figure S13). Figure 4a shows the linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx, (MnFeCoNi)9S8, (FeCoNi)9S8, and (NiFe)9S8. The HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx exhibits an overpotential of 295 mV when reaching a current density of 100 mA cm−2, which is 20, 35, and 50 mV lower than those of (MnFeCoNi)9S8, (FeCoNi)9S8, and (NiFe)9S8, respectively. To exclude the contribution from carbon substrate for OER, a bare carbon substrate without catalysts was tested, where negligible anodic current was measured compared to that by HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx (Figure S14). The OER activity is found

Page 15

to increase monotonically with the number of mixing metallic elements in MxSy. The significantly enhanced electrochemical activity of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles echoes the predicted synergistic effect in HEMS by modeling, which can indeed tailor the electronic structure to modulate the optimal catalyst-adsorbate interactions toward better catalytic performance. The Tafel slope of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx (66 mV dec-1) was derived from the LSV curve, which is smaller than that of (MnFeCoNi)9S8 (76 mV dec-1), (FeCoNi)9S8 (87 mV dec-1), and (NiFe)9S8 (81 mV dec-1) (Figure 4b). In general, a smaller Tafel slope within a reasonable range infers faster kinetics based on the similar mechanism. Thus, the Tafel analysis supports the superior activity of our HEMS catalyst for the OER application. To access the electrochemical active surface area (ECSA), the current densities at the selected potential from the regions of no Faradaic processes were measured (Figure S15a). The (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx catalysts have a double layer capacitance (Cdl) of 84 mF cm-2 (Figure S15b) and ECSA of 2100 cm2, indicating abundant active sites for the electrocatalytic process. The stability of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx catalysts was further evaluated by chronopotentiometry. The (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx maintains decent OER stability for 10 h at 100 mA cm−2 with only a slight potential increase (i.e., < 35 mV) (Figure 4c). On the contrary, the binary (NiFe)9S8 presents much more dramatic potential polarization in the initial 8 h chronopotentiometry test. A multi-step chronopotentiometry test with various current densities was carried out to evaluate the rate performance of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx (Figure 4d). With ramped

13

Page 16

current density from 20 to 50, 100, 150, and 200 mA cm-2, the potential of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx slightly increases and quickly plateaus, indicating good rate performance. The good stability of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx can be attributed to the high-entropy nature to stabilize its phase as well as its interface with the carbon substrate [21, 22] (Figure S16). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx catalyst shows the lowest charge-transfer resistance and least Rct increment after stability test, compared with other metal sulfide catalysts (i.e., binary, ternary, quaternary) (Figure S17, Table S4). After the longterm durability test, the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx showed no significant change in terms of XRD whereas the binary sulfide catalyst (NiFe)9S8 showed structural change after the OER (Figure S18). Further, the S 2p spectra of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx after the stability test also show no binding energy shift (valence change) compared to the ones before OER, indicating the stability of the (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx during OER (Figure S19a). The content of sulfur after OER stability test show no significant leach out compared to that before OER (Figure S19b). The structural stability of HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx was further confirmed by DFT calculations on the formation energy of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx in comparison with the unary MxSy (Table S5). Our computational result indicates that the Gibbs free energy change for the formation of HEMS is 0.91 eV, suggesting that HEMS is thermodynamically more stable than a system of isolated unary metal sulfides. We can synthesize the HEMS on different carbon substrates (carbonized wood, carbon paper, etc). The effect of the carbon substrates can be further optimized by choosing better

Page 17

substrates (such as carbon paper, Figure S20) with better stability (7 mV increment after 100 h Chronopotentiometry at 100 mA cm-2, Figure S21) and less influence on the performance of HEMS. The histogram in Figure 4e and Table S6 manifests the favorable OER activity of HEMS, in which the overpotential and Tafel slope of OER decreases as the metal element number of the catalyst increases (the OER curves and Tafel plots of unary MxSy are shown in Figure S22). The prominent OER activity of HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx catalysts is ascribed to the synergistic effect among multi-metallic elements that modulates the electronic states of active sites, which is unattainable by conventional MxSy. The electrocatalytic performance of (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx is comparable to the best MxSy catalysts reported in the literature (Figure 4f). Compared with other transition-metal sulfide catalysts synthesized via conventional methods, our HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx demonstrates outstanding OER performance in terms of low overpotential, small Tafel slope, and good stability.

Conclusion In this study, we synthesized homogeneously mixed and entropy-stabilized HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanoparticles through a pulse thermal decomposition method to overcome the immiscibility limits. Computational efforts suggest the migration in the charge state among multiple metallic sites, which modulates the electronic structure to affect the OER performance. When tested as an OER catalyst, our HEMS (CrMnFeCoNi)Sx nanocatalyst validates the computational prediction in terms of the synergistic effect for a good catalytic activity and long durability. The HEMS offers good potential to be applied as electrocatalyst while the synthesis method offers a general approach to expand the compositional space of this class of materials. This study therefore opens up a new door toward synthesizing various high-entropy compound nanoparticles (oxides, sulfides, phosphides, and selenides, etc.) for a range of energy and catalysis applications.
