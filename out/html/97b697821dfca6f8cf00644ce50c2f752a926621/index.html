Abstract Co-doping of nanostructured carbon with boron and nitrogen has emerged as an attractive strategy to modulate the catalytic activity. The combination of B, C, and N forms a BCN framework with different chemical bonding networks ranging from pure hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) to graphene. Herein, we employ density functional theory (DFT) calculations to systematically investigate the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) activity of different possible configurations, including individually doped structures with boron or nitrogen, a variety of h-BN domains within the carbon lattice, or carbon domains within a h-BN lattice. We show that the interface between h-BN domains and graphene exhibits unique catalytic behavior that can preferentially drive the production of H2O2. We

Page 2

synthesize several BCN materials experimentally with a facile synthetic method and demonstrate the existence of such h-BN domains using x-ray spectroscopy. These synthesized structures yielded higher activity and selectivity toward H2O2 than structures with individual B- or N-doping. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first example of h-BN domains in carbon identified as a novel system for electrochemical production of H2 O2 .

Introduction Heteroatom co-doping has emerged as an appealing strategy to modify the electronic and surface structures of carbon-based materials in order to tune catalytic activity towards different reactions.1-6 Co-doping with boron and nitrogen is particularly attractive due to the environmental abundance of these elements along with the ability to form a wide range of possible active sites. In extreme cases where high boron and nitrogen concentrations are used, domains of hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) are formed within the carbon lattice. 5,7-9 Previous studies have shown that segregation of either graphene islands in a planar h-BN matrix, or h-BN islands in a graphene matrix is thermodynamically favorable.5,7-9 Depending on the boron, nitrogen and carbon stoichiometry, some hybridized and randomly distributed domains of h-BN and C phases can be formed with compositions ranging from pure h-BN to pure graphene. Such hybrid boron-carbon-nitrogen (BCN) systems provide unique electronic structures that can be used to tune the catalytic properties by varying the relative amounts of h-BN and graphene. Leveraging this behaviour, BCN materials have been tested as supercapacitor electrodes10,11 and for several catalytic applications, such as the hydrogen evolution reaction,12 oxygen evolution reaction13, and CO2 reduction reaction14.

Page 3

In particular, BCN materials have recently drawn increasing interest as catalysts for the four-electron oxygen reduction reaction (ORR).15-21 In many of these instances, however, the exact nature of the active site motif in the BCN material remains elusive and much remains to be understood towards identifying structure-activity relationships. In addition to the 4e- ORR process, the 2e- process to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is also of particular interest for decentralized applications such as water treatment in remote areas.22 Recently, theory and experiment has been used to investigate various catalysts for the electrochemical synthesis of H2O2, including noble metals23-25, metal alloys24,26,27 and carbon-based materials28-33. Though defective carbons have shown good activity and selectivity towards H2O2, there are numerous defect site structures that can be formed during preparation and it is a challenge to selectively tailor synthetic processes to induce the formation of the most active and selective active site structure(s).29,33 In contrast, BNC materials provide a more tunable system since the concentration and types of Band N- doping can be controlled by careful selection of synthetic conditions and precursor molecules. Furthermore, the suitability of heteroatom-doped carbon structures for H2O2 production28,31 suggests it is of interest to study the effects of B and N codoping in an attempt to discover highly active and selective catalyst materials for this reaction.

Herein we use density functional theory (DFT) calculations to systematically study the ORR activity of a variety of different h-BN compositions in a carbon lattice. In addition, we study the activity of different carbon domains in a h-BN lattice. We pay particular attention to a variety of possible h-BN/graphene interfaces and show that the interface

Page 4

presents intrinsically different catalytic behavior. Our calculations suggest that the interface of h-BN domains in a graphene substrate drives the two-electron ORR, leading to H2O2 as the major product. We also computationally screen different N-doped, Bdoped and carbon defect structures to achieve a comprehensive understanding and comparison among potential active sites. Guided by theoretical results, we synthesize carbon materials with h-BN/graphene interfaces by an approach involving oxidative polymerization of a monomer containing both B and N atoms, followed by high temperature pyrolysis. Spectroscopic characterization shows that the h-BN domains and N-doped sites co-exist in these graphitic structures and contribute to the observed ORR performance. We found that h-BN domains in graphene structures provide higher activity and selectivity for the two-electron ORR process in comparison to individual B- or Ndoped structures. This work provides insights into design and development of heteroatom doped carbon structures for electrochemical production of H2O2, which can be used for the design of improved catalyst materials or extended towards other reactions of interest.

The presented analysis herein is purely thermodynamic, which has played an essential role providing insight into the nature of active site structures and guiding the design and optimization of various catalysts.24,26,33,34 Thermodynamic predicted activity volcanoes have also been shown to be in close agreement with predicted kinetic activity volcanoes.35 Therefore, there is a close connection between the kinetic and thermodynamic formulations for oxygen reduction reaction.

Figure 1b displays the calculated limiting potential (See computational details) as a function of OH* adsorption energy (∆𝐺:; ) for both the two- and four-electron ORR. The first step in the reduction of O2 is the formation of OOH*, which is a common intermediate in both the two- and four-electron pathways. As a result, the two- and four-electron activity volcano maps (Figure 1b) are identical on the right side (i.e., relatively weak OH* binding energy). Previous studies suggest that materials sitting on the right leg of the two-electron volcano bind oxygen weakly (∆𝐺:; > 1.1 𝑒𝑉 ), and tend to favor H2O2, whereas materials on the left leg possess a strong thermodynamic driving force towards the four-electron H2O forming process.24,26,33 Based on our DFT results, the BCN configurations with carbon domains in a h-BN lattice represent strong oxygen binding energies (∆𝐺:; < 1.1 𝑒𝑉), which leads to further reduction of the OOH* intermediate (eq. 6) and thus the H2O forming four-electron pathway. In all these configurations, the interface between C atoms in graphene domains and B atoms in the h-BN phase are the active site, and the ORR activity varies by changing the local chemical environment of the C-B bond.

On the other hand, all of the BCN configurations with h-BN domains in graphene substrates bind oxygen weakly (∆𝐺:; < 1.1 𝑒𝑉), leading to the protonation of OOH* to form H2O2 (eq. 3) as the main product. In all of these instances, a boron atom in the hBN/graphene interface is the active site. The local chemical environment of the boron is the determining factor for the wide range of observed activity. It is also important to note that there is a tradeoff between activity and selectivity. Far to the right side of the volcano, activating oxygen to form OOH* is the limiting step due to the weak interaction. Therefore,

higher overpotentials and lower limiting potentials are expected for the very weak oxygen binding configurations.

As hetero atom-doping is known to create a wide range of defects in carbon-based materials,36 we also include the possibility of having N-dopants, B-dopants and co-doped N and B (BN) in the defective carbon. Previously, we have demonstrated that N-doping in the most thermodynamically stable defects (Stone Waals defect (55-77)) drives the four-electron reaction.34 Figure 1b shows that while N-, B- and BN-dopants in defect free graphene (labelled in Figure 1b as N-Gr, B-Gr and BN-domains, respectively) drive the two-electron process, whereas the incorporation of N- and B-dopants into the 55-77 defect structures (labelled in Figure 1b as N-Gr-D and B-Gr-D, respectively) leads to the four-electron reaction.

Materials Synthesis and Characterizations To gain a deeper understanding on the effects of N doping and B, N-co-doping on ORR performance, we prepared a series of carbon based materials by varying the ratios of two different precursors during polymerization, namely aniline and 3-aminophenylboronic acid (3-APBA). This is a modified version of previously reported methods for preparing nitrogen-doped carbons from a polyaniline hydrogel precursor38,39 (Fig.2a). We hypothesize it may be easier to form stable h-BN domains using a precursor such as 3APBA since both boron and nitrogen are present in the same molecule. The initial loading of boron versus nitrogen can also be tuned by changing the relative ratio of 3APBA to aniline. Ammonium persulfate was used as the oxidant to promote the copolymerization of aniline and 3-APBA to form poly(aniline-co-3-aminophenylboronic acid). Phytic acid was used as the crosslinker and also as an activation agent to create porosity and edge site exposures in the carbon materials as shown in our previous work38. After the formation of the hydrogel, the sample was freeze-dried and carbonized at 1000 o

C for six hours under inert atmosphere. The final products were named as follows (refer

to Fig.2a for nomenclature): N-C for samples made from only the aniline monomer (m=0,

The as-synthesized samples were characterized by several different techniques. Figure 2 shows the results from SEM imaging and gas sorption measurements. For N-C made from the aniline monomer, a macroscopic network of particles was formed with macropores of micron dimensions (Fig. 2b). The only monomer used for BN-C1 was 3APBA, which tends to have a self-hardening effect since the polymer can be selfcrosslinked through the intermolecular reaction between boronic acid groups and imines.40 This leads to the formation of irregular spherical particles, as evidenced from the SEM images (Fig. 2d). When the sample was made from the mixture of aniline and 3APBA, porous network was formed as the case of N-C, as shown in the SEM image of BN-C2 in Fig.2c. Gas sorption further confirms the effect from such co-polymerization. BN-C1 gives a BET surface area of 74 m2/g. When 2/3 of the monomer was changed to aniline (BN-C2), the surface area improves significantly to 750 m2/g. Gas sorption measurements on N-C gives a comparable surface area of 630 m2/g (Fig. 3e,f). All these carbon materials showed significant fraction of micropores smaller than 2 nm.

To gain more insights into the local electronic structures in these materials, and in particular to more accurately characterize the types of nitrogen and boron in the carbon structures, we employed near-edge x-ray absorption fine structure spectroscopy (NEXAFS). The C 1s NEXAFS (Fig. 3d) for all samples shows the spectral fingerprints of sp2-hybridized carbon sheets and this is essentially unperturbed by B/N co-doping. These fingerprints consist of peaks at 285.4 and 292 eV, corresponding to the 1s→ π* and 1s→ σ* C=C bond resonances of graphitic carbon, respectively.42,43 Comparison of the B and N K-edge NEXAFS for BN-C1, BN-C2 and N-C materials with that of pure hBN gives more insight on the types of the dopant bond motifs (Fig. 3e-f). The close resemblance between the B and N K-edge of h-BN, BN-C1 and BN-C2 suggests the existence of a high concentration of sp2-hybridized B-N bonds in BN-C and BN-C2. Both BN-C1 and BN-C2 show the characteristics of h-BN with a sharp B 1s→ π* at about

Page 13

191.3 eV and three 1s→ σ* resonances at 197.3 eV, 198.7 eV and 203.4 eV. It is also worth noting that there is another sharp peak at 193.4 eV for BN-C1 and BN-C2 that is absent in h-BN. This additional peak arises from B-O motifs44 that is consistent with the high amount of O content observed in the XPS spectroscopy for these two samples. N Kedge NEXAFS further confirms the clustering of B and N dopants in the BN-C1 and BNC2 samples. Both samples show the main N 1s spectral fingerprints of h-BN with a π* resonance at 400.3 eV and two σ* features at 407 eV and 414.5 eV respectively (Fig.3f). For the N-C sample where N is the only active dopant, two main π* peaks arise at 398 eV and 400 eV. The sharp π* at 400 eV and σ* at 406 eV are in close fit with the spectrum for graphitic N, where a C atom in the graphene ring structure is replaced with a N atom. The peak at 398 eV on the other hand indicates less electronegative N-C bonds that could be assigned to pyridinic or pyrrolic N.45,46 Thus, the N dopants in N-C sample exist in the form of mixtures of quaternary nitrogen, pyridinic nitrogen and pyrrolic nitrogen. These two peaks are also found in the B, N co-doped samples, indicating that BN-C1 and BNC2 not only consist of isolated patches of h-BN and boron oxide, but also a significant amount of single N dopant sites. To further confirm these h-BN motifs are indeed in the form of small isolated patches of hexagonal h-BN within the carbon framework rather than distinct h-BN sheets, High Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM) was performed for BN-C1 sample (Fig. 4). From the energy filtered TEM images, homogeneous distribution of all four elements, B, C, N and O is observed, and no phase separation or formation of large h-BN domains were identified.

ORR performance The ORR activities for N-C, BN-C1, and BN-C2 were tested with a rotating ring disk electrode setup at 1600 rpm in O2-saturated electrolyte (Fig. 5a,b). For the disk current, all three samples exhibited a similar onset-potential (defined as the potential at which a current density of 0.5mA/cm2 is achieved) of 0.80 V vs. RHE, indicating the presence of similar active sites within these materials. These active centers could very well be the single N-sites that are present in similar concentrations in all these samples and have previously been reported active for the 4e- ORR.34,47 H2O2 production is indicated by the 16

Page 17

ring current that gives a slightly lower onset potential of ca. 0.79 V vs. RHE (defined as the potential at which a current density of 0.25mA/cm2 is achieved) for BN-C1 and BNC2. On the other hand, N-C only starts to produce a similar ring current at a potential of ca. 0.67V, much lower than that observed on B,N- co-doped samples. This indicates there are distinct active centers in BN-C1 and BN-C2 that can facilitate the two-electron ORR at higher disc electrode potentials. It is worth noting that a slightly higher onset potential than the thermodynamic limit of 0.7 V vs. RHE is observed, which has also been found on other high surface area materials such as Pt-Hg/C nanoparticles and various nanostructured carbon-based materials.24,32,48 This could be attributed to a possible pHindependent rate-determining step in the 2e- ORR reaction that is currently under study. 33

BN-C1 and BN-C2 both have unique h-BN clusters that are absent in N-C. As shown

in the theoretical calculations, BN co-doping (13%) in the form of isolated h-BN clusters is expected to be much more active for the two-electron ORR process. Therefore, the exceptionally high two-electron ORR activity for BN-C1 and BN-C2 may be assigned to the isolated h-BN clusters and whereas, for N-C, the active center is still the single Nsites. This result suggests h-BN sites favors the two-electron ORR process and single Nsites are more favorable for the four-electron ORR process, consistent with other reports in literature where four-electron ORR dominates over two-electron for nitrogen-doped carbon34,47,49. For BN-C1 and BN-C2, which consists of both active site structures, there may be competing effects for the two ORR pathways, as shown schematically in Fig. 5c. Based on our experimental characterization, BN-C1 has the largest content of BN clusters, whereas for BN-C2, a relatively lower concentration of h-BN versus single N-sites is identified. Thus, it may explain why BN-C1 has the highest selectivity for H2O2, while

Page 18

N-C has the lowest selectivity for H2O2 and BN-C2 has an intermediate selectivity between these two. This is in good agreement with our theoretical calculations (Fig. 1b), predicting activity for h-BN domain in a carbon lattice that comparable with PtHg4, whereas nitrogen-doped in defective carbon has a stronger oxygen binding energy and favors the four-electron process.

To systematically compare the performance of B, N co-doped carbon with other types of carbons, boron-doped carbon and defective carbon structures were also explored and were not found to be as active and selective as BN-C1 (Fig.S4). The performance of state-of-the-art catalyst for 2e- ORR is also summarized in Table S1. Compared with other carbon-based materials (defective carbons and nitrogen-doped carbons), BN-C1 is among the most active catalysts for 2e- ORR in base condition. Moreover, this highly tunable BNC system opens up more possibility for the further development of catalysts with improved performance.

Conclusion In this work, we used density functional theory (DFT) calculations to systematically investigate the ORR activity of different B- and N-doped carbon structures, including a wide range of h-BN/graphene interfaces. We showed that the interface between possible h-BN domains and the host graphene lattice exhibits unique catalytic behavior that preferentially drives the production of H2O2 via a two-electron ORR. Inspired by this theoretical finding, we further designed a facile method to prepare a series of carbon materials that consist of isolated patches of h-BN within the carbon framework. By tuning the initial co-monomer precursor ratios, we controlled the BET surface area and the total content of B and N dopants in the carbon structures.

Coupled with a

comprehensive study of three other carbon materials containing solely N- or B- dopants, in addition to various C defects, we demonstrate that co-doped B and N in a graphene lattice provides the most promising catalyst for H2O2 production, consistent with theoretical findings. By applying different characterization techniques, including XPS, NEXAFS and HRTEM, we confirm the presence of h-BN domains within the carbon framework of these materials, along with the coexistence of single N-doped sites, which drive the four-electron ORR in parallel and reduce the overall selectivity toward H2O2. This work designs a new type of h-BN-containing carbon catalyst that is promising for electrochemical production of H2O2. The insight for this new system also provides guidance for the design of other improved carbon catalysts.

Methods Computational Details. The simulations are handled using the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE). 50 The electronic structure calculations are performed using QUANTUM ESPRESSO program package.51 The electronic wavefunctions are expanded in plane waves up to a cutoff energy of 500 eV, while the electron density is represented on a grid with an energy cutoff of 5000 eV. Core electrons are approximated with ultrasoft pseudopotentials.52 We use the BEEF-vdW exchange-correlation functional,53 which has been shown to accurately describe chemisorption as well as physisorption properties on graphene. All the structures are modeled as one layer with super cells of lateral sizes 4x4, to 8x8. The Brillouin zone is sampled with (4x4x1) and (2x2x1) Monkhorst-Pack k-points. A vacuum region of about 20 Å is used to decouple the periodic replicas. We use the computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) approach which exploits that the chemical potential of a proton-electron pair is equal to that of gas-phase H2, at Uelec = 0.0 V vs. the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE). The effect of the electrode potential on the free energy of the intermediates is taken into account through shifting the electron energy by –eUelec where e and Uelec are the elementary charge and the electrode potential, respectively. We consider the associative mechanism with OOH*, O* and OH* as ORR intermediates. The calculated limiting potential, defined as the highest potential at which all the reaction steps are downhill in free energy diagram, is used as a metric for activity of different configurations. It is worth mentioning that the presented theoretical results in this work are based on thermodynamic analysis which have played an essential role in providing insights on the nature of active sites and guiding the design and optimization of various catalysts. Thermodynamic predicted

activity volcano for transition metals has been shown to be in close agreement with the predicted kinetic activity volcano. Therefore, there is a close connection between the kinetic and thermodynamic formulations for four-electron oxygen reduction reaction.

Catalyst synthesis. All the chemicals and solvents were readily available and purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. For the synthesis of Defective-C aerogel precursor, phenol (0.522 g) and 35wt% formaldehyde (0.825 mL) were dissolved in n-propanol (2.5 mL). Then 37% HCl solution (0.463 mL) was added into the solution and heated at 85 oC for 24 hours in air.54 The precursor for boron-doped carbon was synthesized using a similar method while adding boric acid was used as the precursor in ethanol.55 In a typical synthesis, 2 mmol phenol, 3 mmol phloroglucinol, 0.017 mmol Na2CO3, and 1 mmol boric acid were dissolved in 2.34 mL ethanol, and 0.787 mL formaldehyde solution was added into the mixture. Then the solution was heated at 80 oC for 24 hours for aerogel formation. Nitrogen-doped carbon was prepared from polyaniline hydrogel by using phytic acid as the crosslinker.38,39 Specifically, 2.5 mmol ammonium persulfate was dissolved in 1mL DI water as solution A; 1 mmol phytic acid,and 5 mmol aniline were dissolved into 2 mL DI water solution as solution B. Then solution A and B were mixed while the mixture was cooled in an ice bath. After a few minutes, a stable hydrogel was formed and freezedried overnight to remove water. BN co-doped carbon applies similar methods except replacing some of the aniline monomer with 3-aminophenylboronic acid (3-APBA). For BN-C, 5 mmol 3-APBA monomer instead of aniline was added; for BN-C-2:1, 5/3 mmol aniline and 10/3 mmol 3-APBA were added; and for BN-C-1:2, 10/3 mmol aniline and 5/3 mmol 3-APBA were added. All these aerogel precursors were carbonized in a tubular

furnace under nitrogen atomosphere using a heating ramp rate of 2 oC/min up to 600 oC, 5 oC/min from 600 to 1000 oC, and then holding at 1000 oC for 6 hours to get the desired graphitic carbon products.

Physical and chemical characterization. HRTEM and energy filtered TEM were carried out using a spherical aberration (image) corrected FEI Titan operated at 300 kV. Elemental composition was measured with XPS (PHI 5000 Versaprobe, Al Kα source). Nitrogen sorption experiments were performed using an Autosorb iQ2 (Quantachrome) low-pressure gas sorption analyzer with 99.999 % N2 at 77 K. Raman spectra were obtained using a WiTech confocal Raman microscope equipped with a 532 nm NiYAG laser. Soft X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) measurements on the material were conducted on beamline 8-2 at Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource (SSRL). A ring current of 500 mA and a 500 mm−1 spherical grating monochromator were used, operated with 35 µm entrance and exit slits, providing ~2.0 · 1010 ph/s at 0.25 eV resolution in a 1 mm2 beam spot.

Electrochemical characterization. If not specified, all the electrochemical testing were carried out in 0.1M KOH in water (Fisher Chemical 1kg P250-1 Potassium Hydroxide Certified ACS), pH measured at 13. Electrochemical tests were carried out through either a rotation ring disk electrode (RRDE) or rotation disk electrode (RDE) setup in a four/three electrode setup utilizing a graphite rod counter electrode, and a Ag/AgCl reference electrode for alkaline-based experiments. In case of acidic condition, an Hg/HgSO4 reference electrode was used.

Catalysts were prepared through an ink-based spin-coating process. For testing in alkaline solutions, the working electrode ink was prepared by mixing 1 mg of material, 98 µL of ethanol and 2 µL of cation-exchanged Nafion 117 solution(Sigma-Aldrich). For testing in acidic conditions, the ink was prepared by mixing 1 mg of material, 98.66 µL of ethanol and 1.34 µL of Nafion 117 solution (Sigma-Aldrich). 10 µL of the resulting catalyst ink was then drop-casted onto a glassy carbon (GC) disk working electrode (Sigradur G HTW Hochtemperatur-Werkstoffe GmbH) spinning at an initial rate of 300rpm and advanced to 600rpm to obtain a final catalyst loading of 0.51 mg/cm2.

Electrochemical measurements were carried out by sweeping the disk potential between 0.2 V and 1.1 V vs. RHE at 10 mV/s while holding the Pt ring at 1.2 V vs. RHE to oxidize H2O2 species formed on the disc electrode, allowing for ORR product quantification. The potential scale was calibrated to the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE) using a Pt wire (Sigma-Aldrich) as the working electrode in a H2-saturated electrolyte, and a value of 0.959 V was obtained. The ORR activity was determined by subtracting the current obtained in an N2-saturated electrolyte from that obtained in an O2-saturated electrolyte.
