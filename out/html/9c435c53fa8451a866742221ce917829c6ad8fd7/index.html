Abstract: Hydrogen production through facile photocatalytic water splitting is regarded as a promising strategy for solving global energy problems. Transition metal carbides (MXenes) have recently drawn attention as potential co-catalyst candidates for photocatalysts. Here, we report niobium pentoxide/carbon/niobium carbide (MXene) hybrid materials (Nb2O5/C/Nb2C) as photocatalysts for hydrogen evolution from water splitting. The Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composites were synthesized by a one-step CO2 oxidation of Nb2CTx. Nb2O5 grew homogeneously on Nb2C after mild oxidation during which some amorphous carbon also formed. With an optimized oxidation time of 1.0 h, the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C shows the highest hydrogen generation rate (7.81 μmol·h-1gcat-1), which is 4 times as high as that of pure Nb2O5. The enhanced performance of Nb2O5/C/Nb2C can be attributed to the intimate contact between the Nb2O5 and the conductive Nb2C, and the separation of photogenerated charge carriers at the Nb2O5/Nb2C interface, showing the promise of transition metal carbide as co-catalysts for photocatalytic hydrogen production.

clean and recyclable energy source has attracted great attention because of increasing global energy demand and environmental concerns. The generation of hydrogen from water using photocatalysis is regarded as one of the promising strategies for solving global energy problems[1, 2]. Hundreds of semiconductorbased photocatalysts have been developed for hydrogen evolution reaction, such as TiO2[3], CoO[4], MoS2[5], C3N4[6] and so on.[7] Among the most promising photocatalysts is niobium pentoxide (Nb2O5), with excellent chemical and photochemical stability, and has been tested for reactions including photocatalytic degradation of organic pollutant [8, 9], photocatalytic selective oxidation of benzylamine[10], and water splitting to produce hydrogen[11, 12]. However, due to the high recombination rate of photogenerated electron–hole pairs, the photocatalytic activity of Nb2O5 is rather limited. To improve the photocatalytic activity of Nb2O5, several strategies have been proposed to enhance the charge carriers separation in Nb2O5. Photocatalytic activity of Nb2O5 can be improved by loading metal (Pt[13], Au, Cu[11]), nonmetal (N[14]), or metal oxide (NiO[11], TiO2[15], ZnO[16]) as co-catalyst. These co-catalysts can effectively prevent the recombination of electrons and holes, thereby enhancing the photocatalytic efficiency. For example, due to the conduction band edge potential of Nb2O5 (−0.45 eV) is more negative than that of rutile TiO2 (−0.19 eV), the photo-generated electrons in the conduction band of Nb2O5 will transfer to rutile TiO2. Moreover, the photo-generated holes can reversely transfer under the driving force of Fermi energy difference between the two semiconductors, thus, the separation efficiency of photogenerated electron–hole pairs was enhanced[15]. Besides, a series of Pt, Au, Cu and NiO nanoparticles were loaded as cocatalyst to enhance the photocatalytic activity of Nb 2O5, and Pt loaded niobium pentoxide catalyst exhibited the highest hydrogen production rate. This enhanced photocatalytic activity is due to the fact that well-dispersed Pt clusters into the niobium pentoxide texture can act as sink for photo-induced charge carriers and promote interfacial charge transfer process [11]. However, the high price and scarcity of the Pt co-catalyst restrict its large-scale application. MXenes, a new family of 2D transition-metal carbides and carbonitrides, have first been reported in 2011[17] and have been widely studied in recent years[18-20]. MXene is synthesized by etching the bulk layered MAX phase, where the “M” element is a transition metal, “A” is a group A element such as Al, and “X” is either C or N[21]. MXene is composed of stacks of 2D early transition metal carbides and/or carbonitrides with general formula Mn+1XnTx, where n = 1, 2, or 3, and Tx represents various surface termination (–OH, –O, and/or F groups)[21, 22]. Because the volumetric capacitance of MXene is significantly higher than those of conventional carbon materials, MXene has attracted

Page 2

FULL PAPER intense attention in the field of electrochemical supercapacitors[23-25]. Furthermore, strong connection between MXene and various semiconductors can easily be constructed due to numerous hydrophilic functionalities (–OH and –O) on the surface of MXene. The excellent electronic conductivity of MXene make it easier for charge carrier to transfer from the semiconductor to the MXene, and a Schottky barrier will be formed at the semiconductor/MXene interface, which will improve the separation efficiency of electron-hole[26, 27]. In addition, the charge accumulation in the MXene can cause a negative shift and alignment of the Fermi level and improve the photocatalytic hydrogen evolution.[28] More importantly, the HER activity for photocatalyst is related to the Gibbs free energy for hydrogen adsorption (ΔGH), and the most desirable value for ΔGH should be close to zero to obtained the highest HER performance. Interestingly, MXene with appropriate surface terminations shows near-zero ΔGH, such as hydrogenated W 2C, oxidized Mo2C[29], O-terminated Ti3C2,[27] and O-terminated Nb2C and Ti2C[30]. With these outstanding properties, MXene has been considered as one of the most promising photocatalysts or cocatalysts and hence has been studied immensely[26, 27, 30-32]. Our previous study indicates that[26], the TiO2/Nb2CTx (Nb2CTx as the co-catalyst), shows the higher photocatalytic activity as compared to that of the TiO2/Ti3C2Tx and the TiO2/Ti2CTx. Moreover, MXene has been used as a precursor for oxide recently[33, 34], and in the partly oxidized MXene, the oxide is intimately contact with the MXene. In this study, we investigate the role of Nb2C MXene phase as co-catalyst in Nb2O5/C/Nb2C for H2 evolution. Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composite photocatalysts are prepared by oxidizing the surface of the Nb2CTx to generate a surface Nb2O5 layer by using CO2 as the mild oxidant. We found that the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composite photocatalyst exhibits significant enhanced catalytic activity as compared with that of Nb2O5 and the mechanically mixed Nb2O5/Nb2CTx sample under identical experimental conditions. We further elucidate the role of Nb2C as an active co-catalyst for the Nb2O5, which suggests considerable value of MXene materials as co-catalyst in photocatalysis.

PL emissions originate from the radiative recombination of photo-generated electron–hole pairs. Therefore, it is a useful method to understand the fate of electron–hole pairs in semiconductor by measuring their PL emission intensity. In general, lower emission intensity indicates higher separation efficiency of electron-hole pairs[44] in the semiconductor photocatalysts. PL emission spectra of the Nb2CTx, oxidized Nb2CTx, and Mix-NN with 325 nm excitation wavelength at room temperature are displayed in Figure 5B. As shown in Figure 5B, the emission peak intensity of the NCN-0.5 and NCN-1.0 samples decreased compared with Nb2O5, suggesting that the NCN-0.5 and NCN-1.0 had a lower recombination rate of photogenerated charge carriers. The efficient transfer of the electron could be ascribed to the high conductivity of interior Nb 2C, which was effective in electron–hole pair separation. Moreover, the interfacial charge transfer rate was improved by the Schottky barrier formed at the Nb2O5/Nb2C interface, thus, the recombination of photoinduced electron–hole pairs was inhibited. Besides, with the increase of oxidation time, the emission peak intensity of the oxidized Nb2CTx increased. By increasing the oxidation time, more Nb2O5 can be produced, and more electron–hole pairs were generated under UV illumination. However, the surface Nb2O5 layer was thicker, and the crystallite size was increased as confirmed by XRD patterns, which is detrimental to electron transfer from the surface Nb 2O5 surface layer to Nb2C. Thus, the PL intensity of the oxidized Nb2CTx becomes stronger with the increase of oxidation time. In combination of the above characterization (XRD, Raman spectra, SEM, TEM, DRS, and XPS) results, it can be concluded that the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composite photocatalysts have been produced through a facile CO2 oxidation process with Nb2O5 particles homogeneously grown on the surface of Nb 2C and amorphous carbon in-between. Such a composite photocatalyst could take advantage of the photocatalytic activity of the Nb 2O5 nanoparticles and the high conductivity of Nb 2C, which is beneficial to the fast electron transfer from the Nb2O5 to Nb2C, thus increasing the separation efficiency of the photo-generated electrons and holes, as demonstrated below. The NCN-1.0 electrode under light irradiation exhibited a higher photocurrent than that of the Nb2O5 and Nb2CTx electrode, suggesting the positive synergistic effect between Nb2O5 and Nb2C. The enhancement of the photocurrent illustrated the increase in the photoinduced carrier transport rate and the improvement in photogenerated electron–hole pair separation. Nb2C serves as an acceptor and a transporter for the electrons generated from the Nb2O5 covered on the surface of Nb2C. The formation of a Schottky junction at the Nb2O5/Nb2CTx interface can efficiently capture the photoinduced electrons and inhibit the electron diffusing back to the Nb2O5, which will separate the photoelectrons and holes and increase the photocurrent[26, 27]. Furthermore, the extent of electron–hole recombination in the NCN-1.0 sample was also supported by the above PL measurements (Figure 5B). Thus, the photocatalytic activity of the Nb2O5 will be improved by the enhanced separation of electron-hole. The NCN-1.5 sample shows lower hydrogen evolution rate of 2.58 μmol h-1gcat-1 due to the thicker Nb2O5 layer and the decrease amount of Nb2C in the sample. In the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composites, the electron-hole pairs can only generate in the Nb2O5 portion. When the oxidation time is 0.5 h (NCN-0.5), the molar ratio of Nb2O5/Nb2C is 0.36, which indicated that the main portion of the catalyst is Nb2C. Although the electron-hole pairs can be separated between the Nb2O5 and Nb2C and have low PL intensity, the hydrogen production rate is still low due to the limited number of electrons generated on the Nb2O5. With the increase of oxidation time to 1.0 h (NCN-1.0), the molar ratio of Nb2O5/Nb2C increase to 0.51, more electron-hole pairs can be generated on the Nb2O5 and more electrons can move to Nb2C for hydrogen production reaction, which shows higher hydrogen production rate than the NCN-0.5. Meanwhile, the thickness of Nb2O5 layer increases, which is adverse to the migration of the electrons to the Nb2C, thus NCN-1.0 shows higher PL intensity than NCN-0.5. When the oxidation time increased to 1.5 h (NCN-1.5) with the Nb2O5/Nb2C molar ratio of 1.07, the Nb2O5 layer is much thicker than that of NCN-1.0. Therefore, only a few electrons can move to Nb2C, the high recombination of the electron-hole pairs accounts for the low hydrogen production rate and the high PL intensity. The mechanically mixed sample presents lower activity (1.47 μmol h-1gcat-1) than that of Nb2O5. This result can be explained by the prevention of efficient light absorption by Nb2O5 owing to the suspended Nb2C in the solution that reflects/scatters the light from absorbing by Nb2O5. In addition, the loose contact between Nb2O5 and Nb2C hinders efficient charge transfer from Nb2O5 to Nb2C. On the contrary, in the chemically formed Nb2O5/Nb2C composites, as indicated by the SEM and TEM images, the Nb2C materials are covered by Nb2O5 nanoparticles. This intimate contact between Nb2O5 and Nb2C ensures sufficient light absorption by Nb2O5 and rapid charge transfer from Nb2O5 to Nb2C.

For example, when the TiO2 combine with carbon, the photogenerated electrons can be scavenged by carbon. Thus, the presence of C decreases the possibility for the recombination of electron-hole pairs.[57, 58] In addition, the separation efficiency of photoexcited electron-hole pairs can also be increased due to the strong electronic interaction between zero-band gap carbon and Nb2O5[59]. In the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C composites, the Nb2O5 was connected to the Nb2C by a thin film of disordered carbon, and the carbon seems to function as a “binder” to maintain the structural integrity and the electron channel between the Nb 2O5 and Nb2C for the separation of photo-generated electron and holes. Therefore, the oxidized Nb2CTx prepared by a one-step CO2 oxidation technique shows a remarkably higher hydrogen yield. Proposed reaction mechanism In light of the above characterization results from XRD, Raman spectra, SEM, DRS, XPS, PL, and PEC, a mechanism of photocatalytic hydrogen production is proposed to illustrate the high photocatalytic activity of oxidized Nb2CTx as shown in Scheme 1. Under ultraviolet irradiation, the surface Nb2O5 is excited to produce electrons (e−) and holes (h+), then the photogenerated electrons can be transferred from the conduction band (CB) of Nb2O5 to Nb2C due to the presence of carbon species and the electronic conductivity of Nb2C[22, 60]. Carbon species, such as the carbon quantum dots,[61, 62] graphite carbon,[63] and amorphous carbon[64] were widely used as the electron reservoir or electron transport bridge in the photocatalyst due to their excellent electronic conductivity. Therefore, it is possible that the amorphous carbon formed between the Nb2O5 and Nb2C act as the electron transport bridge for the electron transfer from Nb2O5 to Nb2C. Furthermore, with the accumulation of the negative charge in Nb2C and the positive charges in Nb2O5, a space-charge layer is formed, and the CB and VB (valence band) of Nb2O5 are bent ‘upward’. Therefore, a Schottky junction is formed at the Nb2O5/Nb2C interface, which will prevent the electrons from transferring back to the Nb2O5. As a result, the Schottky junction can serve as an electron sink to efficiently capture the photo-induced electrons and inhibit the electron diffusing back to the Nb2O5, which greatly enhances the photocatalytic activity of Nb 2O5. Most importantly, the MXene materials were considered as promissing HER catalysts, thanks to their near-zero ΔGH for hydrogen adsorption with appropriate surface terminations. [29, 30] Especially, the ΔGH of O-terminated Nb2C was reported to be near-zero value of 0.02 eV based on the density functional calculations, which can be used as an excellent catalyst for HER.[30] Furthermore, the adsorption of H2O and the formation of H2 on the MXene are energetically favorable.[32] Consequently, for Nb2O5/C/Nb2C system, electrons were transferred from Nb2O5 to Nb2C and then further rapidly shuttled to the Nb2C surface due to its excellent electronic conductivity. And the protons in the aqueous solution were efficiently reduced to evolve H2 by the photo-induced electrons on Nb2C thanks to its excellent HER capability. This mechanism is in accordance with

the previous works on other MXene materials[26-28]. Apart from Nb2O5, Nb2C is also a good co-catalyst for TiO2 according to our previous study.[26]

To investigate the stability of the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C catalyst, photocatalytic H2 production was continuously performed on the NCN-1.0 sample for 32 h. As shown in Figure 10A, no decrease in the photocatalytic H2 production activity of the NCN-1.0 sample was observed during the repeated test cycles, and the XRD patterns of NCN-1.0 sample before and after reaction are similar (Figure 10B). Conclusions In summary, Nb2O5/C/Nb2C photocatalysts were successfully synthesized by one-step CO2 oxidation technique. In the Nb2O5/C/Nb2C system, Nb2O5 grew on the surface of the Nb2C that provides intimate interaction between Nb2O5 and Nb2C. The photogenerated electrons can transfer from the conduction band of Nb2O5 to Nb2C with Nb2C serving as an electron sink. A Schottky-junction is formed at the Nb2O5/Nb2C interface, which promotes the separation of photogenerated charge carriers. The variation of the oxidation time for Nb2O5/C/Nb2C influences its optical properties and photocatalytic performance. The Nb2O5/C/Nb2C-1.0h exhibited an enhancement of the hydrogen production by photocatalytic water splitting as compared to other catalysts, due to the intimate contact between Nb2O5 and Nb2C, a balanced light absorption and efficient charge separation. Our results demonstrate that the Nb2C can be used as an effective cocatalyst for photocatalyst in the hydrogen production reaction, further broadening the use of MXene as a co-catalyst for photocatalysis.

The oxidized Nb2CTx (Nb2O5/C/Nb2C) was prepared through a onestep CO2 oxidation technique (Scheme 2). The preparation procedures are described in the following: 0.1 g of Nb2CTx powder was loaded into a ceramic crucible and placed in a horizontal quartz tube furnace.
