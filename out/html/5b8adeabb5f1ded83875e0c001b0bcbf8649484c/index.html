Abstract Exploring efficient and low-cost electrocatalysts for Oxygen Reduction Reaction (ORR) and Oxygen Evolution Reaction (OER) is critical for developing renewable energy technologies like fuel cells, metal-air batteries, and water electrolyzers. A rational design of a catalyst can be guided by identifying descriptors that determine its activity. This article presents a descriptor study on ORR/OER of spinel oxides. With a series of MnCo2O4, the Mn in octahedral sites was identified as active site. This finding was then applied to successfully explain the ORR/OER activities of other transition metal spinels, including MnxCo3-xO4 (x = 2, 2.5, 3), LixMn2O4 (x = 0.7, 1), XCo2O4 (X = Co, Ni, Zn), and XFe2O4 (X = Mn, Co, Ni). We conclude a general principle that the e g occupancy of active cation in octahedral site is the activity descriptor for ORR/OER of spinels, consolidating the role of electron orbital filling in metal oxide catalysis.

Oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER) are critical elements in many renewable energy technologies such as fuel cells,[1] metal-air batteries,[2] and water electrolyzers.[3] The sluggish kinetics of these two oxygen electrocatalytic reactions results in the requirement of high overpotentials and therefore considerable energy loss.[3b] Great effort has been made in the past decades in exploring active catalysts for these two reactions. The most efficient catalysts for ORR and OER have been identified as Pt[1a,4] and IrO2 & RuO2,[5] respectively. Unfortunately, these state-of-art catalysts are precious metal-based and the scarcity of these elements hampered their large-scale application. With comparable activities to precious metal-based catalysts, earthabundant metal oxides have become increasingly attractive in these years, particularly like manganese-based oxides for ORR[6] and cobalt-based oxides for OER.[7] In pursuit of further enhanced oxygen electrocatalytic activity, it is necessary to understand the role of transition metal cations in oxides and to identify the activity descriptor. Inspired by the success of d-band center model in elucidating some catalysis reaction at metal surfaces (e.g., ORR,[4,8] hydrogen evolution reaction,[9] CO oxidation[10] and CO2 reduction),[11] recent efforts have focused on molecular orbital theory, which correlates the catalyst-reactant bond strength to the occupancy of antibonding orbitals. The established activity descriptors for perovskite family include the number of d electrons,[12] eg occupancy,[13] bulk formation energy,[12b] metal-oxygen covalency,[3b,13a] and position of O-p band center.[3a,7a] However, it is not apparent if these descriptors found in the perovskite structure can be applied universally to other oxide structural families such as the spinels, which consist of metal cations occupying in octahedral and tetrahedral sites. Recently, there has been increased interest in spinel oxides for ORR/OER. Remarkable performance has been demonstrated using spinel catalysts such as NiCo2O4,[14] MnFe2O4,[15] CoxMn3-xO4,[16] Co3O4/rmGO (reduced graphene

Some of them have used hybrids,[17-18] in which it is formidable to quantify the surface area of metal oxide and therefore estimating the true catalytic contribution from oxide is difficult. Thus, the lack the analysis of intrinsic specific activity (kinetic current density normalized to catalyst surface area) has hindered the development of a fundamental understanding of the physicochemical principles governing catalytic activities. As a result, the descriptor for spinel oxides’ ORR/OER remains unclear. In the spinel structure, the coexistence of tetrahedral and octahedral sites (Figure 1a) provides multiple sites for accommodating different transition metal cations and in a wide range of valence states to form a large number of oxides. This work focuses on transition metal spinels and reports key parameters that govern the ORR and OER activities. We start from tuning the electronic structure of MnCo2O4 cubic spinels by varying the synthesis temperature. Utilizing the X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS), the correlation between ORR/OER catalysis and their physicochemical properties (e.g., Mn/Co valence state, cation distribution) has been systematically studied. Mn valence state on the octahedral sites forms a volcano trend with the ORR/OER activity. The discovered governing role of e g filling of the active cation in octahedral sites is further explored for explaining the origins of ORR/OER catalytic activities on other transition metal spinel oxides, such as Mn xCo3-xO4 tetragonal spinels, LixMn2O4 (x = 0.7, 1), XCo2O4 (X = Co, Ni, Zn), and XFe2O4 (X = Mn, Co, Ni) cubic spinels. MnCo2O4 cubic spinels were synthesized by solid state chemistry method at different temperatures. Oxides used in this work were pure phase (Figure 1b) except for MnCo 2O4-500 oC, where < 3 w% MnOx/CoOx is present (Figure S1). The impurities in MnCo 2O4-500 oC are considered to be inevitable. Even calcination in form of pellets for a longer duration (with intermediate re-grinding and re-pressing) does not give a complete single phase (Figure S1). Transmission electron microscopy images (Figure S2) show as-synthesized MnCo2O4 particles are in polycrystalline, different sizes, and irregular shapes. This eliminates the concern that ORR/OER performance differs from different facets/orientation. [5a] In this work, the change in the Mn valence state has been correlated with ORR/OER activities. First, in the K-edge X-ray near edge spectroscopy (XANES, Figure 2a and 2b), Mn exhibits significant edge shifts with various synthesis temperatures; but the position of Co K-edge remains almost constant, which indicates that the valence state of Mn is decreasing with increasing temperature, but Co not. To know the exact valence state, edge energy is determined by integral method (Figure S3). [19] Results (Figure 2e) show that Mn valence state ranges from +3.2 to +3.7 in the synthesized MnCo 2O4 cubic spinels, while Co almost does not change (remains at ~+2.5). Second, EXAFS fitting of the characteristic octahedra peak (at ~2.5 Å) and tetrahedra peak (at ~3 Å) gives the cation distribution. [20] It can be seen that in all MnCo2O4 cubic spinels, either Mn or Co occupation is kept generally the same (Figure 2f, ~80% Mn cations occupy octahedron and ~10% Mn cations locate at tetrahedron). Further detailed EXAFS analysis (Table S3) shows that in each MnCo 2O4 oxide, the coordination number of Mn-O bond (either at tetrahedral or octahedral site) is almost equal to that of Co-O bond. Since the coordination number reflects the ratio of surface atoms to bulk, [22] our result indicates the surface Mn/Co ratio follows the bulky ratio (see explanation in Table S3) [22] and this rules out the possibility that the different activity originates from the formation of a Mn-rich or Co-rich surface. In addition, it should be clarified that δ, the indication of the oxygen non-stoichiometry, is the variable resulted from changing cations’ valence state. Therefore, directly looking at the cations’ valency is preferred here. As revealed by XAS, synthesis temperature of MnCo2O4 cubic spinels changes the valence state of Mn, but without changing Co and the overall cation occupancy of tetrahedral/octahedral sites. Below we correlate ORR/OER activities of these MnCo2O4 cubic spinels with the changing Mn-valence state on the octahedral site. ORR and OER geometric current densities of MnCo2O4-900 oC, as an example, are shown in Figure 3a and 3b. The ORR limiting current density reaches ~5.5 mA cm-2disk, which is within 10% of the theoretical value for 4e- pathway.[23] This rationalizes the application of Koutecky-Levich analysis on electron transfer number.[23] The Koutecky-Levich plot is parallel with Pt/C and the slope is 0.151 mA cm-2disk rpm-0.5 (Figure 3a, inset), indicating a 4e- ORR reaction.[6b] Since catalysis only involves the atoms on the surface,[24] geometric activity does not reflect the intrinsic catalytic activity. To obtain the intrinsic activity, we normalize the ORR/OER kinetic current density to the surface area of the catalyst, which is determined by Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) measurement (Figure 1c and S6), generating the so-called specific activity (is). It is reasonable that BET surface area is not equivalent to the real electrochemically active surface area. However, assessing ORR/OER activity by BET surface area has been well established as a benchmark methodology to screen metal oxide catalysts. [25] The drop-casted electrode is able to reflect the difference of surface area characterized by BET technique [25] This is further proved by our result that the capacitive current density recorded at the potential window of ORR/OER measurement (Figure S7) compares well with BET result, confirming the applicability of BET surface area to calculate the specific activity. The surface-area-normalized specific activity of all MnCo2O4 cubic spinels are plotted as a function of iR-corrected potential (Figure 3c and 3d). To demonstrate the reliability of our electrochemical measurement, state-of-art catalysts were also tested. Commercial Pt/C (40 wt%, Sigma-Aldrich) gives an ORR limiting current density of ~5.7 mA cm-2disk (Figure S8a) and the Tafel plots of mass activity (160 mA mg-1Pt at 0.9 V vs. RHE) and specific activity (380 μA cm-2Pt at 0.9 V vs. RHE) are consistent with previous works. [25b,26] Moreover, OER specific current density (Figure S8b) of IrO2 polycrystalline nanoparticles also agrees well with landmark reports.[27] The ORR Tafel plots of different MnCo2O4 spinels (Figure 3c) locate across a potential window of 200 mV, indicating the activity difference is ~3 orders of magnitude; and the OER activity (Figure 3d) spans over ~2 orders of magnitude. Generally speaking (Figure 3c and 3d), higher synthesis temperature gives better ORR/OER activity. Of notable interest is that the intrinsic ORR/OER activity of MnCo2O4-900 oC is approaching to state-of-art Pt/C[25b] and IrO2 catalysts,[27] respectively.

To understand the different ORR/OER activities on MnCo2O4, it is proposed that Mn cations on the octahedral site are key for catalyzing ORR/OER of spinels and that the ORR/OER activity is correlated with the Mn valence on the site. To support this hypothesis, we firstly compared the ORR/OER performance of Co3O4 and ZnCo2O4 (XRD, BET results in Figure S9 and S10). Zn2+ cation has a strong preference for the tetrahedral site[20a,28] and is considered as inactive because of its d10 electronic structure. Thus a comparison between Co3O4 and ZnCo2O4 gives an opportunity to reveal the role of tetrahedrally coordinated Co2+. As shown in Figure S11, both the ORR and OER Tafel plots of these two catalysts almost overlap, suggesting the tetrahedrally coordinated Co2+ is not significantly more active than the octahedrally coordinated Co3+. Our finding is consistent with several previous studies on spinels: electrochemical deposited Co3O4 and ZnCo2O4 electrodes show same OER activity;[28] the replacement of Li+ by Co2+ at tetrahedral site does not improve the OER performance at Li2Co2O4;[7c] on the other hand, Co3O4 outperforms (Co2+)tet(Fe23+)octO4 in ORR.[29] Based on the above set of results, we suggest that the octahedral site plays the leading role in spinel catalysts. Secondly, metal cation with tetrahedral coordination has different d-orbital splitting compared with octahedral coordination.[30] In octahedral coordination (Figure S12), the high-lying eg orbital directly points to O, generating a strong spatial overlap with O-2p orbital. The strong overlap rationalizes the role of eg as the activity descriptor in perovskite catalysts. While in tetrahedral coordination (Figure S12), neither the high-lying e nor the low-lying t2 orbital directs its shape towards O. The angle between the e-orbital and the ligand is 54o44’; for t2, is 35o16’.[30] The mismatch causes weaker spatial overlap with O.[30] This results in a generally weak interaction between tetrahedrally-coordinated metal and O, as evidenced by Co2+ in spinel tetrahedral site.[31] Here we discuss the ORR/OER intrinsic activity as a function of the Mn valence state on the octahedral sites as most Mn cations occupy on the octahedral sites and the Mn valence state influences

the antibonding orbital occupancy of Mn-O bond, which has been hypothesized to influence the ORR and OER activities of perovskite.[13] The specific current density was normalized to the percentage of Mn cations at octahedral sites. The intrinsic activity is defined as the potential at a given specific current density, i.e., 25 μA cm-2Oxi for ORR and OER. Interestingly, MnCo2O4 spinels exhibit a similar ORR volcano trend with Mn valency to that extracted from previously reported Mn-based perovskites.[23b] The summit of the spinels studied here (Figure 4a) locates at Mn3.4+ for ORR, which is the same as that for Mn in the perovskites.[23b] The shape of the volcano plot can be attributed to the hypothesis that when the Mn valency is high, it gives an oxygen adsorption which is too strong; while low Mn valency is too weak for optimal Mn-oxygen interaction.[3b] On the other hand, the OER activity of MnCo2O4 versus Mn valency shows a linear relation (Figure 4b), where the lower Mn valence state gives better OER performance. Although MnCo 2O4-500 oC contains < 3 w% impurity, it possesses the same activity as predicted by Mn valency. Similar observation can also be found in the landmark descriptor studies, where some catalysts are not 100 % pure in phase but the establishment of activity descriptor is not affected.[13,25a] The low Mn occupation on the octahedral sites explains why the ORR and OER activities of MnCo2O4-150 oC are lower before normalization to the fraction of Mn on the octahedral sites (below the gray dash line, Figure S13) even though the valence state of Mn in it is ~+3.2: only ~50% Mn occupies the octahedral site and thus less Mn cations are able to contribute. After occupation-correction, MnCo2O4-150 oC fits well with the volcano plots (Figure 4). It further suggests that synthesis methods may affect the occupation of cations, which may be the reason for different catalytic activities observed on same formula spinels but synthesized through different methods. It should be noticed that earlier reports[32] consider that each site is only occupied by Mn cation of single valency (such as MnFe2O4+δ, where the tetrahedral site is solely occupied by Mn2+ and octahedral site is only by Mn3+).[32] Recent studies,[16a,21] however, have revealed that the multi-valency of Mn can present in both tetrahedral and octahedral sites. The above findings indicate that Mn governs the ORR/OER activity of MnCo2O4 spinels, while Co plays a minor role. This seems to be conflicting with the literature observation that the top oxygen electrocatalysts are usually Co-containing oxides.[7a,13b,25a] However, it needs to be noticed that when Co is put in the environment of spinel structure, the activity is usually low,[7c,31,33] and thus the catalysis relies more on other cations. For example, in the study on the coordination effect of Co, Co3O4 possesses the lowest activity among 9 Co-based metal oxides.[31]

We further examine if the correlation of Mn valency on the octahedral sites to the activity of ORR/OER can be applied to other transition metal spinels. To correlate activities across different transition metal cations, we therefore postulate that the eg occupancy of active transition metal cations at octahedral site is the activity descriptor for ORR/OER at spinels following earlier investigations of this issue.[7c,28-29] The cation distribution was quantified by EXAFS (Figure S14), and the eg/t2g electron assignment was obtained by knowing the valence state from XANES (Figure S15) and spin state from literature data (Table S6). The prerequisite of our finding is to resolve the occupation argument, identifying octahedral coordination as the active site with the aid of EXAFS fitting and electrochemical measurement. The role of eg electron at the octahedral site can be explained by the four-step proton/electroncoupled reaction mechanism,[35] where the binding strength of ORR/OER reaction intermediates is governed by eg filling.[13] At the left branch of ORR volcano profile (eg < 1), Moct-O2- bond is so strong that the limiting step is OH- regeneration (step 4, Figure S16). At the right branch (eg > 1), the ORR kinetics is hindered by the formation of stable Moct -OO2- and the limiting step is OO2-/OHdisplacement (step 1). As for OER, too less eg electron (eg < 1) limits the rate of proton removal of Moct-OOH- (step 3, Figure S16) to form Moct -OO2-, and too much eg electron filling (eg > 1) retards the O-O bond formation in Moct-OOH- (step 2). The ORR/OER activity can be optimized at a moderate eg filling (eg ≈ 1) at the octahedral site, which balances the competition between rate-limiting steps.

Transition metal cations on the tetrahedral site give different electron assignments (Figure S12 and Table S6). The spatial overlap of d-orbitals with oxygen ligands is poor, and the e antibonding orbitals are over-filled (more than 2), indicating weak interactions with molecular oxygen and oxygenated species. To prove this point, the ORR/OER activities were plotted as a function of antibonding filling (e-occupancy) at the tetrahedral site element. The resulting plot (Figure S17) does not exhibit an apparent relationship, which suggests that the antibonding occupancy at tetrahedral cannot account for the large activity difference (~3 orders of magnitude). The different crystal field on the octahedral site induced by the Jahn-Teller effect[36] (the elongation of c lattice parameter and consequently the increasing Mn-O bonding length along z-axis: see Figure S18) does not appear to influence the ORR/OER activity. Our results demonstrate tetragonal (Mnx-2Co3-x)tet(Mn2)octO4+δ spinel (red circle, Figure 5) does not deviate from the observed trend in comparison to cubic (Co)tet(MnCo)octO4+δ. This observation is consistent with the ORR/OER examination on perovskites of 3 different space groups: the orthorhombic (space group, Pnma) crystals can have 3 metal-oxygen bonding lengths at the octahedral site (Table S7) but they all fit the eg theory.[13] We notice that the spinels with Co or Ni as the active site (Co3O4, ZnCo2O4, and NiCo2O4) positively deviate from the expected trend. The origin is attributed to the covalency of metal-oxygen bond at the octahedral site. With the same eg filling, stronger covalency gives better activity.[3b] The concept derived from our work can be compared with LaMnO3, LaCoO3 and LaNiO3 which have the same eg occupancy (eg = 1).[3b,13a] Due to the covalency, the activity overpotential shows a ~120 mV deviation from the indicated trend,[3b,13a] but does not change the overall volcano picture. This observation links to the electronegativity of metal cation and further supports the importance of eg molecular orbital on the octahedral site of spinel oxides.

In summary, we found that the ORR/OER activity of various MnCo2O4 exhibits a volcano shape as a function of the Mn valence state in octahedral sites. The summit of the ORR/OER volcano trend locates at the Mn valency of ~+3. The as-revealed governing role of octahedral-coordinated Mn valency was further extended to quantitatively describing the ORR/OER activities of other transition metal spinels (17 total). We conclude that the e g occupancy of the active cation on the octahedral sites is the activity descriptor for spinels. This work demonstrates that eg theory is not only applicable to perovskite family, but also spinel family. This may possibly inspire more insight studies into the catalysis in nature: oxygen evolving complex (OEC) in Photosystem II (PSII),[37] where the Mn3+ is under a quasi-spherical octahedral coordination and gives the best OER performance on this planet.[3b]
