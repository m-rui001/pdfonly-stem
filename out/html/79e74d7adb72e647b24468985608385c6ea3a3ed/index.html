THE BASIC ARCHITECTURE OF THE VASCULAR TREE The vascular system is a closed network of arteries, veins and interconnecting capillaries, organized into tree-like structures. Endothelial cells (ECs) line the lumen of blood vessels and regulate the dynamic passage of nutrients and cells. ECs exhibit organ-specific molecular and functional properties that provide heterogeneity to the vascular tree. As such, the endothelium of arteries and veins forms a continuous monolayer, whereas the endothelium

t

of capillaries can be continuous, fenestrated or discontinuous depending on their (tissue-

In the adult organism, ECs rarely proliferate and remain in a dormant, quiescent state for protracted periods of time, yet they retain the ability to rapidly initiate the formation of new vessels from pre-existing ones, a tightly controlled process termed angiogenesis. This process of new blood vessel formation is for example initiated in nutrient deprived, ischemic/hypoxic environment, where restoration of blood flow (in order to provide oxygen and nutrients) is required to support the growth and function of a tissue. Remodeling of the vascular network occurs throughout life as capillaries grow and regress according to functional demands 2.

CURRENT LIMITATIONS AND NEEDS OF ANTI-ANGIOGENIC THERAPIES

The initial postulate that growth of solid tumors depends on angiogenesis had set high expectations for the therapeutic potential of AATs, which, due to the importance of the VEGF pathway in developmental angiogenesis and the abundance of VEGF in tumors, were almost exclusively directed towards inhibiting the VEGF pathway 64, 65. The initial enthusiasm was tempered by observations that such AATs are not uniformly active across all tumor types. Indeed, overall survival (OS) did not increase in prostate cancer, breast cancer, pancreatic
