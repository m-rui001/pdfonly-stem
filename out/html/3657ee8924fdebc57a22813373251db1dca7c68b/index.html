Abstract: Vacancy-rich layered materials with good electrontransfer property are of great interest. Herein, a full-spectrum responsive vacancy-rich monolayer BiO2@x has been synthesized. The increased density of states at the conduction band (CB) minimum in the monolayer BiO2@x is responsible for the enhanced photon response and photo-absorption, which were confirmed by UV/Vis-NIR diffuse reflectance spectra (DRS) and photocurrent measurements. Compared to bulk BiO2@x, monolayer BiO2@x has exhibited enhanced photocatalytic performance for rhodamine B and phenol removal under UV, visible, and near-infrared light (NIR) irradiation, which can be attributed to the vacancy VBi-O’’’ as confirmed by the positron annihilation spectra. The presence of VBi-O’’’ defects in monolayer BiO2@x promoted the separation of electrons and holes. This finding provides an atomic level understanding for developing highly efficient UV, visible, and NIR light responsive photocatalysts.

Developing of highly efficient photocatalysts for environ-

mental purification and energy conversion has attracted increasing attention.[1–4] To date, diverse photocatalysts have been reported that absorb UV or visible light to catalyze different photochemical reactions.[5, 6] However, the NIR light accounting for more than 50 % of the energy in solar irradiation, has rarely been utilized by these photocatalysts, owing to their relatively larger band gaps.[7] A series of upconversion composites as NIR-driven photocatalysts were reported, such as phosphor coupled C-TiO2 and CaF2 :Yb@BiVO4 composites.[8, 9] However, these photocatalysts can only indirectly utilize NIR light, moreover, their excitation wavelength is limited around 980 nm.[10] To overcome this limitation and improve the utilization efficiency of solar energy, a few NIR light directly responsive photo-

catalysts have been developed,[11, 12] however, high-efficient NIR light responsive photocatalysts still need to be exploited for degrading organic pollutants. Substantially, other strategies, such as making quantum dots,[13] making complicate heterojunctions,[14] fabricating defective materials,[15] and synthesizing ultrathin materials[16] have also been developed and demonstrated to potentially improve the photocatalytic activity of catalysts. Especially, when defective-rich ultrathin layered materials, such as Bi2WO6[17] and NiCo2O4[18] have been achieved. These ultrathin layered materials not only contain a large fraction of uncoordinated surface atoms and expose more active sites compared to their bulk materials,[19, 20] but also, the diffusion lengths of photogenerated carriers in these materials are shorter, the benefit of which could be to restrain or even eliminate bulk electron–hole pair recombination.[21] As has been demonstrated in BiOCl, when the thickness of BiOCl nanosheets was reduced to single-unitcell scale, the predominant defects changed from isolated defects VBi’’’ to triple vacancy associations VBi’’’VOCCVBi’’’, ultrathin BiOCl nanosheets showed significantly red-shift of optical absorption, and further ensured efficient photocatalytic activity in the UV and visible light regions than BiOCl nanoplates.[22] Inspired by defecting engineering and ultrathin layered material engineering strategies, herein we synthesized the full-spectrum responsive vacancy-rich monolayer BiO2@x. Comprehensive study of this monolayered material with different diffraction, spectroscopy, microscopy and modelling techniques indicated that the presence of VBi-O’’’ in monolayer BiO2@x decreased photoexcitation energy, which was lower than that for valance band (VB) to conductive band (CB) transition, making its photo-absorption extend from 850 nm to 890 nm and resulting in superior photocatalytic degradation of rhodamine B (RhB) and phenol in aqueous solution under UV, visible, and NIR light irradiation. This study will provide new insights into developing full-spectrum responsive highly active photocatalyst for organic pollutant degradation. Monolayer BiO2@x material has been synthesized via liquid exfoliation method from BiO2@x bulk materials (Figure S1 in the Supporting Information). BiO2@x bulk materials with thickness about 5–10 nm (Figure S2,S3) have been hydrothermally synthesized and then be swelled and exfoliated into nearly transparent ultrathin nanosheets. X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) was employed to identify their crystallographic phase and purity, the as-synthesized samples can be indexed to the cubic phase BiO2@x with space group of Fm3̄m (JCPDS:47-1057) and its lattice parameters was calculated to be a = 5.476 c against powder XRD pattern (Figure 1 a). Referring to the crystal structure of monolayered BiO2@x, it possesses abundant OVs and partial Bi atoms are exposed outside, suggesting that the Bi atoms may escape from the lattice much more easily than the inner Bi atoms. As the thickness of BiO2@x nanosheets is down to monolayer scale, the Bi atoms become easier to escape from the lattice, which can simultaneously carry O atoms to break away the lattice, resulting in the predominant defects of dual vacancy associates for monolayer BiO2@x, which has been further confirmed by the result of relative intensity of positron lifetime. It is worth noting that each vacancy associate employed two electron and hole pairs, which can act as the trapping center of carriers.[26] The OVs concentrations of BiO2@x nanoplates and monolayer BiO2@x are estimated to be 8.5 % and 36 %, respectively based on TG measurements (Supporting Information, Section S5). While XPS measurement (Supporting Information, Figure S6). shows that their OVs concentrations are 11.5 % and 20.5 % in nanoplates and monolayers, respectively. Although the oxygen vacancies concentrations calculated based on these two techniques are deviated from each other due to their different sensitivity in bulk and surface composition.[28, 29] The obtained data reveal the same tendency that monolayered BiO2@x possesses more oxygen vacancies than bulk BiO2@x. The above results have supported our point that ultrathin structure and increased OVs induced stronger light absorption in monolayer BiO2@x. To better understand the ultrathin structure and charge behavior, DFT calculations were carried out to study the electronic structure of BiO2@x nanoplates and monolayer BiO2@x. Figure 3 b reveals that the calculated density of states (DOSs) of the monolayer BiO2@x and BiO2@x nanoplates with the presence of OVs state. It can be seen both monolayer BiO2@x and BiO2@x nanoplates show a half-metallic characteristic, mostly originating from the O 2p and Bi 4p states. The monolayer BiO2@x exhibits an obviously increased density of state (DOS) at the CB edge as compared to its bulk counterpart. In that case, it can be anticipated that the monolayer BiO2@x will display improved photo-absorption and photo-responsibility,[30] which has been further confirmed by the photocurrent measurement (Supporting Information, Figure S7). Moreover, the exact location of valence band has been determined with XPS as shown in Figure 3 c, BiO2@x nanoplates display a VB with the edge of the maximum energy at about 0.95 eV and its CB minimum energy is about @0.51 eV according to its UV-vis-NIR DRS spectrum. While for monolayer BiO2@x, its VB maximum energy up-shifts by 0.1 to 0.85 eV as compared to that of BiO2@x nanoplates. Moreover, the CB minimum energy of

monolayer BiO2@x upper shifts by 0.04 eV to @0.55 eV as compared to that of BiO2@x nanoplates (Figure 3 d). The increased VB width can enhance the separation of the photogenerated electron–hole pairs, as the wider VB width can inherently enhance the transferability of the photogenerated holes, which contribute to stronger photo-oxidation of holes.[22, 28] After fully understanding the structure of the obtained materials, we continue to examine the full spectrum responsive photocatalytic activity of monolayer BiO2@x and BiO2@x nanoplates by the standard photocatalytic degradation experiments for RhB in aqueous solution under UV light (l = 365 nm), visible light (l = 420 nm) and NIR (770 nm < l < 860 nm) irradiation. Monolayer BiO2@x exhibits better photocatalytic activity than BiO2@x nanoplates and shows a degradation efficiency of 100 %, 100 % and 95.0 % for RhB in aqueous solution under UV, visible and NIR light irradiation, respectively (Figure 4 a, Figure S8), the degradation process by the photocatalysts is attributed to the photocatalysis, instead of the thermal effect (Figure S9). Also, for excluding the potential dye sensitization effects, the photocatalytic degradation of colorless phenol solution is also carried out under NIR light irradiation. The degradation rate of phenol solution by monolayer BiO2@x and BiO2@x nanoplates are 52.0 % and 47 % under NIR light irradiation, respectively, indicating the photocatalytic degradation of phenol in aqueous solution is mainly caused by direct semiconductor photocatalysis (Figure 4 b). The cyclic experiments for photocatalytic degradation of RhB in aqueous solution demonstrate monolayer BiO2@x is of good stability. The photocatalytic degradation ratio remains 84 % for RhB in aqueous solution after 5 cycles and the decreased degradation ratio can be attributed to the loss in recovered catalyst process, meanwhile the morphology of the catalysts remain similarly with the crystal edges less sharp (Figure S10).[31] To investigate possible photocatalytic mechanism, ESR characterizations and trapping experiments were carried out

under UV, visible and NIR irradiation, respectively (Supporting Information, Figure S11). The characteristic signals of the DMPO-CO2@ can be only detected under UV-visible light irradiation compared to the NIR and dark conditions. Besides, the signals of the DMPO-COH cannot be observed under UV-visible and NIR irradiation. Hence, it can be concluded that h+ is the main active species in the photocatalytic process under the NIR irradiation, while, the main active species under UV and visible lights irradiation can be attributed to CO2@ and h+, which can be further confirmed by chemical trapping experiments. The above results show when monolayer BiO2@x is exposed on the irradiation of UV or visible light with high photonic energy, the photogenerated electrons are mainly excited to the CB, resulting the formation of CO2@ ; while as the NIR light with relative low photonic energy works as light source, the excited electrons may easily be trapped in VBi-O’’’ defect states and the electrons with low photonic energy canQt escape from the defect states, resulting in the separation of photogenerated electrons, which has demonstrated by photoluminescence (PL) measurements (Figure 4 c). Therefore, the photogenerated carriers in vacancies-rich monolayer BiO2@x structure can efficiently separation in photocatalytic process.[32, 33] Based on the above discussion, a possible photocatalytic schematic diagram of BiO2@x is illustrated in Figure 4 d. As the thickness of BiO2@x nanoplates decreased to monolayer thickness, new VBi-O’’’ defect states were formed, the dominant electrons transfer process could be from the VB to CB under UV or visible light irradiation. Whereas, the photogenerated electrons under NIR light irradiation with low photonic energy may be easily captured by VBi-O’’’ defect states, resulting in the separation of carriers. Electrons in the CB of monolayer BiO2@x can react with O2 to produce CO2@ caused by the more negative ECB (@0.55 eV vs. normal hydrogen electrode (NHE)) than the redox potential of O2/ CO2@ (@0.33 eV vs. NHE).[34] Finally, the photo-generated h+ will attack and oxidize RhB and phenol molecules in aqueous solution. In conclusion, we synthesized a full-spectrum responsive monolayer BiO2@x and demonstrated the abundant defects in the monolayer BiO2@x could affect its photocatalytic activity. These defect states in monolayer BiO2@x narrow the band gap with both up-shifted VB maximum and CB minimum, promoting the efficient separation of the photogenerated electron–hole pairs as demonstrated by PL spectra and photocurrent measurement. Benefiting from the presence of rich VBi-O’’’ defects, the monolayer BiO2@x exhibits superior photocatalytic activity in the degradation of RhB and phenol in aqueous solution under UV, visible, and NIR irradiation. Our study paves the way for developing the next-generation monolayer bismuth oxide photocatalysts for capturing a broad range of the solar spectrum for environmental decontamination.
