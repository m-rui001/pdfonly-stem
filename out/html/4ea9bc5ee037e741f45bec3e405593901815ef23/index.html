Electrocatalysts for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) under acidic conditions are important components of proton-exchange membrane (PEM) water electrolyzers. However, widely used Ir-based catalysts suffer from unsatisfactory activities, while highly active high-valence-Ru catalysts dissolve under acidic OER conditions, yielding low operating stability (<10 hours). It has been reported that incorporating stable metal modulators inhibits the dissolution of Ru by preventing over-oxidation; however, in these studies, the activity was lowered correspondingly. It remains an unmet challenge to achieve simultaneously high activity and stability in acidic OER catalysts. In this work, we explore a conceptual approach to exploring the composition space Sr-Ru-Ir: Sr promotes the formation of high-valence Ru sites and achieves high activity; Ir anchors the active sites and realizes high stability. Using a sol-gel method, we synthesize Sr-Ru-Ir ternary compositions that – from DFT studies of calculated overpotentials – show particular promise. Experimentally, the new Sr-Ru-Ir catalysts provide 1500 h of continuous operation at 10 mA cm-2 and demonstrate a low 223 mV overpotential at this current density following 1500 operating hours. When used in a PEM electrolyzer, the catalyst reduced commensurately the full cell voltage, achieving 1.50 V at 1 A cm-2. Electrochemical studies, in-situ X-ray absorption spectroscopy, and further DFT studies show that Sr facilitates the formation of high-valence Ru sites via charge redistribution, while Ir anchors high-valence Ru sites and prevents their dissolution.

The overpotential in the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) is one key contributor to the overall performance of several energy storage and conversion devices such as water splitting1-3 and CO2 reduction4,5 electrolyzers. It serves also as the counter-reaction for metal-winning6, cathodic protection, and electro-organic syntheses7. Large-scale hydrogen generation from water splitting has been dominated by alkaline electrolysis technology for many years8,9. This technology suffers from low current densities due to large ohmic losses, unstable electrolytes reacting with CO2 in the atmosphere, and the formation of explosive gas mixtures of H2 and O2. Proton exchange membranes (PEM), which lever a gas-tight (< 200 μm thickness) solid–phase polymer membrane with proton charge carriers instead of hydroxyl ions10-13, enable acidic OER water electrolyzers. However, corrosive acidic conditions and high anodic current densities (corresponding to high overpotentials) in PEM electrolyzers motivate the development of OER catalysts with lower overpotentials and longer operating stabilities.

iridium (Ir) oxides14-16. Ir oxides (IrO2) exhibit high stability but low activity, while Ru oxides (RuO2) possess high catalytic activity but suffer from poor stability due to the formation of soluble high-valence Run+ species (n>4) under OER17,18. Several strategies have been reported to improve the stability and activity of Ru-based catalysts for acidic OER. Sr2+ and Zn2+ were utilized as low-valence dopants to increase the valency of Ru (> 4+ oxidation state), yielding improved activity19. However, the high-valence Ru sites are soluble under acidic conditions, leading to an activity vs. stability compromise. Another metal stabilizer (e.g. Ir, Sn, Pt and Pd)20-23 can be used to maintain the oxidation state of Ru species at 4+ oxidation state and therefore avoid dissolution: this has improved stability, but at the expense of losing activity since Ru4+ is less active. For example, RuO2-IrO2 mixed oxides exhibited higher stability than does pure RuO2, but their activity was diminished due to the loss of highly active Ru sites1,21,24,25 We pursued the following strategy to stabilize the highly active Ru sites in the rutile structure. We would use synergetic modulators, Sr and Ir. Using high-throughput screening methods via DFT, we optimized the content ratio of the two modulators (Sr, Ir) to obtain high-valence Ru sites in a Pourbaix-stable ternary phase. Then, by using a modified sol-gel method, we synthesized ten Sr-Ru-Ir ternary rutile oxides from 2,201 possible candidates suggested by DFT. The new catalysts achieve low overpotential of 223 mV at 10 mA cm-2 following 1500 operating hours – >140 mV lower than the overpotential of the best previously-reported acidic OER catalysts reported (RuMnOx: 370 mV following 10 hours of operation26). We demonstrate 1500 hours of operating stability, a 150x improvement relative to RuMnOx’s demonstrated 10-hour operating stability. When incorporated into a PEM electrolyzer, Sr-Ru-Ir oxides reduced the overall cell voltage to 1.50 V at 1 A cm-2, the lowest reported in a PEM electrolyzer. Results DFT screening of optimal Sr-Ru-Ir ternary electrocatalysts for OER. We screened for optimal ternary Sr-Ru-Ir oxide electrocatalysts via high-throughput DFT calculations. Ten different Sr-Ru-Ir ratios were considered and 2,201 structures across ternary oxide crystals were explored computationally (Fig. 1a). The (110) surface of the most stable ternary oxide crystals is verified (Fig. S1 in Supplementary information (SI)) to further evaluate the surface OER activity as in previous reports27,28. We calculated the OER overpotential from the adsorption free energies of OER intermediates (OH*, O* and OOH*) on the catalyst surface. The OER active sites are on Ru, and not Ir nor Sr. The calculated OER overpotentials with different Sr-Ru-Ir ratios (Fig. 1b and Table S1 in SI) are presented in a ternary composition diagram with the molar ratios among the constituent elements on the vertices. The lowest calculated overpotential was ~210 mV located in the red region in Fig. 1b. In this region, the ratio 3

According to the theoretical OER pathways (Fig. 1c), Sr and Ir doping optimizes the binding energy of OER intermediates on Ru sites (such as *O and *OOH), and this facilitates the rate-determining process (O-O coupling) in OER. Electrochemical performances. We synthesized therefore Sr-Ru-Ir ternary catalysts with 10 different compositions. To fulfill the interaction among elements at an atomic level3, we used a sol-gel method and then annealed to achieve the desired structure (see Methods). The catalysts were then tested in 0.5 M H2SO4 (pH = 0.3) to evaluate their OER activities. The distribution of the experimental overpotential at 10 mA cm-2geo (geometric current density) in Fig. 2a and Fig. S2a agrees well with theoretical distribution diagram in Fig. 1b: the red regions substantially overlap. The best performing ternary composition was found to have a 3:5:1 Sr:Ru:Ir ratio (feed ratio, denoted SrRuIrOx in the following discussion). The catalyst achieves a low overpotential of 180±5 mV at 10 mA cm-2geo, which is 72 mV and 132 mV lower than commercial RuO2 and IrO2 benchmarks, respectively (Fig. S2b and S3). Interfacial charge transfer of SrRuIrOx was also accelerated (Fig. S4 and S5). To compare the intrinsic catalytic activities of these catalysts, we calculated their mass activities according to the total loading mass of Ru (or Ir) (Fig. 2b) and the turnover frequency (TOF) on each Ru site (Fig. 2c, Supplementary note 1 in SI). The SrRuIrOx reached the highest mass activity of 632 A gRu-1 per Ru mass at η = 300 mV, much higher than that of SrRuOx (245 A gRu-1), RuIrOx (263 A gRu-1), RuO2 (60 A gRu-1) and IrO2 (8 A gIr-1). The TOF of SrRuIrOx (0.85 s-1 at η = 300 mV) is nearly 6 and 10 times higher than the RuO2 and IrO2, respectively (Fig.2c and Table S2). We then investigated the stability of the catalyst under acidic OER. We did this using chronopotentiometry at a constant current density of 10 mA cm-2geo. SrRuIrOx over the course of 1500 hours (Fig. 2d) demonstrated an increase in overpotential at a rate of 0.01 mV h-1: this is over 20 times slower than for IrO2 (0.21 mV h-1). In comparison, the OER activity of RuO2 decreased dramatically: the overpotential increase rate reached 5.8 mV h-1 during the first 8 h and completely lost activity after ~14 h, as in previous reports20,29. Interestingly, SrRuOx catalysts demonstrated high TOF at the initial reaction stage (Table S2), but showed a similar poor stability like RuO2. RuIrOx catalysts were more tolerant to acidic electrolytes but exhibited limited activity (TOF=0.33 s-1). To evaluate activity and stability together, we measured the overpotential after longterm operation (>1000 hours) and compared it to the best prior reports. As shown in Table S3, the overpotential of SrRuIrOx increased from the initial 180 mV to 216 mV after 1000-hour operation at 10 mA cm-2, and to 223 mV after 1500-hour operation. 4

The overpotential of our catalyst after 1500 hours of continuous operation (223 mV) is 147 mV lower than the best reported catalyst (RuMn oxides, the overpotential increased from the initial 158 mV to 370 mV after only 10 hours of operation26) as shown in Table S3. SrRuIrOx catalysts demonstrate simultaneously high activity and stability for acidic OER. We then used SrRuIrOx catalysts in a 5 cm2 PEM electrolyzer at 80oC to evaluate the performance of OER catalysts in operating systems (Fig. S6). The steady-state polarization curves in Fig. 2e indicate that the OER activity of SrRuIrOx reduced the overall cell voltage to 1.50 V at 1 A cm-2, which is superior to Ir0.7Ru0.3Ox catalyst synthesized by Adams fusion methods, the reported highly active in PEM electrolyzers13 (Table S4). Upon applying a constant current of 1 A cm-2, no significant increase in cell voltage was observed over 150 hours of electrolysis (Fig. 2f). Morphology and local structure of SrRuIrOx. We investigated the catalyst (positive and control) structure after OER tests. High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) (Fig. 3a) images show that the SrRuIrOx catalyst particles have an average particle diameter of ~5 nm, and that Sr, Ru and Ir are homogeneously dispersed within a given particle (elemental mapping in Fig. S7). Fast Fourier transform (FFT) of a cluster of catalyst particles (Fig. 3b) shows a rutile structure (P42/mnm) with low crystallinity, in agreement with synchrotron X-ray diffraction patterns (Fig. S8). High-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscope images reveal that the d-spacing of the (110) plane in SrRuIrOx is increased by ~0.1 Å compared to the case of pristine RuO2 (Fig. S7), consistent with the insertion of dopants into the rutile lattice of Ru oxides: DFT calculations also show a ~7% increase in the d-spacing of (110) plane after Sr insertion (Table S5). We further investigated the local chemical environment in SrRuIrOx using extended Xray absorption fine structure (EXAFS). Using a wavelet transform of EXAFS (WTEXAFS), we analyzed elemental distribution and atomic distance in SrRuIrOx30. The R≈3.2 Å, k≈13 Å-1 peak (R and k represents the radial distance and wave vector, respectively) in Ru K-edge WT-EXAFS (Fig. 3c) and the R≈3 Å, k≈8.5 Å-1 peak in Ir L3-edge WT-EXAFS (Fig. 3d, Fig. S9 and Supplementary note 2) indicate that Ir is a substitutional dopant along the c-axis of the RuO2 rutile structure. Fourier transformed EXAFS (FT-EXAFS) spectra of Ru K-edge and Ir L3-edge reveal that the Ru-O bonds and Ir-O bonds were shortened in SrRuIrOx compared to pristine RuO2 and IrO2 (Fig. 3e and 3f). Structural parameters of FT-EXAFS spectra (Supplementary note 2) coincide well with the model of the most active structure obtained by DFT calculations (Fig. 3g, Table S6 and appended CIF model). incorporated into the lattice of rutile RuO2 at atomic scale, and that they induce structural distortions. Electronic structure studies of SrRuIrOx. We then combined in-situ XAS and DFT studies to investigate further the origins of high catalytic activity and stability in SrRuIrOx. In-situ Ru L3-edge XAS spectra in Fig. 4a show that, with increased applied potential, the peak at ca. 2840 eV shifts to higher energy, suggesting an increase in the oxidization state of Ru in SrRuIrOx31. Such a valence increase was also observed in the SrRuOx sample (Fig. S11), while the valence of Ru in pristine RuO2 and RuIrOx controls remained +4 (Fig. S12 and S13). These results demonstrate the modulation of Sr on the promotion of high-valence Ru. Bader charge analysis from DFT models similarly shows that Ru is more positively charged in SrRuIrOx than in pristine RuO2, indicating charge redistribution among Sr-Ru-Ir (Table S7). The valence orbitals (4d) of Ru were investigated by analyzing in-situ Ru L3-edge XAS results, since these correlate with OER process through binding with intermediates32. SrRuIrOx samples demonstrated a significantly higher white-line peak intensity than pristine RuO2 without peak splitting at ca. 2840 eV (Fig. S14), showing that more unoccupied states were generated in the eg states of Ru after Sr incorporation33,34. From the partial density of states (PDOS) analysis of surface Ru sites from DFT (Fig. 4b), we see that – compared with pristine RuO2 – more empty states (ca. 1.35-fold of RuO2, according to the integral area) are observed in the eg orbitals in Sr-modulated SrRuIrOx. The d-band PDOS moves to a higher energy, implying that surface Ru in SrRuIrOx is more positively charged than RuO2, which agrees well with the Bader charge analysis. Because the eg orbitals directly participate in the σ-bonding with surface adsorbates, the eg occupancy can influence the binding of oxo-intermediates, further facilitating OER32,35. Overall, Sr incorporation redistributed the charge of the elements and promoted the formation of high-valence Ru sites, which in turn facilitated OER. Investigation of the stability of SrRuIrOx. We then investigated the origins of high stability SrRuIrOx in acidic electrolytes. We first measured ion dissolution during electrolysis via inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICPOES)36,37. Fig. 4c indicates that Sr ions leached out rapidly at the beginning of the reaction, and then reached a stable state after ~12 h, while Ru and Ir remained. With the dissolution of Sr, the overpotential increased accordingly until dissolution stopped. In contrast, in IrOx/SrIrOx14, the overpotential decreased with Sr dissolution, suggesting distinct roles for Sr in the present vs. in the IrOx/SrIrOx study. High-valence Ru species in SrRuOx leached out of the structure, and this was accompanied by a loss in activity (Fig 2c and Fig. S15). We conclude that Ir plays an important role in anchoring highvalence Ru during OER.

We further evaluated stabilization using an analysis of Pourbaix-stable phases, chemical structures, and composition. Fig. 4d depicts computed phase stability in Pourbaix diagrams of the SrRuIrOx phases in an aqueous environment with respect to dissolved and solid phases. In the experimental working potential (U = 1.4 to 2 V vs. RHE) and electrolyte pH region (pH = 0 to 1), the Sr1Ru6Ir1O16 crystal is predicted stable (Fig. S16 and Supplementary note 3). The molar ratio of Sr-Ru-Ir in simulation matched with the experimental elemental quantification of the sample using ICP-OES and EDX mapping (Sr:Ru:Ir = 1:6:1) during steady state OER operation after 12 h (Fig. S17). FT-EXAFS of Ru K-edge after 24 h, 48 h, and 120 h electrolysis further verified the structural stability of SrRuIrOx (Fig. S18). Thus, in the ternary SrRuIrOx catalyst, the doping of Ir stabilizes the structure and anchors high-valence Ru sites. We further calculated the energy of Ru and Sr atoms dissociation from the surface by DFT (Fig. S19). The results show that, after incorporating Ir into the lattice, Ru and Sr need more energy to dissociate into solution, explaining the anchoring effect of Ir. Based on the above results, we conclude that doping Sr into RuO2 lattice improves the OER activity through the formation of high-valence Ru sites, while doping Ir anchors the highvalence Ru and structures of SrRuIrOx. The atomic level dual-modulation of Sr and Ir in Ru oxides finally made SrRuIrOx as highly active and stable acidic OER electrocatalysts.
