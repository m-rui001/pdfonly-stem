* Corresponding authors: M.N.: naguib@tulane.edu, M.W.B.: barsoumw@drexel.edu, Y.G.: gogotsi@drexel.edu Abstract Since their discovery in 2011, the number of two-dimensional (2D) transition metal carbides and nitrides (MXenes) kept steadily increased. In time, they may develop into the largest family of 2D materials. Currently more than 40 MXene compositions exist. The ultimate number and is far greater. MXenes’ unique properties, such as their metal-like electrical conductivity reaching ~20,000 S/cm, render them very useful in a large number of applications including energy storage, optoelectronic, biomedical, communications and environmental. The number of MXene papers and patents published has been growing quickly. The first MXene generation was synthesized using selective etching of metal layers from the MAX phases, layered transition metal carbides and carbonitrides using hydrofluoric acid. Since then, multiple synthesis approaches have been developed, including selective etching in a mixture of fluoride salts and various acids, non-aqueous etchants, halogens and molten salts, allowing for the synthesis of new MXenes with better control over their surface chemistries. Herein, we provide a historical overview of the first ten years of MXene research and a perspective on their synthesis and future development. The fact that their production is readily scalable in aqueous environments with high yields, bodes well for their commercialization.

Introduction After the discovery of the unusual physical properties in single- and few-layer graphene in 2004, two-dimensional (2D) materials captured the imagination of scientist, engineers and investors, becoming a very dynamic and exciting research field.1 This discovery attracted the attention to 2D materials beyond graphene and quickly led to the exfoliation and exploration of other materials having readily available layered precursors, from graphite-like hexagonal boron nitride h-BN to dichalcogenides and layered oxides.2 Soon after, 2D Si,3 Ge,4 Sn5 and several other materials that do not have weakly bonded layered precursors was demonstrated. This was a clear indication that the family of 2D materials was projected to expand, covering numerous compounds with a variety of structures and chemistries. In 2018, the computational 2D Materials Database (C2DB) reported on a variety of structural, thermodynamic, elastic, electronic, magnetic, and optical properties of around 1500 2D materials distributed over more than 30 different crystal structures.6 This potentially allows for an exciting prospective to use 2D building blocks with complementing properties to create artificial materials and build entire devices using various assemblies of 2D flakes.7-8 In 2011, and in spite of the existance of numerous 2D materials, the vast majority were semiconductors, semimetals or insulators with low electronic conductivities and carrier concentrations. As importantly, few 2D materials could be made in amounts sufficient for applications outside the microelectronics field. Solution processed 2D materials, except for graphene and h-BN, were quite small in flake size due to low mechanical strength leading to fracture of 2D sheets during delamination. Many of the the 2D materials were also hydrophobic and unstable in air. Therefore, the discovery of a family of 2D carbides and nitrides with metallic conductivity, hydrophilicity, ease of processing, relatively high yields and large flakes, had a profound effect on the entire materials science field. Early transition metal carbides and nitrides are an important class of materials due to their high metallic electrical conductivity, high hardness, and excellent chemical stabilities.911

MXene Synthesis As noted above, our first report on 2D transition metal carbide was focused on titanium carbide (Ti3C2) which was synthesized using aqueous hydrofluoric acid, HF, as an etchant for Al at room temperature, RT.21 It is worth noting that HF was used to selectively etch the A layers with the goal of creating more space for electrochemical Li intercalation/deintercalation in the MAX phases, which were researched for Li-ion battery anodes, not with the intention to make a 2D material. More details about the facinating story of the MXenes discovery can be found in the preface we wrote in Ref [25]. Like Ti3C2, other representatives of the first generation of MXenes were all synthesized by selectively etching the Al, layers from different MAX phases (Ti2AlC, V2AlC, Nb2AlC, Nb4AlC3, Ta4AlC3, Ti3Al(C0.5N0.5)2, (Ti0.5Nb0.5)2AlC, (V0.5Cr0.5)3AlC2, (Ti0.5V0.5)2AlC, (Ti0.5V0.5)3AlC2, and (Nb0.5V0.5)4AlC3) using aqueous HF at RT.23, 26-28 However, the etching conditions (acid concentration, temperature, time) had to be modified for each composition to achieve the conversion, since the bond strengths between the different M elements and Al varied.29 Another reason for the importance of tuning the etching conditions is to maintain high yields and minimize the degradation of 2D flakes in the acid, because their chemical stability varies depending the structure and composition. Since the synthesis is carried out in a fluoride containing aqueous solution, the transition metal surfaces of MXene are terminated by O, OH and F (for simplicity Tx is used hereafter to describe mixed surface terminations).21-22 The resulting MXene, after HF etching, is a multilayer material with a morphology that resembles kaolinite clay. Due to surface terminations, these multilayers are held together by a mixture of hydrogen and van der Waals bonds.30 Unlike the pristine MAX phases, these secondary bonds allow for intercalation between the layers. Intercalation of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in Ti3C2Tx followed by sonication results in a colloidal solution of delaminated Ti3C2Tx in water.31 Interestingly, the DMSO intercalation was not a universal approach, since it did not intercalate all MXenes. Fine tuning the MAX phases can affect the synthesis conditions of MXenes. For example, adding very small amount of Sc to the precursors of V2AlC results in formation of a solid solution of V-Sc that reduces significantly the etching time needed to make V2-yCTx (y < 0.05) since traces of Sc change the stability of the MAX phase.49 Having a solid solution at the A sites in the MAX phase like in the case of Ti3(Si0.75Al0.25)C2 and Ti3(Si0.1Al0.9)C2 weakens the bonding between the carbide sheets through the A layers and therefore allows for exfoliating these materials with the aid of ultrasonication, which is not possible for either Ti3SiC2 nor Ti3AlC2.50 Here the end result is not MXenes however, but thin MAX platelets. Another example of the effect of MAX phase chemistry on MXene is the work by Mathis et al.51 who showed significant enhancement in both stability and conductivity of MXene by having excess Al during the synthesis of Ti3AlC2, which leads to formation of highly stoichiometric MAX and MXene. Simple modifications for the etching conditions such as the ratio of fluoride salt to acid,52 or bubbling nitrogen gas during etching53 may change the properties of the resulting MXene significantly. For MXenes beyond Ti3C2Tx - the first and most studied MXene - further optimization and modification of the synthesis conditions are needed to improve flake size and minimize the concentration of defects.

Many cations and organic molecules can be intercalated spontaneously in-between the MXene sheets, further adding to the versatility of their compositions and properties.31 For example, very recently, Brady et al.,88 reported on the spontaneous intercalation of up to 2 Na per Ti3C2 unit by soaking multilayer MXenes in a solution of Na-biphenyl radical anion complex at RT. With about 30 reported distinct stoichiometric MXenes and countless possible compositions due to solid solutions on both M and X sites and various terminations, the number of these materials will indubitably continue growing and in due course, may become the largest family of 2D materials. A group of leading MXene researchers formulated a list of question in the MXenes field that should be addressed and those were discussed and ranked by several hundreds of attendees of the 3rd International Conference on MXenes in October 2020.114 Environmentally friendly, safe, efficient, and scalable synthesis methods, control of surface chemistry, improvement in chemical stability, and understanding of physical properties of MXenes were rated as the highest priority for further expansion of the field and for moving towards practical applications. Understanding the health and environmental safety/toxicity of MXenes with different transition metals and surface terminations is also a high priority. A majority of the fundamental studies on synthesis optimization and effect of synthesis and processing parameters on MXenes’ surface chemistry and properties have been carried out on Ti3C2Tx. Therefore, systematic studies of the effect of synthesis and processing conditions on structure, surface chemistry and properties of MXenes with different M, X and n will be of a great value for advancing our understanding of these materials.
