20 1. Introduction 21 Fault diagnosis serves an important role in pursuing the relationship between the monitoring 22 data and the health states of machines [1, 2], which has been a widely concerned issue in 23

machine health management. Traditionally, the relationship is caught by abundant experience 1 and huge expert knowledge of engineers. For example, an experienced engineer is able to 2 diagnose the faults of engines depending on the abnormal sound or locate the bearing faults by 3 using advanced signal processing methods to analyze the vibration signals. In engineering 4 scenarios, however, the machine users would like an automatic method to shorten the 5 maintenance cycle and improve the diagnosis accuracy. In particular, with the help of artificial 6 intelligence, the procedure of fault diagnosis is expected to be intelligent enough to 7 automatically detect and recognize the health states of machines [2-5]. 8 Intelligent fault diagnosis (IFD) refers to applications of machine learning theories, such as 9 artificial neural networks (ANN), support vector machine (SVM), and deep neural networks 10 (DNN), to machine fault diagnosis [6, 7], which is promising to achieve the above purpose. This 11 kind of methods uses machine learning theories to adaptively learn the diagnosis knowledge of 12 machines from the collected data instead of utilizing the experience and knowledge of 13 engineers. Specifically, IFD aims to construct diagnosis models that are able to automatically 14 bridge the relationship between the collected data and the health states of machines. 15 In recent years, IFD has attracted much attention from academic researchers and industrial 16 engineers, which deeply relates to the development of machine learning [6-9]. We count the 17 number of publications about IFD based on the search results from the Web of Science, which is 18 shown in Fig. 1. According to the results on the topic of machine fault diagnosis by using 19 machine learning, the number of publications has rapidly increased since the 2010s. Step 2: Artificial feature extraction 10 Artificial feature extraction includes two steps. First, some commonly-used features, such as 11 time-domain features, frequency-domain features, and time-frequency-domain features, are 12 extracted from the collected data. These features contain the health information reflecting the 13 health states of machines. Second, feature selection methods, such as filters, wrappers, and 14 embedded methods, are used to select sensitive features to health states of machines from the 15 extracted features. It is beneficial to getting rid of the redundant information and further 16 improving the diagnosis results. These two steps are detailed as follows. 17 2.3.1. Feature extraction 18 The commonly-used features can be extracted from the time domain, the frequency domain 19 analysis, or the time-frequency domain. 1) The time-domain features can be divided into the 20 dimensional ones and the dimensionless ones. The former includes mean, standard deviation, 21 root amplitude, root mean square, peak value, etc., which are affected by the speed and the load 22 of machines. The later mostly includes shape indicator, skewness, kurtosis, crest indicator, 23

clearance indicator, impulse indicator, etc., which are robust to the operation conditions of 1 machines [56, 57]. 2) The frequency-domain features are extracted from the frequency 2 spectrum, such as mean frequency, frequency center, root mean square frequency, standard 3 deviation frequency, etc., which are introduced in Ref. [56, 57]. They contain the information 4 that cannot found in the time-domain features. 3) The time-frequency-domain features, such as 5 energy entropy [56, 57], are usually extracted by wavelet transform (WT), wavelet package 6 transform (WPT) or empirical model decompose (EMD). These features are able to reflect 7 health states of machines under non-stationary operation conditions. 8 2.3.2. Feature selection 9 The extracted features from the time domain, the frequency domain, and the time-frequency 10 domain contain the redundant information. They may aggravate the computation cost, and even 11 result in the curse of dimensionality. To weaken this problem, some publications [58-65] select 12 sensitive features to the health states of machines from the collected features. They can be 13 divided into three categories, i.e., filters, wrappers, and embedded methods. 14 (1) Filter-based methods 15 Filters directly preprocess the collected features, which are independent to the training of 16 the classifier [58]. Some filters are briefly introduced as follows. 1) Relief [66] and Relief-F [67] 17 construct a relevant indicator to determine the sensitivity of features to the health states of 18 machines. 2) Information gain and gain ratio [68], from the information theory, are also two 19 commonly-used methods for feature selection. The features with the greater information gain 20 and the higher gain ratio would be selected to train diagnosis models and improve their 21 diagnosis results. 3) Minimum Redundancy Maximum Relevance (mRMR) [69] attempts to 22 select features subject to the maximal dissimilarity with each other. 4) Fisher score [70] is 23

Page 13

Page 13

regarded as a distance metric to feature selection, which aims to select a feature that is able to 1 maximize the among-class distance, but minimize the in-class distance. 5) Distance evaluation 2 (DE) [71, 72] is used to select a feature set by the distance metric, in which the sensitive features 3 are subject to the small in-class distance and the large among-class distance. 4 (2) Wrapper-based methods 5 Different from filter-based methods, wrappers focus on the interaction of feature selection 6 with training classifiers [58]. In other words, the performance of classifiers is used to assess the 7 selected feature set. If the selected feature subset cannot produce the optimal classification 8 accuracy, another subset is reselected in the next iteration until the selected features enforce the 9 classifiers at the most favorable performance. Las Vegas wrapper (LVW) [73] is widely used to 10 select the features, in which the Las Vegas algorithm is employed to search for the feature 11 subset, and the error of the classifiers is considered as the metric to feature assessment. 12 (3) Embedded methods 13 Embedded methods integrate the feature selection into training the classifiers. Generally, 14 they impose the regularization terms on the optimization objects of the classifiers, and 15 automatically select the features once the training of classifiers is done [58]. Two regularization 16 terms are commonly considered. One is the L1 regularization [74], and the other one is the L2 17 regularization [75]. Both them can alleviate the over fitting that occurs in training with a small 18 amount of training samples. In contrast, the L1 term prefers to obtain the sparse parameters, 19 which is able to abandon redundant features in classification and further enforce the classifier to 20 achieve the high classification performance. 21 2.4.
