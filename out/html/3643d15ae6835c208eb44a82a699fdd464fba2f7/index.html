Abstract Substantial improvements in energy density for lithium-ion batteries require the development of highcapacity electrodes. Alloy anodes with much higher capacity have been recognized as promising alternatives to commercial graphite. Without pre-stored lithium in alloy anodes, the energy density is limited by the low capacity of lithium metal oxide cathodes. Recently, Li metal has been revived as a high-capacity anode, but faces many challenges resulting from its high reactivity and uncontrolled dendrite growth. Here we develop a series of Li-containing anodes as alternatives to Li metal, inheriting the desirable properties of alloy anodes and pure metal anodes. This large-scale freestanding LixM/graphene foil (M = Si, Sn, Al etc.) consists of fine nanostructures of densely-packed LixM nanoparticles encapsulated by large graphene sheets. With fully-expanded LixM confined in the highlyconductive and chemically-stable graphene matrix, this foil maintains a stable structure and cyclability in half cells. The LixSi/graphene foil is successfully paired with high-capacity Li-free cathodes, such as V2O5 and Sulphur, to achieve stable full-cell cycling. LixM/graphene foils are stable in air, owing to their unique structure as well as the hydrophobicity and gas impermeability of graphene sheets. By addressing electrochemical and environmental stability simultaneously, these Li metal alternatives represent a significant breakthrough in battery research.

The emerging markets of electric vehicles have stimulated intensive research on lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries with high energy density and long cycle life1,2. Conventional anodes, primarily based on graphite with a theoretical capacity of 370 mAh/g, struggle to completely fulfill the demand3. Alloy anodes with much higher theoretical capacity (e. g. Si: 4200 mAh/g, Sn: 990 mAh/g, and SiO: 2670 mAh/g), have been widely recognized as promising alternatives for high energy density applications4. They suffer from the potential mechanical disintegration of the electrode structure and the instability of the

lithiation/delithiation5,6. Diverse microstructure/nanostructure designs were utilized to address these problems to achieve long-term cycling with high capacity7,8. Particularly, free-standing Si/C composites films were fabricated to realize light-weight electrodes with further improved electrochemical performance9-11. However, alloy anodes are free of Li and typically paired with low-capacity Li metal oxide cathode materials (<200 mAh/g), which significantly limits the energy density of Li-ion batteries12,13. Generally, Li-free cathode materials (e. g. Sulphur: 1672 mAh/g, V2O5: 441 mAh/g) have much higher specific capacity14-16. As a result, there is a strong motivation to develop a new full cell configuration with a Li-containing anode17. Li metal is an attractive anode to construct the full cells with Li-free cathodes, because of its highest specific capacity of 3860 mAh/g and the lowest anode potential18,19. However, Li metal anode has serious safety concerns and low Coulombic efficiency (CE), due to its uncontrolled dendrite formation, relative infinity volume change during Li plating and stripping, and high chemical reactivity with organic electrolytes20-22. Extensive works, including Li/3D matrix composites23,24, electrolyte additives25,26, mechanically and chemically stable interphase27,28, and spatial deposition control29, have significantly suppressed the dendrite formation and pushed the CE in corrosive carbonate electrolytes up to 98%. However, it is still far from the real application. A new Li-containing anode with long-term stability and high CE is therefore highly desired. In addition, the use of Li metal is challenging due to its demanding processing requirements. Li

Page 4

metal is corroded immediately once exposure to humid air30. Li metal anodes with fine structures typically show improved electrochemical performance and worsened stability associated with their enlarged surface area. Li2CO3 coating on Li microparticle has been employed to enhance its stability, but the use of costly dry room is still necessary31,32. Other coating such as Al2O3 by atomic layer deposition improves the stability under the ambient condition (40% relative humidity (RH))30. However, the surface texture of Li metal is obviously changed after the air exposure for several hours. An air-stable, Licontaining anode is therefore highly desired. Here we develop a facile process to fabricate large-scale and free-standing LixM/graphene foils (M = Si, Sn, Al etc.) with the novel nanostructure of densely-packed reactive LixM nanoparticles (NPs) encapsulated by large graphene sheets (Graphene refers to few-layer (<10 layers) graphene. Fig. 1a). The volumetric capacity of the LixSi/graphene foil is 1800~2000 mAh/cm3, approaching the theoretical value of Li metal (2061 mAh/cm3)33. With fully-expanded LixSi confined in the graphene matrix, the foil avoids the intrinsic problems of volume expansion and dendrite growth that plague Li metal, exhibiting stable cycling (400 cycles with 98% capacity retention) at areal capacity of ~2.4 mAh/cm2 in half cells. The LixSi/graphene foil is successfully paired with Li-free cathodes (V2O5 and Sulphur) to achieve stable full-cell cycling. The cells are in the charged state after assembly, delivering an estimated energy density of ~ 500 Wh/kg. The excellent air-stability of LixM/graphene foils is attributed to their unique structure that each LixM cluster has 3D and nanoscale protection of the surrounding graphene sheets. The hydrophobicity34,35 and gas impermeability36 of the graphene sheets prevent the absorption and penetration of gas molecules.

Fabrication and characterizations of the LixM/graphene foil. We have developed a scalable approach to fabricate the LixSi/graphene foil (Fig. 1b). Similar to our previous study, LixSi NPs were synthesized in gram-scale, by heating a mixture of Li metal and Si NPs under mechanical stirring in an argon glovebox37. LixSi NPs were mixed with graphene sheets and poly(styrene-butadiene-styrene) (SBS)

Page 5

rubber (80:10:10 by weight) in toluene to form a slurry, which was then casted on a polyethylene terephthalate (PET) release film. After drying, it can be easily peeled off as a large freestanding foil. In case there are bare LixSi NPs on the surface, thin layer of graphene sheets could be coated on the double sides of the foil to further enhance the protection. The whole foil preparation process is applicable to other Li alloy forming materials, such as Al and Sn. The photograph (Fig. 2a) shows a large LixSi/graphene foil with 8 cm width and 24 cm length. In supplementary movie 1, the foil is rolled on a tube with a diameter of 3 mm and then unfolded without any damaging. The superior mechanical property of LixSi/graphene foil is further demonstrated by the uniaxial tensile test, which shows similar trend to Li metal foil with much higher stress (Fig. 2d). LixSi/graphene can also be coated on double sides of copper foil to form an electrode similar to the one used in cylindrical cells (Fig. S1). The graphene sheets were prepared by exfoliation of graphite with the assistance of metal halide38. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image with low magnification (Fig. 2b) shows overlapped and interconnected graphene sheets with size up to several microns. A double-layer graphene is clearly observed in Fig. 2c, with the average inter-layer distance of 0.334 nm (Fig. S2a). TEM image in Fig. S2b shows graphene sheets with 3~6 layers. Raman spectroscopy analysis also suggests multilayer graphene (Fig. S2c)39. The D to G peak intensity ratio (ID/IG=0.05) indicates the low concentration of defects in graphene40. The quality of graphene is further confirmed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (Fig. S2d), showing extremely low oxygen content. X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern (Fig. 2e) of the LixSi/graphene foil contains peaks of Li22Si5 (PDF# 01-077-2882), graphitic carbon (PDF# 00-0560159), and Li2O (PDF# 04-008-3420). LixSi NPs are identifiable underneath the thin graphene sheet (Fig. 2f). LixSi alloy is already in its fully-expanded state, so no void space need to be created to accommodate the volume expansion of Si. Accordingly, LixSi/graphene foil is ready to be thoroughly calendared without damaging its microstructures. After calendared at large stress of 40 MPa, LixSi NPs are in a densely-packed state as shown in the cross-sectional view of the SEM image (Fig. 2g).

Stability of the LixSi/graphene foil. The gas impermeability of graphene, and the hydrophobicity of graphene and SBS rubber improve the stability of LixSi/graphene foil. A water drop placed on top of the pure graphene foil gives a contact angle of 93.1º (Fig. 3a). SBS is dissolved in toluene and then spincoated on Si wafer. The contact angle of 99.9º confirms the hydrophobicity of SBS. After dispersed in the SBS/toluene solution for 2 h, LixSi NPs are enwrapped with a ~ 40 nm thick coating (Fig. S3). Theoretically, graphene is impermeable to gases and robust against oxidation (Fig. 3b, upper), suggesting that few-layer graphene sheets are effective anti-corrosion barriers36. The hydrophobic nature repels the contact with water molecules. Furthermore, the gas impermeability nature ensures the graphene sheets can prevent gas penetration, even if the molecules in the air are adsorbed on the foil. The cross-sectional SEM image (Fig. 3b, bottom) shows the LixSi NPs aggregate to form a cluster during calendaring and the cluster is perfectly encapsulated by graphene sheets. Therefore, each LixSi cluster has 3D protection of graphene sheets. The photos of Li foil and LixSi/graphene foil exposed to ambient air indicate the superior stability of LixSi/graphene foil (Fig. 3c). The humidity level in the daytime is 20%~30% RH and 50%~60% RH at night. After exposure to ambient air for just 3 min, the color of Li metal turns black. The XPS analysis (Fig. S4) indicates the formation of Li2CO3 after 48 h exposure41. The color of LixSi/graphene foil remains the same even after 48 h. The cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. S5a,b) confirm the thickness change is negligible after exposure to ambient air for 1 day. To test whether the foil is stable enough for the baking process, the foil with 19 µm thickness was baked at 80 ºC for 6 h in the dry room (dew point −50 ºC). The areal capacity of LixSi/graphene foil remains almost unchanged, indicating this foil can survive the baking process to remove residual solvent and water (Fig. 3d). To investigate the dry-air stability, the foil was stored in the dry room for 2 weeks and examined the remaining capacity over time. After 2 weeks, it still maintains 94.3% of its original capacity, exhibiting a high areal capacity of 3.6 mAh/cm2 (Fig. 3e). XRD confirms there is no obvious change after dry-air exposure (Fig. S6b). Nano-scale Li alloy is extremely unstable in the ambient air, losing most of the capacity within just several hours (inset of Fig. 3f). With the protection of graphene sheets, the

Page 7

LixSi/graphene foil exhibits excellent stability even in the air with high humidity level (20%~60% RH). After exposed to ambient air for 3 days, the foil exhibits a high areal capacity of 3.1 mAh/cm2, showing capacity retention of 81% (Fig. 3f). The XRD pattern reveals small peaks belonging to LiOH (PDF# 00032-0564), which is related to H2O contamination in the humid air (Fig. S6c). Rietveld refinement quantitative analysis confirms Li22Si5 to be remained as the major component. With lower amount of graphene, the foil (LixSi:graphene:SBS=90:5:5 by weight) exhibits large capacity drop (42%) after exposure to the ambient air for the same time (Fig. S7). The remarkable stability of the LixSi/graphene foil increases the possibility for safe handling and simplifies the requirement on the industrial battery fabrication environment.

Half-cell and full-cell performance of the LixSi/graphene foil. The LixSi/graphene foil affords remarkable electrochemical performance in both half and full cells. The first-cycle delithiation capacities of LixSi/graphene foils with the thickness of 19 µm and 42 µm were 3.8 mAh/cm2 and 8.3 mAh/cm2 in half cells, respectively (Fig. 4a and Fig. S8). Based on the foil thickness and areal capacity, the volumetric capacity is ~2000 mAh/cm3, close to the theoretical value of Li22Si5 (2240 mAh/cm3) and Li (2061 mAh/cm3)4,33. The experimental values for Si anodes reported in the literatures are generally lower than 1300 mAh/cm3, due to the low tap density of nanostructured Si and void space created to accommodate the volume expansion8. The specific capacity of LixSi/graphene foil is ~1600 mAh/g based on the mass of LixSi. The foils with the thickness of 12 µm and 19 µm are used to evaluate the long-term cycling performance (Fig. 4b). From the fourth to 400th cycle at a current density of 1 mA/cm2, the capacity retention of 19 µm thick foil is ~98%, showing a high areal capacity of 2.4 mAh/cm2. There are two characteristics that allow for the superior cycling performance. The first is the high conductivity of graphene sheets (1400 S/cm). The second is Si is already in its fully lithiated and expanded state. The morphology change of individual LixSi clusters during cycling is indicated by the cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. 4c). LixSi cluster (~ 5 µm) is encapsulated by graphene sheets. Void space is created during

Page 8

the delithiation process, which accommodates further volume expansion of subsequent lithiation process. Therefore, the foil avoids the intrinsic problems of volume expansion that plague alternatives like Li metal or unlithiated Si. The LixSi/graphene foil is in the fully charged state and discharged directly to deliver high capacity at the first cycle. Therefore, there is no 1st cycle CE. CE of the following cycles is defined as delithiation capacity over lithiation capacity. The 2nd cycle CE is 98.7%, and the CE of the later cycles reaches a high value of 99.92%. The improved early and later cycle CE is ascribed to several characteristics. In the foil, the LixSi cluster is enwrapped in graphene sheets, which is chemically and structurally similar to graphite anodes. LixSi NPs are packed densely in cluster form and encapsulated by graphene sheets to lower the surface accessible to the electrolyte and therefore reduces the side reactions. The excellent performance of LixSi/graphene foil in half cells makes it possible to be used in full cells. First, the LixSi/graphene foil is paired with traditional cathode LiFePO4 (LiFePO4:Super P:PVDF=70:20:10 by weight). Fig. 4d shows the voltage profiles of foil-LiFePO4 and Li-LiFePO4 cells between 2.5 and 3.8 V. The voltage drop of full cell is due to the higher voltage plateau (~0.4 V) of Si. The full cell and half cell show similar capacities of 132.2 mAh/g and 130.6 mAh/g at 1C (The capacity and rate are both based on the mass of LiFePO4). The rate capability was tested at various rates from 0.2 C to 20 C (Fig. 4e). At low rate, the capacities of LiFePO4 in full cell and half cell are similar. LiFePO4 in full cell exhibits higher capacity especially at a high rate (~91.7 mAh/g at 10 C and 84.0 mAh/g at 20 C). Both cells exhibit stable cycling for 200 cycles at 1C (Fig. S9). The average CE of the full cell is 99.92%, slightly higher than the half cell (99.84%). After cycling, cells were dissembled to check the morphology of LixSi/graphene foil and Li counter electrode. As shown in Fig. S10b, Li metal cracks and the superficial Li is even peeled off after 200 cycles, due to the uncontrolled Li stripping/plating. The magnified SEM image shows massive dendrite formation on Li metal (Fig. S10c). The morphology of LixSi/graphene foil remains almost the same (Fig. S10e,f). The XPS spectra before and after sputtering confirm the SEI formed on the Li metal is thick and mainly consists of Li2CO3 and LiF (Fig. S11)41,42.

In contrast, XPS performed on the foil mainly detects signals from the electrolytes. The LixSi/graphene foil can also be paired with V2O5 cathode (V2O5 nanosheets:Super P:PVDF=70:20:10 by weight), exhibiting stable cycling performance at the capacity around 365 mAh/g at 1C (The capacity is based on the mass of V2O5 in the cathode. 1C=441 mA/g of V2O5. The mass ratio of anode to cathode is 1:4. Fig. S12). If the average voltage is treated as 2.5 V, the estimated energy density is around 510 Wh/kg.

Characterizations of the Sulphur electrode and the Sulphur batteries. SEM image of the graphitic C-encapsulated sulphur composite (denoted as G-S) indicates Sulphur can permeate into the graphitic C cage by capillary force and the composite maintains the original morphology of the cage (Fig. 5a). TEM image confirms the Sulphur particles are well encapsulated by the graphitic C shell (Fig. 5b). XRD pattern shows sharp and strong Sulphur diffraction peaks (PDF# 00-08-0247, Fig. S13a). The broad peak between 23° and 30° is ascribed to the graphitic C cage. Fig. 5c shows the voltage profile of the LixSi/graphene foil-S cell between 1.3 and 2.6 V, which shows a specific capacity of 1086 mAh/g with an average voltage of ~1.8 V (The capacity is based on the mass of G-S in the cathode.). The mass ratio of anode to cathode is 1.8:1. Therefore, the estimated energy density is around 490 Wh/kg. There are two discharge voltage plateaus at 2.1 and 1.7 V, which agree with the Li-S discharge plateaus (2.4 and 2.1 V) and Li-Si (0.4 V) in half cells43. The foil-S cell (Fig. 5d) demonstrates excellent cycling stability at 0.5 C and maintains a capacity of 858 mAh/g after 110 cycles with CE of 99.5%, higher than that of half cell (98.8%), indicating its great potential to replace Li metal in Sulphur batteries (1C=1672 mA/g of S). In the Li-S cell, the highly soluble polysulphides can easily diffuse from cathode to anode, and form a deposit of Li2S2/Li2S on the Li metal, which is confirmed by the XPS spectrum of S 2p (Fig. 5e)38,42. The counts of Li2S2 and Li2S peaks are highly suppressed for the foil, which indicates the protection of chemically-stable graphene sheets minimizes the shuttle effect significantly. The relatively lower peaks of O-C=O and SO32- confirms the decomposition of Li salt is also suppressed in the foil-S cell. Without the passivation of LiNO3 additive, the foil-S cell loses 57% capacity after 110 cycles and consistently

exhibit lower CE, which confirms the necessity of LiNO3 additive in the foil-S cells (Fig. S13b).

The whole foil preparation process is applicable to other materials. LixM NPs were synthesized by heating a mixture of M NPs and Li metal under inert atmosphere. M refers to Sn, Al and potentially other materials forming alloy with Li. LixM/graphene foil is prepared via vacuum filtration of a suspension of LixM and graphene sheets in toluene. The slurry coating and peeling-off approach mentioned above is also suitable. The XRD pattern indicates LixSn/graphene foil is made of Li22Sn5 (PDF#01-081-6569) and graphitic carbon (Fig. S14). The XRD pattern of the LixAl/graphene foil confirms the existence of Li3Al2 (PDF#04-007-1619) and graphitic carbon. The LixSn/graphene foil and LixAl/graphene foil deliver high areal capacities of 2 mAh/cm2 and 2.5 mAh/cm2, respectively (Fig. S15). The LixM/graphene foils exhibit stable cycling at C/20 for the first three cycles and C/2 for the following cycle, which is contributed by the similar structure that fully-expanded LixM confined in the highly-conductive graphene matrix (Fig. S16a). After exposed to the ambient air for three days, the LixSn/graphene and LixAl/graphene foils exhibit high capacity retention of 91% and 88% respectively (Fig. S16b). In this work, we have developed a facile process to fabricate large-scale and free-standing LixM/graphene anodes with the unique foil structure consisting of densely-packed LixM NPs encapsulated by large graphene sheets. M refers to Si, Sn, Al and potentially other materials forming alloy with Li. With long-term cyclability and high CE in half cells, LixSi/graphene foil is successfully paired with high-capacity Li-free cathodes (Sulphur and V2O5) to achieve stable full-cell cyclability. The LixM/graphene foil is highly stable in different air conditions due to its unique structure that each LixM cluster has 3D and nanoscale protection of graphene sheets. By addressing electrochemical and environmental stability simultaneously, these novel Li-containing anodes serve as attractive alternatives to Li metal, representing a significant breakthrough in energy research.

Methods

Page 11

Preparation of LixM/graphene foils. M represents Si, Al, Sn and other materials forming alloy with Li. M NPs (Si NPs (100 nm, MTI Inc.), Al NPs (50 nm, MTI Inc.), and Sn NPs (<150 nm, Sigma-Aldrich)) were dried under vacuum for 48 h to remove trapped water. LixM was prepared by mechanical stirring of a stoichiometric mixture of M NPs and Li metal foil at 250 ºC for 48 h in a glove box filled with argon (H2O level <0.1 p.p.m. and O2 level <1.0 p.p.m.). The synthesis temperature of LixSn is slightly different, maintained between the melting points of Li metal and Sn to ensure the preservation of the morphology of Sn NPs. The graphene sheets were fabricated by intercalation-expansion-exfoliation method, which was exfoliated via a graphite intercalation compound with negligible oxidation as described in the reference38. LixM alloy was mixed with graphene sheets and SBS (Kraton D1161) rubber (80:10:10 by weight) in anhydrous toluene to form a slurry, which was then casted on a commercial PET release film. After drying, it was peeled off as a large freestanding LixM/graphene foil. LixM/graphene foils were also prepared via vacuum filtration of a suspension of LixM and graphene sheets (80:20 by weight) in anhydrous toluene. In case there are bare LixSi NPs on the surface of the foil, thin layer of graphene sheets was coated on the double sides of the foil to further enhance the protection. After drying, a pressure of 40 MPa was applied on the foil and held for 0.5 h. The whole foil preparation process was performed under argon atmosphere. Electrochemical testing. For LixM/graphene foil half cells, the working electrodes were assembled in 2032-type coin cells with Li metal (Alfa Aesar) as the counter electrode. The electrolyte was 1.0 M LiPF6 in 1:1 v/v ethylene carbonate/diethyl carbonate (BASF, LP40) with 10 vol% fluoroethylene carbonate and 1 vol % vinylene carbonate. After a cell was assembled, it took about 6 h for the electrode to reach equilibrium as determined by the stabilized potential of the anode. For the fabrication of LiFePO4 cathode, a LiFePO4 slurry (LiFePO4 (MTI Inc):Super P (TIMCAL, Switzerland):PVDF (Kynar HSV 900)) =70:20:10 by weight) was coated onto Al foil and dried under vacuum. The total mass loading was ~10 mg/cm2. In the foil-LiFePO4 cell, the mass ratio of anode to cathode is 1:5. For the fabrication of V2O5 cathode, a V2O5 slurry (V2O5 nanosheets:Super P:PVDF=70:20:10 by weight) was coated onto Al foil

Page 12

and dried under vacuum. For the fabrication of graphitic C-encapsulated sulphur electrodes, a sulphur slurry (G-S:Super P:PVDF=70:20:10 by weight) was coated on Al foil and dried under vacuum. The mass loading of the sulphur cathode was ~1 mg/cm2. The electrolyte was 1.0 M lithium bistrifluoromethanesulphonylimide in 1:1 v/v 1,3-dioxolane/1,2-dimethoxyethane with 0.4 M LiNO3 additive. In the foil-S cell, the mass ratio of anode to cathode is 1.8:1. Galvanostatic cycling was carried out using an MTI 8 Channel battery tester and the voltage depends on the specific cell. For the full cell performance, rate and capacity are both based on the mass of active cathode materials. After assembly of LixSi/graphene foil-S cell, the cell was in the charged state. For the calculation of CE, the 1st discharge capacity was ignored. The 2nd CE= Capacity2nd discharge/Capacity1st charge ×100%. Characterizations. Powder X-ray diffraction was performed on a PANalyticalX’Pert with Ni-filtered Cu Kα radiation. XPS analysis was performed on PHI Versa Probe 5000, Physical Electronics, USA. SEM images were taken using a FEI XL30 Sirion SEM. The TEM images were acquired using FEI Titan 80–300 environmental TEM at an acceleration voltage of 80 kV. The stress-strain test was performed on TA Instrument Q800 dynamic mechanical analyzer. Data availability. The data that support the plots within this paper and other finding of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Figure 1 | Schematic of the microstructure and fabrication process of the LixM/graphene foils. a, A schematic illustrates the unique foil structure consisting of densely-packed reactive LixM NPs encapsulated by large graphene sheets. M corresponds to Si, Sn, Al and potentially other materials alloying with Li. This foil is highly stable in different air conditions thanks to its unique structure as well as the hydrophobicity and gas impermeability of graphene sheets. The photograph of large LixSi/graphene foil shows the good flexibility and the potential to scale up towards industrial fabrication. b, LixM NPs were synthesized by heating a stoichiometric mixture of M NPs and Li metal under mechanical stirring in an argon atmosphere. LixM NPs were mixed with graphene sheets and SBS rubber (80:10:10 by weight) to form a slurry, which was then casted on a PET release film. After drying, it was peeled off as a large freestanding LixM/graphene foil.
