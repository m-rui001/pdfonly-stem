Figure S1. Hermetic vessel designed and used for LiF coating. The digital camera image showing the hermetic vessel used in this work for LiF coating, where the pressure gauge is used to control the pressure of Freon R-134 gas in the vessel. The valve at the top is used to pump the vessel and fill the Freon R-134 gas. The Li electrodes can be adhered to the bottom inside the vessel. Temperature can be controlled by heating the bottom with an external heat source.

Figure S5. Stability comparison of untreated Li foil (top) and LiF coated Li foil (bottom) in ambient air condition. The untreated Li foil gets corroded instantly in the air and quickly turns black due to the formation of Li3N, while the LiF coated Li foil remains the original silvery color without notable change during the whole test. The temperature of the testing environment is ~18-20 °C, with relative humidity (RH) ~30-40 %.

Figure S7. XPS depth profiles of C1s. Starting from the curve on the top, 30 s sputtering was performed between two measurements. Overall the C1s signals decays dramatically after sputtering, which indicates that only small amount of organic compound was absorbed on the surface. Signals corresponding to CF2 (~291-292 eV) and CF (~287-288 eV) were found in the spectra.

Page 8

Figure S8. X-ray diffraction (XRD) of LiF coating on Li metal foil. The strong Li background can be attributed to the thick Li foil underneath the LiF coating. Characteristic peaks of LiF can be observed from the XRD pattern, indicating the crystalline nature of LiF coating. The amorphous halo at ~20° can be attributed to the Kapton tape covered on the surface which avoids the direct air exposure of Li.

Figure S9. Digital photo images showing the pristine layered Li-rGO (left) and the LiFcoated layered Li-rGO (right). Color change was observed after applying Freon R-134a treatment.
