第 3 页

Abstract

Electrochemical conversion of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ into ammonia $ (\mathrm{N H}{3}) $ recycles nitrogen and offers a route to $ \mathrm{N H}{3} $ production that is more valuable than dinitrogen gas. However, today's development of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ electroreduction remains hindered by the lack of a mechanistic picture of how catalyst structure may be tuned to enhance catalytic activity. Here we demonstrate enhanced nitrate reduction reaction $ (\mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R}) $ performance on $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ alloy catalysts, including a 0.12 V upshift in the half-wave potential and a 6-fold increase in activity compared to pure Cu at 0 V vs. reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE). Ni alloying enables tuning of the Cu d-band center and modulates the adsorption energies of intermediates such as $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ $ \mathrm{N O_{2}} $ , and $ \mathrm{*N H_{2}} $ . Using density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we identify a $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R} $ -to- $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ pathway and offer an adsorption energy- activity relationship for the CuNi alloy system. This correlation between catalyst electronic structure and $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R} $ activity offers a design platform for further development of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R} $ catalysts.

第 4 页

Introduction

Human activities to an anthropogenically-induced increase over time in the concentration of environmental nitrate $ \mathrm{(NO_{3}^{-})}:^{1,2} $ the combustion of fossil fuels emits nitrous oxides $ \mathrm{(NO_{x})}; $ fertilizer-intensive agriculture releases $ \mathrm{NO_{3}^{-}} $ into soil and groundwater; and $ \mathrm{NO_{3}^{-}} $ -containing waste is discharged from industrial sources. The accumulation of $ \mathrm{NO_{3}^{-}} $ induces acid rain and photochemical smog $ ^{3,4} $ and the uptake of $ \mathrm{NO_{3}^{-}} $ in mammals results in its in vivo conversion to nitrite $ \mathrm{(NO_{2}^{-})},^{5,6} $ a cause of methemoglobinemia and a known carcinogen.

Closing the nitrate-nitrogen cycle $ ^{1,2,7,8} $ is therefore of interest: it is desirable to transform nitrates to harmless $ ^{9-11} $ or better yet to value-added products. $ ^{12,13} $ The electrochemical reduction of nitrate provides a route to ammonia production, $ ^{9,13} $ with widespread use as a fertilizer precursor, chemical feedstock and fuel:

$$ \mathrm {N O} _ {3} ^ {-} + 6 \mathrm {H} _ {2} \mathrm {O} + 8 \mathrm {e} ^ {-} \rightarrow \mathrm {N H} _ {3} + 9 \mathrm {O H} ^ {-}, \mathrm {E} ^ {0} = 0. 6 9 \mathrm {V} \mathrm {v s}. \mathrm {R H E} (\mathrm {p H} = 1 4) ^ {1 4} $$

Achieving higher-performance $ \mathrm{N O}_{3}^{-} \mathrm{R R} $ electrocatalysts remains challenging, in significant part because the relationship between catalyst structure and activity is poorly understood.

To date, Faradaic efficiencies (FE) greater than 90% for $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ reduction to $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ have been achieved on Cu-based catalysts, but typically these require potentials more negative than -0.27 V vs. RHE in $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ -containing 1 M KOH electrolytes, corresponding to an overpotential exceeding 0.96 V. $ ^{15-19} $ Previous studies found that alloying Cu with Ni results in a positive shift in the $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR half-wave potential $ (\mathrm{E}{1 / 2}) $ , the potential at which the current is equal to one half of the mass transfer limiting current, by $ \sim0.1 $ V, $ ^{18,19} $ a finding that corresponds to enhanced catalytic activity at a given potential. A mechanism wherein Cu performs the adsorption of $ \mathrm{N O{3}^{}} $ and Ni is the binding site of $ \mathrm{H^{}} $ was proposed by Simpson and Johnson; $ ^{20} $ however, this mechanism does not explain enhanced $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR activity on CuNi alloys: Ni converts $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ to

第 5 页

$ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ inefficiently, $ ^{19} $ so that replacing surface Cu atoms with Ni would be expected to reduce the density of active sites for $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ production.

We noted that upshifts in the half-wave potential $ \left( \mathrm{E}_{1/2} \right) $ in reduction reactions typically suggest an increase in electrocatalytic activity. This, we posited, could arise due to a modulated intermediate adsorption energy in CuNi alloys compared to pure Cu. Drawing parallels with oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) literature, we noted that platinum-nickel (PtNi) alloy catalysts typically exhibit an upshifted half-wave potential of $ \sim 0.1 $ V compared to pure Pt catalysts. $ ^{21,22} $ Decreasing adsorption energies for oxygenated species on PtNi alloys leads to increased activity, an instance of scaling relations for ORR catalysts. $ ^{23} $ This has been associated with the shifted d-band center position and surface atomic arrangement reported by Marković and co-workers. $ ^{22} $

In sum, the introduction of heteroatoms modulates the electronic structure of catalysts, enabling the enhancement of electrocatalytic activity. $ ^{2 2-2 5} $ . We explore herein how such a strategy can be employed to design catalysts exhibiting enhanced $ \mathrm{N O}_{3}^{-} \mathrm{R R} $ activity and selectivity.

We began by preparing a series of CuNi alloys with various Cu:Ni compositions, and observed a 0.12 V upshift in $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ , and a 0.2 V lower overpotential required for peak $ \mathrm{N H}{3} $ FE. This occurred at a composition - $ \mathrm{C u}{50} \mathrm{N i}{50} $ alloy catalysts - that simultaneously produced a 6-fold increase in $ \mathrm{N O}{3} ^{-} $ RR activity compared to the case of pure Cu at 0 V vs. RHE $ \mathrm{(p H=1 4)} $ . We utilized X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), operando X-ray adsorption spectroscopy (XAS) and ultra-violet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) to investigate the electronic structure of the catalysts and found that the Cu d-band center upshifted toward the Fermi level in CuNi alloys. In addition, we investigate the reaction pathway with DFT calculations, and build an intermediate adsorption energy- $ \mathrm{N O}{3} ^{-} $ RR performance relationship for the CuNi alloy system.

第 6 页

Results and discussion

Catalyst synthesis and characterization. We began by electrodepositing catalysts on both rotating disk electrodes (RDEs) and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) membranes covered with 300 nm thick Cu seed layers (300 nm Cu/PTFE). We used electron microscopy to investigate the morphological and crystalline structure of the catalysts. The CuNi catalysts with Cu-to-Ni ratios of 80:20, 50:50, and 30:70 in the deposition solutions, labeled $ \mathrm{C u_{80} N i_{20}} $ $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ , and $ \mathrm{C u_{30} N i_{70}} $ , exhibited dendritic morphologies (Fig. 1a, Fig. S1, and S2) with dendrite diameters in the range of 200-400 nm. Using high resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) we observed lattice spacings of 0.208 and 0.179 nm for the Cu(111) and Cu(200) facets (Fig. 1b) of the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalysts due to the formation of the CuNi alloy phase; compared to pure Cu dendrites exhibiting lattice spacings of 0.210 and 0.181 nm for Cu(111) and Cu(200) facets that agree with cubic Cu (Fig. 1d). $ ^{26} $ The Cu-to-Ni ratio in the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst, quantified by electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS, Fig. 1e-h), was ~52:48. We observed similar ratios at other randomly-selected positions (Fig. S3 and Table S1), arguing against a main role for catalyst heterogeneity in catalytic performance. XRD reveals a decrease in Cu lattice spacings when Ni is incorporated (Fig. 1i and S4). The Cu(111) and Cu(100) $ d $ -spacings of the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst revealed by XRD agrees with the results observed with HRTEM.

We also looked for evidence of changes in the electronic properties of the Cu and Cu:Ni catalysts. XPS indicated a notable decrease in the Cu2p binding energy and an increase in the metallic Ni2p binding energy for the alloyed catalysts (Fig. 1j and S5). Among the three alloyed catalysts, we found the largest Cu2p binding energy shift of $ \sim 0. 3 5 $ eV in $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ . This can be explained through electron redistribution $ ^{2 7} $ which leads to an opposite shift of the Cu3d band toward the Fermi level, $ ^{2 8} $ tuning the adsorption energy of both $ \mathrm{H^{}} $ and $ \mathrm{N O}_{3} $ $ ^{} $ .

In the case of the pure Ni catalysts, only the XRD peaks of the Cu/PTFE support were observed (Fig. 1i). XPS measurements exhibited a very similar Cu2p binding energy compared to pristine Cu/PTFE (Fig. S6). SEM, elemental mapping, and Ni2p XPS (Fig. S5 and 7) reveal only Ni, accompanied by $ \mathrm{N i O_{x}} $ formed by oxidation in air, fully covering the Cu/PTFE fibers.

NO $ {3} ^{-} $ RR activity and kinetics. To investigate the electrocatalytic activity and kinetics of $ \mathrm{N O{3}}^{-} $ reduction, we tested each catalyst on rotating disk electrodes (RDEs). With the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst, we found an onset potential of $ \sim0.25 $ V vs. RHE (pH=14) for $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ reduction (Fig. 2a). The current density then increased sharply to its transport-limited ceiling of $ \sim170 $ mA $ \mathrm{c m}^{-2} $ (according to equation 1 in Supporting information) at 100 rpm in 1 M KOH+100 mM $ \mathrm{K N O_{3}} $ (pH=14) electrolyte. On the pure Cu catalysts, we found that a much more negative cathodic potential was required to reach this same $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ -transport-limited current: the current density was only 100 mA $ \mathrm{c m}^{-2} $ at -0.2 V vs. RHE which was only 60% of that obtained by operating the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst at the same potential. The pure Ni catalyst is almost inactive for $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ reduction (Fig. S8a and b).

The current density, normalized to the electrochemically active surface area (ECSA), increases exponentially to $ \sim 1 $ mA $ \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ along with the cathodic potential for the case of $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ In contrast, an evident multi-electron transfer process (Fig. 2a and b) was seen on the pure Cu catalyst. $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ catalysts exhibited a 40% lower ECSA, determined by its double-layer capacitance, compared to pure Cu (Fig. S9a-c). This translated to a 6-fold increase in ECSA normalized current density for $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ , compared to pure Cu at 0 V vs. RHE (Fig. 2b). The intrinsic $ \mathrm{N O}_{3}^{-} $ RR activity was significantly improved using the CuNi alloy systems.

To gain insight into the kinetics, we plotted Koutecky- Levich (K-L) curves for $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ reduction on the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ and pure Cu catalysts (Fig. 2c) using their current density vs. potential (j-V) profiles in 1 M KOH+10 mM $ \mathrm{K N O_{3}} $ (pH=14) electrolyte (Fig. S10 and S8c).

K-L analysis revealed a four-electron-transfer process for $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ production on both the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ and Cu catalysts. The kinetic current density obtained using the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst was calculated from the intercept of the K-L plot and was 220 mA $ \mathrm{c m}^{-2} $ at -0.25 V vs. RHE, 2x higher than in the case of pure Cu controls (Table S2).

We investigated the $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ of $ \mathrm{N O}{3}^{-} $ RR on catalysts with different Cu:Ni ratios in 1 M KOH $ + 1 0 \mathrm{m M} \mathrm{K N O}{3} $ electrolyte. On $ \mathrm{C u}{3 0} \mathrm{N i}{7 0}, $ $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0}, $ $ \mathrm{C u}{8 0} \mathrm{N i}{2 0} $ alloys, we found increasing $ \mathrm{N O}{3}^{-} $ RR $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ compared to the pure Cu catalyst. For instance, at 100 rpm, $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ catalyst exhibited the highest $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ of 0.08 V vs. RHE among all catalysts, while an $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ of -0.045 V vs. RHE was seen in the case of pure Cu (Fig. S11). The improvement in $ \mathrm{E}{1/2} $ further increased to $ \sim 1 2 0 \mathrm{m V} $ when catalysts were tested in 100 mM $ \mathrm{K N O}{3} $ at the same rotating rate (Fig. 2a). For all $ \mathrm{N O}{3}^{-} $ concentrations, $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ catalysts perform better than the pure Cu as evidenced by the upshifting of $ \mathrm{E}_{1/2} $ and the reduced overpotential required for the same current density (Fig. 2d and S7d).

$ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} \mathrm{R R} $ selectivity. We investigated $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ selectivity using catalysts deposited on Cu/PTFE supports, e.g. the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ catalyst on PTFE (labeled $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50}} $ /PTFE), in a flow electrolyzer. $ ^{29} $ We quantified the $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ product concentration as a function of a range of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ concentrations using an indophenol blue method (Fig. S12). To confirm that the $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ produced indeed comes from $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ reduction, $ ^{15} \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ electroreduction was performed using the same catalyst (Fig. S13).

We achieved a 99 $ \pm 1 \% $ Faradaic efficiency (FE) for $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ at $ \sim-0.15 $ V vs. RHE on the $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50} / P T F E} $ catalyst in 1 M KOH+100 mM $ \mathrm{K N O_{3}} $ electrolyte (Fig. 2e and Table S3). The peak FE for $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ , on $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50} / P T F E} $ , shifted to a 50 mV lower overpotential compared to that of the pure $ \mathrm{C u / P T F E} $ (Fig. S14). The corresponding current density using $ \mathrm{C u_{50} N i_{50} / P T F E} $ was more than 1.3 times higher than that obtained using pure Cu controls (Table. S5). We checked

Electronic structure studies. To shed light on the electronic structure of the CuNi alloy catalysts under $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ RR conditions, we turned to operando hXAS. $ ^{30} $ The $ \mathrm{C u}{50} \mathrm{N i}{50} $ catalyst in 1 M KOH + 10 mM $ \mathrm{K N O_{3}} $ electrolyte at a series of applied potentials exhibited pure metallic features in both Ni and Cu K-edge spectra under steady-state operation conditions (Fig. 3a-d, Fig. S20). We then calculated the operando coordination numbers (CNs) of Ni at different potentials. The CNs of metal-metal bonds stayed above 11.5 at all potentials (Table S6 and 7). This result suggests that, in our work, there is unlikely to be a prominent role for subsurface oxygen species as previously reported in related catalysts under distinct electrochemical (acidic) conditions. $ ^{31} $

Since the adsorption energy of intermediates is strongly correlated with the d-band center position of catalysts, $ ^{32} $ we performed UPS studies for the pure Cu and the CuNi alloys (Fig. 3e). The pure Cu catalyst exhibited a d-band center location of -2.84 eV (E-E $ {F} $ , Fermi level) on the background-corrected spectrum. Increasing the Ni composition in the alloys causes an upshift of the d-band center towards the Fermi level by 0.14, 0.28 and 0.32 eV for $ \mathrm{C u}{8 0} \mathrm{N i}{2 0}, $ $ \mathrm{C u}{5 0} \mathrm{N i}{5 0} $ and $ \mathrm{C u}{3 0} \mathrm{N i}_{7 0}, $ respectively. These results were in agreement with the XPS results, wherein we observed the 2p electron redistribution which leads to a positive shift of the Cu3d band towards the Fermi level. This indicates decreasing anti-bonding occupation and stronger adsorbate bonding, $ ^{32} $ which means that alloying Ni with Cu greatly enhances the adsorption energies of intermediate species.

Taken together, the activity, selectivity and $ d $ -band center positions, allow us to reason that enhanced $ \mathrm{N O}_{3}^{-} $ RR intermediate adsorption, arising due to the shifted $ d $ -band center

DFT studies. We sought to investigate, using DFT, the relationship between intermediate adsorption and the $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R} $ activity of different CuNi catalysts (Fig. 4a). The stability of CuNi alloy systems were screened via doping Ni into Cu at various layers and distributions (Fig. S21, 22, and Table S8). Thermodynamics first force Ni atoms to replace the subsurface Cu when the Ni:Cu ratio is less than 1:1. The substitution takes place on Cu surfaces with a further increase in the ratio (Fig. S22).

The electrochemical reaction $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}+6 H_{2} O+8 e^{-}\rightarrow N H_{3}+9 O H^{-}} $ was represented by a series of deoxidation reactions: $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}\rightarrowN O_{2}\rightarrowN O\rightarrowN} $ followed by hydrogenation reactions of $ \mathrm{N\rightarrowN H\rightarrowN H_{2}\rightarrowN H_{3}} $ according to a previous report. $ ^{33} $ With the stable CuNi structures we built, we took different adsorbed orientations of intermediates on all possible active sites into account (Fig. S23 and 24). For all intermediates, the most stable adsorption configurations (Fig. 4a) with the lowest total energies were employed to illustrate the $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-} R R} $ pathway, and hence assess the activities of different catalysts.

A full $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ RR pathway in 1 M KOH $ \mathrm{(p H=1 4)} $ , including the deoxidation/hydrogenation reactions and intermediates, $ ^{3 3} $ was then calculated (Fig. 4a). On pure Cu, the first $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ adsorption step is the potential-dependent step (PDS) of which the maximum reaction free energy is 0.40 eV at -0.14 V vs. standard hydrogen electrode (SHE). Introducing Ni atoms moves the PDS from $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ adsorption to the hydrogenation of the $ ^{} \mathrm{N H_{2}} $ intermediate $ \left( ^{} \mathrm{N H_{2}}+\right. $ $ \mathrm{H_{2} O+e^{-}\rightarrow * \mathrm{N H_{3}+O H^{-}} $ , Figure 4b), as the result of enhanced adsorption caused by the upshifted $ d $ -band center (Fig. 3e). A volcano-type relationship between the $ ^{*} \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ adsorption

By increasing Ni concentration in CuNi alloys, a stronger adsorption of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ on the surface further modifies the energetics of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}}^{-} $ RR. However, on $ \mathrm{C u_{30} N i_{70}} $ alloy and pure Ni, the reaction free energy for $ \mathrm{N H_{2}} $ hydrogenation increases to -0.39 and -0.34 eV at -0.14 V vs. SHE, as high Ni fractions lead to $ \mathrm{N H_{2}} $ intermediate adsorptions exceeding the optimal values. Conclusions

This work presents the relationship between intermediate adsorption energies and $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR activity on CuNi catalysts. By replacing 50% Cu with Ni, we achieved significantly improved $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR-to- $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ performance. This includes a 0.12 V upshift in the half-wave potential, a 0.2 V lower overpotential required to achieve the optimal $ \mathrm{N H_{3}} $ FE, and a 6-fold increase in $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR activity on $ \mathrm{C u}{50} \mathrm{N i}{50} $ alloy catalysts compared to pure Cu at 0 V vs. RHE in alkaline conditions (pH=14). The electronic structure studies revealed an upshifting of the d-band center toward the Fermi level, a feature that enhances intermediate adsorption energies. This relationship was then validated by our DFT calculations, wherein we found that introducing Ni atoms moves the PDS from $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ adsorption to $ ^{*} \mathrm{N H_{2}} $ hydrogenation due to the enhanced adsorption energy of $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ on the CuNi surface, and as a result, lowers the overpotential. Our work demonstrates the effect of the d-band center positions and the induced adsorption properties on the $ \mathrm{N O_{3}^{-}} $ RR activity and selectivity.
