ABSTRACT: Aqueous Zn batteries are promising energy storage devices for large-scale energy-storage due to low cost and high energy density. However, their lifespan is limited by the water decomposition and Zn dendrite growth. Here, we suppress water reduction and Zn dendrite growth in dilute aqueous electrolyte by adding dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) into $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O} $ , in which DMSO replaces the $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ in $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ solvation sheath due to a higher Gutmann donor number (29.8) of DMSO than that (18) of $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ . The preferential solvation of DMSO with $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ and strong $ \mathrm{H_{2}O}-\mathrm{DMSO} $ interaction inhibit the decomposition of solvated $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ . In addition, the decomposition of solvated DMSO forms $ \mathrm{Z n}{1 2} \left( \mathrm{S O}{4} \right){3} \mathrm{C l}{3} \left( \mathrm{O H} \right){1 5}. $ $ 5 \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O}, $ $ \mathrm{Z n S O}{3} $ , and ZnS enriched-solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) preventing Zn dendrite and further suppressing water decomposition. The $ \mathrm{Z n C l{2}-H_{2}O}-\mathrm{DMSO} $ electrolyte enables Zn anodes in $ \mathrm{Z n} \parallel \mathrm{T i} $ half-cell to achieve a high average Coulombic efficiency of 99.5% for 400 cycles (400 h), and the $ \mathrm{Z n} \parallel \mathrm{M n O}_{2} $ full cell

with a low capacity ratio of $ \mathrm{Z n: M n O_{2}} $ at 2:1 to deliver a high energy density of 212 Wh/kg (based on both cathode and anode) and maitain 95.3% of the capacity over 500 cycles at 8 C.

INTRODUCTION

Metallic zinc (Zn) is an ideal anode material for aqueous batteries because of its high theoretical capacity (5851 mAh $ \mathrm{mL}^{-1} $ and 820 mA h $ \mathrm{g}^{-1} $ ), low redox potential $ (-0.76\mathrm{V} $ versus the standard hydrogen electrode), high abundance, water compatibility, and intrinsic safety. $ ^{1-6} $ However, the reaction of Zn anodes with water in aqueous electrolytes reduces Coulombic efficiency (CE), consumes water and Zn, and promotes Zn dendrite growth. $ ^{7-10} $

Parasitic water reduction during Zn deposition is accelerated by the high overpotential associated with strong Coulombic interactions between the solvated $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ion and its surrounding $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ shell. $ ^{9-11} $ The increase in local pH envirment due to $ \mathrm{H}{2} $ evolution promotes the formation of $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ -insulating passivate layer (such as Zn hydroxides) on the Zn surface, $ ^{12,13} $ reducing the Zn utilization and cycle life, and promoting Zn dendrite growth. $ ^{14,15} $ To suppress the water reduction and Zn dendrite, the bonding strength between $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ion and solvated $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ should be weakened, and a uniform Zn-ion conductive solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) has to be constructed, which allows $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ transport while preventing $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ from penetration to Zn anode surface. To weaken the bonding strength between $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ion and $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ , Wang et al. $ ^{7} $ developed a 21 m LiTFSI $ - \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O}-1 $ m $ \mathrm{Z n} ( \mathrm{T F S I} ){2} $ water-in-salt, where the support $ \mathrm{L i}^{+} $ strongly bonds with $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ , while $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ions are closely coordinated with $ \mathrm{T F S I}^{-} $ rather than $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ , thus significantly reducing the $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ activity in the electrolyte. Ji et al. $ ^{16} $ developed a 30 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2}-\mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O}-5 $ m LiCl water-in-salt, where the hydrogen bonding network of water is interrupted and

$ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ions are closely coordinated with $ \mathrm{C l}^{-} $ rather than $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ thus strenghening $ \mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H} $ covalent bonds and decreasing $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ activity in the electrolyte. Chen et al. $ ^{17} $ reported a 7.5 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2} $ electrolyte with interrupted hydrogen bonding network of water and decreased $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ activity at $ -70^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $ . Adding salt concentration can effectvely weaken the bonding between $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ and $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ , thus suppressing the water decomposition during Zn deposition. However, the high cost and high viscosity of the water-in-salt electrolytes remain unsolved. $ ^{18,19} $ Alternatively, the $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ reduction on Zn can also be prevented by forming a $ \mathrm{Z n F}{2}-\mathrm{Z n}{3} \left( \mathrm{P O}{4} \right){2} $ SEI through a hydrophobic organic electrolyte surface coating. $ ^{20} $ However, the complex surface coating of the organic layer electrolyte limits its large-scale application. It is highly desired to form SEI and reduce the $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ activity by simply adding additives into aqueous electrolytes. Liu and Pan et al. $ ^{21} $ added poly(ethylene oxide) polymer (PEO) as additive into $ \mathrm{Z n S O}{4} $ , where ether groups of PEO interact strongly with $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ions and PEO molecules adsorbed onto Zn anodes. However, $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ion transfer kinetics and power density are seriously reduced, although $ \mathrm{H}_{2} $ generation on Zn metal anode is suppressed. The electrolyte additive should satisfy three stringent requirements: (1) The solvent additive should have a

higher Gutmann donor number than $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ enabling it to replace the $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ in the $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ solvation sheath without seriously sacrificing $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ ion transfer kinetics. (2) It should be able to form a strong bond with $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ to reduce $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ activity and suppress $ \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} $ reduction. (3) It should be able to from a dense and self-repairable solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) to prevent water penetration onto Zn anode.

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) has high dielectric constant, high donor number (29.8), and low cost, $ ^{22} $ which can preferentially solvate with Zn ions and exclude water from $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ solvation sheath. Besides, DMSO also has a strong interaction to water, reducing the water activity. $ ^{23} $ DMSO has been added into alkaline, $ ^{24} $ organic, $ ^{25} $ and ionic liquid $ - \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ $ ^{26} $ Zn-ion electrolyte to suppress zinc dendrite formation. In alkaline zinc-air battery, $ ^{24} $ the DMSO inhibited the passivation of zinc surface enhancing the zinc dissolution/deposition. In organic $ ^{25} $ or ionic liquid- $ \mathrm{H}{2}\mathrm{O} $ $ ^{26} $ zinc batteries, the DMSO also enhanced the reversibility of Zn plating/stripping. However, the Zn dendrite suppression mechanism of DMSO in these electrolytes remains unexplored and DMSO has not been added into neutral aqueous electrolytes, which are the most promising electrolytes for aqueous Zn batteries.

Here, we added dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) additive into a dilute aqueous 1.6 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O} $ (mole ratio of $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}:H_{2} O}= $ 1:34) electrolyte forming a 1.3 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}/H_{2}O}-\mathrm{D M S O} $ (volume ratio of $ \mathrm{H_{2}O}/\mathrm{D M S O}=4.3:1 $ ) electrolyte. Since the donor number (29.8) of DMSO is much higher than that (18) of $ \mathrm{H_{2}O}^{22} $ and preferential conscription of cosolvents in the structure of the solvation sheath of Li ions, $ ^{10} $ the $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ solvated $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ sheath was altered by preferential solvation of DMSO (Figure 1). current in 1.3 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O}-\mathrm{D M S O} $ electrolyte than that in 1.6 m $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O} $ during the linear cathodic potential scan of an inert titanium electrode after the activation process (Figure S1). At $ -0.02 $ V vs $ \mathrm{Z n / Z n^{2+}} $ , the reduction current reduced from $ -16 \mu\mathrm{A} $ $ \left( \mathrm{Z n+H_{2}} \right) $ to $ -13 \mu\mathrm{A} $ $ \left( \mathrm{Z n} $ plating), as confirmed by almost 100% Coulombic efficiency of Zn plating/stripping in $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O}-\mathrm{D M S O} $ due to the formation of $ \mathrm{Z n_{1 2} \left( S O_{4} \right){3} C l{3} \left( O H \right){1 5}} \cdot 5 \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O}-\mathrm{Z n S O_{3}}-\mathrm{Z n S} $ SEI. DMSO: $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ molar ratio of 1:17, the $ ^{67} \mathrm{Zn} $ chemical shift of $ \mathrm{ZnCl_{2}-H_{2}O}-\mathrm{DMSO} $ (106 ppm) is significantly larger than that of $ \mathrm{ZnCl_{2}-H_{2}O} $ electrolyte (83 ppm) (Figure 2c), indicating the deshielding effect of the solvation sheath on $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $ ions. This is because that DMSO is involved in solvation sheath to replace some $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ due to the higher donor number of DMSO (29.8) than that of $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ $ (18)^{27} $ and lower electron density of O in DMSO than that in $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ (Figure S2). This result is consistent with the increased $ \mathrm{Zn}-\mathrm{O} $ bond length and decreased $ \mathrm{Zn}-\mathrm{H_{2}O} $ bonding strength mentioned previously.

The involvement of DMSO in the solvation structure of $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ was also confirmed by the high-resolution mass spectra (HRMS) of $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O}-\mathrm{D M S O} $ electrolyte. As displayed in Figure 2d, distinct signals of various DMSO-containing $ [\mathrm{Z n} (\mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O}){4} (\mathrm{O H}){3} (\mathrm{D M S O})]^{-} $ complexes at $ m/z=2 6 5 $ , 267, 269 are detected. The peak position and intensity differences of $ m/z $ for the same chemical structure are caused by the isotope pattern of Zn, including 48.6% abundance of $ \mathrm{Z n} (6 4) $ in nature, 27.9% abundance of $ \mathrm{Z n} (6 6) $ , and 18.8% abundance of $ \mathrm{Z n} (6 8) $ . It should be noted that the test result is from a dilute solution in which $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ to the $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2} O}-\mathrm{D M S O} $ electrolyte volume ratio is $ 10^{1 0}:1 $ . The strong persistence of DMSO in the $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ solvation shell from such a dilute solution further confirms the preferential solvation effect of DMSO with $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ compared to that of $ \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}. $

The strong interaction between DMSO and $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ without interference of $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ was confirmed using FTIR and Raman. Figure S3 indicates that the DMSO solvent displays unique stretching modes of the sulfoxide (S=O) group at around $ 1 0 5 0 \mathrm{c m}^{-1} $ . In the DMSO $ - \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ mixture where the DMSO: $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ molar ratio is 1:17, the spectral band (red line) shifts to lower wavenumbers (red shift), compared with that in pure DMSO (blue line). The red shift is attributed to the interaction between H of the O-H bond in water molecules and O of the S=O double bond in DMSO (S=O...H-O). Strong interaction between DMSO and $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ was also confirmed by the difference in thermal stabilities of $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2}- $ $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O}- $ DMSO and $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2}- \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ electrolytes. As shown in thermal gravitimetric analysis (TGA) curves (Figure S4), the addition of DMSO into $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2}- \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ effectively suppresses the $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ evaporation by increasing the evaporation ending temperature (EET) from 67 to $ 9 5 \mathrm{~^\circ C} $ , due to strong interaction between DMSO and $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ . This interaction contributes to the decreased interaction between $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ and $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ . In addition, the strong interaction between DMSO and $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ slightly decreases the conductivity to 42.1 from 70.8 mS $ \mathrm{cm}^{-1} $ of the $ \mathrm{Z n C l}{2}- \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ electrolyte. In summary, DMSO replaces $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ in $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ solvation sheath, weakening the bonding between $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ and $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ . DMSO itself also strongly bonds to $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ , reducing the $ \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ activity. The synthetic impacts of DMSO promote the DMSO reduction but suppress $ \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} $ reduction, forming a robust SEI.

SEI on Zn Anode. The SEI composition on the Zn anode was analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) with an $ \mathrm{A r^{+}} $ sputtering depth profiling characterization. XRD (Figure 5a) and optical image analysis (Figure S11) of cycled Zn anodes in $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2}O} $ electrolyte demonstrated that random platelet-shape $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ -insulating $ \mathrm{Z n}{5}(\mathrm{O H}){8} \mathrm{C l}{2} \cdot \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ byproducts were formed on the cycled Zn electrode surface. However, $ \mathrm{Z n}^{2+} $ conductive SEI layer was formed on Zn that cycled in $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2}O}- $ DMSO electrolyte as evidenced by well-identified characteristic peak of the $ \mathrm{Z n}{1 2} \left( \mathrm{S O}{4} \right){3} \mathrm{C l}{3} \left( \mathrm{O H} \right){1 5} \cdot 5 \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O} $ and $ \mathrm{Z n}{5} \left( \mathrm{O H} \right){6} \left( \mathrm{C O}{3} \right){2} $ crystals in XRD and smooth surface in optical image (Figure S11). CONCLUSIONS

The strong $ \mathrm{H_{2}O}-\mathrm{DMSO} $ interaction decreases the activity of solvated water and suppresses the decomposition of solvated $ \mathrm{H_{2}O}. $ In addition, DMSO replaces solvated $ \mathrm{H_{2}O} $ in the solvation structure of $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $ due to a high Gutmann donor number of DMSO, resulting in preferential reduction of solvated DMSO to in situ form a dense and self-repaired $ \mathrm{Zn}{1 2} \left( \mathrm{S O}{4} \right){3} \mathrm{C l}{3} \left( \mathrm{O H} \right){1 5} \cdot 5 \mathrm{H}{2} \mathrm{O}-\mathrm{Z n S O}{3}-\mathrm{Z n S} $ SEI on Zn anodes. The SEI allows $ \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} $ transport but blocks $ \mathrm{H{2}O} $ penetration, which further inhibits the water reduction and suppresses Zn dendrite growth. Addition of multifunctional DMSO into $ \mathrm{Z n C l_{2}-H_{2}O} $ electrolytes enhances Zn plating/stripping CE to $ \sim 1 00 \% $ enabling $ \mathrm{Z n l l M n O_{2}} $ cells with a low capacity ratio of 2:1 to achieve a capacity retention of 95.3% of the initial capacity after 500 cycles.
