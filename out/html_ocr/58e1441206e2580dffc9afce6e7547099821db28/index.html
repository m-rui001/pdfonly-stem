The electrocatalytic carbon dioxide reduction reaction $ \mathrm{(CO_{2} R R)} $ addresses the need for storage of renewable energy in valuable carbon-based fuels and feedstocks, yet challenges remain in the improvement of electrosynthesis pathways for highly selective hydrocarbon production. To improve catalysis further, it is of increasing interest to lever synergies between heterogeneous and homogeneous approaches. Molecules adjacent to the active site provide additional binding interactions that may tune the stability of intermediates, improving catalytic performance by increasing Faradaic efficiency (product selectivity), and also decreasing overpotential. We offer a forward-looking perspective on molecularlyenhanced heterogeneous catalysis for $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R} $ . We discuss four categories of molecularlyenhanced strategies: molecular-additive-modified heterogeneous catalysts, immobilized organometallic complex catalysts, reticular catalysts, and metal-free polymer catalysts. We introduce present-day challenges in molecular strategies and describe a vision for $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysis toward multi-carbon products. These strategies provide potential avenues to address the challenges of catalyst activity, selectivity, and stability in the further development of $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R} $ .

The electrochemical carbon dioxide $ \mathrm{(CO_{2})} $ reduction reaction $ \mathrm{(CO_{2}RR)} $ to fuels and chemical feedstocks, powered by renewable electricity, offers a pathway to long-term seasonal energy storage. $ ^{1,2} $ In $ \mathrm{CO_{2}RR} $ , electrocatalysts promote the conversion of $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ into value-added products such as carbon monoxide (CO), formate (HCOO-) or formic acid (HCOOH), methane $ \mathrm{(CH_{4})} $ ethylene $ \mathrm{(C_{2}H_{4})} $ , and ethanol $ \mathrm{(C_{2}H_{5}OH)} $ . Today, metal-based heterogeneous catalysts commonly exhibit high activities for $ \mathrm{CO_{2}RR} $ at low overpotentials. $ ^{1,3} $ Nevertheless, key metrics - such as product selectivity, current density as a function of applied bias, the requirement of high $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $

第 4 页

concentration in the source stream, and catalyst stability are among the factors that need to be improved in the context of renewable energy applications.

In contrast to many heterogeneous catalysts, molecular catalysts offer the advantage of synthetic control over the steric and electronic properties in the vicinity of the active site. This in turn enables mechanistic studies to address structure-function relationships that then induce rational design of new catalyst structures toward improved performance. Homogeneous catalysts have been used in the context of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ for decades.4 These systems have shown high selectivity, with near unity product Faradaic efficiency (FE), for CO or HCOO- (HCOOH), and progress toward more reduced products such as $ \mathrm{C H_{4}} $ is emerging.5

There has been long-standing interest in the heterogeneous catalysis community to bridge concepts from homogeneous molecular catalysis to tune heterogeneous catalysts.6 New product profiles can emerge when molecule-like catalysts are interfaced with solid supports.7 An exciting example of this bridge is metalloenzymes, which are able to mediate challenging multi-electron/proton reductive transformations at remarkably fast rates with very little applied bias.8 This is a consequence of metalloenzyme structure, which has exploited multi-metallic active sites with ideally positioned secondary sphere interactions.9 The catalysis research community is interested in defining and employing such design principles in artificial catalyst architectures.10 Transferring such principles to molecularly-enhanced heterogeneous catalysts represents an exciting opportunity for researchers. Introducing molecular approaches to heterogeneous $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R} $ catalysts may introduces new degrees of freedom that enable routes to enhance/alter product selectivity, increase catalyst mass activity, and to lower the overpotential to drive a reaction at a desired rate. We use the term molecular approaches to refer to chemically-modified electrodes those that display motifs derived from molecular chemistry. We also note that controlling

第 5 页

molecules in the electrolyte (water, cations, and buffer species) is another critical dimension in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ but it is a topic well-addressed elsewhere. $ ^{1,11} $

This perspective discusses how molecular structures can be adapted to $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrode materials. Four main strategies are described: molecular-additive-modified heterogeneous catalysts, immobilized organometallic complex catalysts, metal-organic framework (MOF) and covalent organic framework (COF) catalysts, and metal-free polymer catalysts (Fig.1). Molecule-based approaches to improved heterogenous catalysis have been discussed previously. $ ^{6,12,13} $ This perspective seeks to go beyond conceptualizing and classifying these ideas. Instead, it explores the extent to which the community has developed mechanistic pictures of why certain molecularlyenhanced heterogeneous catalysts have improved performance; and from there we speculate regarding new directions that could potentially result in further improvements in molecularlyenhanced heterogeneous $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . Molecule type and the electrode structure determine the role of molecules in each approach (Fig.2); examples are described for each strategy and are summarized in Table 1.

Materials for molecular-enhanced $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $

Molecular-additive-modified heterogeneous catalysts. Electrochemical $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ is complex, with multiple proton-coupled electron transfer reactions depending on the nature of the product. It has been proposed that the overpotential is determined by the binding energies of intermediates associated with each of the multiple reaction steps along the pathway (Fig. 3). $ ^{14} $ To move in the direction of programming reaction outcome, it is important to understand the relationship between the intermediates in proposed reaction pathways. $ ^{15-18} $ Lowering overpotentials is difficult because

第 6 页

the binding energy of one intermediate is typically correlated to others through thermodynamic scaling relations. Scaling relations are established by d-band theory, originating from the interaction between adsorbed intermediate and metal d-states. $ ^{12} $ For decreased overpotential and enhanced product selectivity of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ , strategies are required to break scaling relations and control the direction of reaction pathways toward a specific product.

It has been proposed that organic additives or coatings, such as cysteamine, $ ^{19} $ thiols, $ ^{19,20} $ amine, $ ^{20,21} $ polypyrrole, $ ^{22} $ N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs), $ ^{23} $ 4-pyridylethylmercaptan (4-PEM), $ ^{24} $ glycine, $ ^{25} $ and N-substituted tetrahydro-bipyridine, $ ^{26,27} $ may control the binding energy of $ \mathrm{CO_{2}RR} $ intermediates such as COOH, CO, and *HCOO (Fig. 2a). These reports suggest that tuning the organics to impart desired selectivities is possible. Further studies addressing structure-function correlations are needed for improved control and for accessing new reaction products.

Molecular-additive-modified heterogeneous catalysts have been investigated for the $ \mathrm{C O_{2}-} $ to CO electroconversion, where the reaction pathway is affected by altering the binding energies of $ \mathrm{C O O H} $ and $ \mathrm{C O} $ intermediates (Fig. 3a) - both are supposed to be critical intermediates along the CO pathway. For instance, it has been proposed, with support from density functional theory (DFT) studies, that anchoring cysteamine molecules to Ag nanoparticles (NPs) leads to an increase of the unpaired electron localization at the surface of the NPs. $ ^{19} $ This is believed to result in preferential stabilization of the $ \mathrm{C O O H} $ intermediate at the Ag surface, while the $ \mathrm{C O} $ binding energy is proposed to be less affected. $ ^{19} $ Similar strategy was further applied to Ag NPs modified by amine-capped cysteamine. $ ^{20} $

The coating of molecules on heterogeneous catalysts has also been shown to tune $ \mathrm{C_{1}} $ product selectivity via control over the binding energy of $ ^{} $ COOH and $ ^{} $ HCOO intermediates, which are

第 7 页

proposed by DFT calculations to steer reaction pathways toward CO and HCOO- (HCOOH), respectively (Fig. 3a) $ ^{.23,24} $ For example, it has been suggested that the chelating NHCs ligands bound to a Pd electrode can make an electron-rich Pd surface via the strong $ \sigma $ -donation from the NHCs ligands to Pd, favouring the formation of HCOO- product (Fig. 3a) $ ^{.23} $ In another approach, it was proposed that - by matching the pKa (proton donating ability) with $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ product selectivity - self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) of 4-PEM can facilitate proton transfer from the ligand to $ \mathrm{C O_{2}.24} $

Beyond $ C_{1} $ products, organic functionalization of metal catalysts has also been employed to tune selectivities toward multicarbon products. For $ C_{\geq 2} $ products, lowering the activation energy of CO-CO dimerization is proposed to be necessary to switch $ C_{1} $ pathways to $ C_{2} $ pathways (Fig. 3b). One demonstration of this tuning is the modification of Cu surfaces with an electrodeposited tetrahydro-bipyridine film generated from the reductive coupling of N-arylpyridinium salts, which lowers the partial current densities for $ \mathrm{H}{2} $ and $ \mathrm{CH}{4} $ , and increases selectivity for $ C_{\geq 2} $ products.26 The modification of Cu surface with glycine is reported also to enhance $ C_{2} $ hydrocarbon production compared to the case of bare Cu (Table 1).25

Surface modification of electrodes with organic compounds is a simple, inexpensive, and versatile approach to tune the selectivity profile of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . Acquiring an increasingly detailed understanding of the interactions with the electrode and $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ substrate/intermediates is especially important. In particular for Cu, several theoretical reports suggest that tuning the adsorption energy of CCH and/or CHCHOH intermediates allows control over selectivity between $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{4}} $ and $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{5} O H} $ (Fig. 3b). The added degree of freedom by organic modification of Cu electrodes can potentially be impactful to tune $ \mathrm{C_{2}} $ product selectivities. We envisage, beyond the above theoretical proposals or rationalizations, that the role of the molecules needs to be

第 8 页

investigated further experimentally, such as by detecting $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ intermediates using insitu/operando spectroscopies such as Raman, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and surface enhanced infrared absorption spectroscopy (SEIRAS). Recently, the effect of N-arylpyridinium derived molecular-additive layer on the Cu electrode has been investigated by using SEIRAS during $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R}. $ 28 It has been revealed that molecular-additive layer can control the pH gradient or block the active sites according to the chemical structure of N-arylpyridinium group. This approach will provide more insight to the research community.

Immobilized organometallic complex catalysts. Organometallic complexes, whose design is inspired by metalloenzyme active sites, such as those in CO dehydrogenase (CODH), feature a metal center supported by a ligand framework that tunes the reactivity of the metal by affecting its local environment. $ ^{29} $ The ligand and metal center affect the complex's catalytic properties by the tuning of molecular geometry, electronic states, and intermediate binding, as well as the second coordination sphere interactions, such as hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions. $ ^{30,31} $ Immobilization strategies have been investigated for a wide range of metal complexes, including ligand platforms such as porphyrins, $ ^{32} $ bipyridines, $ ^{33,34} $ cyclams, $ ^{35} $ protoporphyrin, $ ^{36} $ and phthalocyanine $ ^{37} $ (Fig. 2b).

Porphyrin- and phthalocyanine- based organometallic complexes have been studied as effective homogeneous catalysts for the $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ in organic media. $ ^{4} $ The immobilization of these molecular catalysts enables the $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ in aqueous media. For instance, Co phthalocyanine (CoPc) complex + carbon nanotubes (CNTs) hybrid material performed the selective electroreduction of $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ to CO with FE over 90% in aqueous potassium bicarbonate $ (\mathrm{KHCO}_{3}) $ solution, showing a turnover frequency (TOF) of 2.7 s $ ^{-1} $ and a 10 h stability. $ ^{37} $ Interestingly, beyond CO production,

第 9 页

the immobilized Co protoporphyrin systems are also able to produce trace amount of more reduced products such as $ \mathrm{C H_{4}} $ at lower pH. $ ^{36} $

With the aim of obtaining more reduced products, researchers have also explored Cu-based coordination compounds. For instance, when a Cu phthalocyanine (CuPc) complex was supported on multi-walled CNTs, the resulting hybrid material displayed a FE of 66% for the production of $ \mathrm{CH_{4}} $ in aqueous electrolyte. $ ^{35} $ In-situ X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) studies suggest that the CuPc undergoes reversible structural and redox changes to form small, metallic copper particles with a size of $ \sim2 $ nm. The molecular species appears to serve as a precursor to form $ \mathrm{Cu}(0) $ that is the presumed active catalyst for $ \mathrm{CO_{2}RR} $

Organometallic complexes are usually immobilized onto support materials (e.g. graphite, graphene, and CNTs) via interactions such as covalent bonding, $ \pi-\pi $ stacking interactions, electropolymerization, and grafting. $ ^{13,33} $ In these hybrid systems, the electrochemical and catalytic behaviors of the immobilized molecules resemble that of the corresponding homogeneous species, though some shifts of redox processes are often observed. The immobilized complexes act as the redox-active centers to mediate the activation of substrate (e.g. $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ ) via its own oxidation or reduction processes. This behavior is likely due to the weak electronic communications between the immobilized molecules and the support, resulting in a tunneling barrier to electron transfers in these systems.

In contrast, the Surendranath group has recently reported molecular catalysts electronically linked to a graphitic electrode through conjugated aromatic pyrazine linkages. The authors found distinct electron transfer behaviors compared those observed in weakly-immobilized systems.38,39 Unlike homogeneous molecular catalysts with stepwise electron transfer and substrate activation, this graphite conjugated catalyst (GCC) induces concerted electron transfer and substrate

第 10 页

activation.38 The conductive pyrazine linkages also provide strong electronic coupling that minimize the potential drop between the electrode and the metal complex.

The metal active site in GCCs exists inside the electrical double layer. As the Fermi energy level of electrode $ \left( \mathrm{E_{F}} \right) $ is shifted, the redox potential of the metal active center is shifted together and there is no change in the driving force for electron tansfer between graphite and metallic active center in GCCs. No outer-sphere electron transfer or reduction of metal active center is observed in GCCs. $ ^{38,39} $ A potential drop between the GCC site and the solution was found, which induces ion transfers coupled with electron transfers between them. $ ^{38} $ The applied potential modulates the driving force for substrate activation. $ ^{39,40} $

The electrolyte environment also has an effect on the catalytic behaviors of the immobilized molecular catalysts. In $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ using bicarbonate $ (\mathrm{H C O}{3-}) $ electrolyte, there exists an equilibrium of $ \mathrm{C O{2}} $ (aq) $ + \mathrm{H_{2}O} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{H^{+}} $ (aq) $ + \mathrm{H C O}{3-} $ (aq).13 It has been reported that the $ \mathrm{C O{2} R R} $ using organometallic complexes with weak electronic coupling, such as CoPc on CNT, relies on both electron transfer and an ion-electron transfer step.13,41 In low $ [\mathrm{H C O}{3-}] $ , electron transfer induces reduction from Co(II) to Co(I) and COO- adsorption at the Co center. In high $ [\mathrm{H C O}{3-}] $ , coupled ion-electron transfer becomes rate-determining step, and $ \mathrm{H C O}{3-} $ acts as a proton donor. $ \mathrm{C O{2} R R} $ of GCCs is expected to proceed with coupled ion-electron transfer for substrate activation. It has been reported that strong electronic coupling enhances TOF compared to weak electronic coupling.40

An emerging class of materials receiving significant attention is single-atom catalysts (SACs). N-doped conducting carbon materials can provide a matrix for SACs where discrete metal centers are embedded within the N ligand field, mimicking the local environment of metallic centers in organometallic complexes. $ ^{42} $ SACs have proven to be successful materials to perform $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ at

第 11 页

lower overpotentials with high efficiencies for $ C_{1} $ products. An example of DFT modelling has proposed that N-coordinated active metal center promotes $ ^{*} $ COOH binding leading to a high selectivity for $ C_{1} $ products. $ ^{43} $ The pyrolysis of MOF materials has also been employed for the fabrication of SACs, which has allowed for a fine control of the spatial separation among metal atomic sites with N coordination. $ ^{44} $ In SACs, the metal atomic center is directly incorporated within the carbon support, and these are expected to be close to the case of strong electronic coupling. Investigating the oxidation states of metal active center by operando XAS will be helpful to understand the electronic coupling in SACs. $ ^{45} $ Further research on $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ mechanistics in the context of SACs is required.

Related to small molecule heterogenization, the immobilization of enzymatic catalysts represents a related emerging strategy for electrochemical $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . The potential for high selectivity and specificity for complex products at low applied bias is an important advantage of biocatalysts.46 Increasing the $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ selectivity and activity for highly reduced products, accessing alternative reduction products, and increasing the stability of the protein under electrochemical conditions and electrolyte pH, are current challenges in this area.

Reticular chemistry-based MOFs and COFs catalysts. Reticular chemistry provides access to three-dimensional (3D) porous materials that can immobilize molecular catalysts at well-defined positions. MOFs (organic linkers and metal node) and COFs (covalently-connected organic molecules) exhibit properties such as high surface area, tunable porosity, diversity in metal and functional groups, confinement effects, and periodic metal arrangements. $ ^{47} $ These properties make MOFs and COFs attractive for use as catalysts. Reticular systems have several properties suitable for their use as catalysts in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R}. $ $ ^{47,48} $ Coordinatively unsaturated metal sites, functionalized organic linkers, and periodic defects may all serve as active sites. $ ^{49} $ Certain MOFs and COFs can

第 12 页

be integrated without immobilization anchoring materials. They promote the uniform distribution of the metal active center without agglomeration (Fig. 2c). Because of their inherent pore confinement properties, these can produce local $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ concentration enhancement and this may allow for $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ RR catalysis in dilute $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ environments.

Porphyrin-based MOFs and COFs have been widely investigated as $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysts for $ \mathrm{C_{1}} $ products. $ ^{48,50} $ Co porphyrin-based MOFs $ (\mathrm{A l}{2}(\mathrm{O H}){2} \mathrm{T C P P}-\mathrm{C o}) $ exhibit high CO selectivity and activity, in which Co(I), formed by the reduction of Co(II) during $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ , is supposed to be the formal catalytic active site. $ ^{48} $ In an other example, Co porphyrin-based COFs exhibit FE values up to 90% for CO with a TOF of 9,400 h $ ^{-1.50} $ By exploiting a layer-by-layer MOF synthesis, two dimensional (2D) MOF thin films on substrates have been studied, and reticular chemistry used to control the preferred orientation of the MOF. $ ^{51,52} $

Recently, it was revealed that undercoordinated Cu sites in HKUST-1 could be afforded by detaching the carboxylate moieties. According to the degree of thermal calcination at a specific temperature, the degree of Cu dimer distortion toward an asymmetric structure was controlled. This Cu dimer control in the secondary building unit induced the formation of coordinate structure-controlled Cu clusters. Distortion of the Cu dimer resulted in the formation of Cu clusters with lower Cu-Cu coordination numbers, and an overall catalyst material that exhibited a $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{4}} $ FE of 45%.53

Despite these initial promising results, research on MOFs and COFs $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysts is still nascent and currently shows relatively lower current density than conventional metal heterogeneous catalysts. A major challenge for MOFs is their low electrical conductivity, which limits the ability for charges to be carried to the active sites within the periodic structure. Continued efforts to enhance electrical conductivity, robustness under high pH electrolyte, durability at

第 13 页

negative reduction potential, generation of $ \mathrm{C} \geq_{2} $ products, and substrate interfacing are needed for MOFs and COFs catalysts to rival the performance of state-of-the-art heterogeneous electrocatalysts. As efforts to address the limitations presented above continue, MOFs and COFs materials will be an attractive platform for the next generation of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysts.47

Polymer-based metal-free catalysts. Metal-free conjugated and conductive polymers have been used directly as $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ RR electrocatalysts. 54-56 Semiconducting polymers, such as polypyrrole (62% for $ \mathrm{C H_{3} C O O H} $ , 41% for HCOOH, and 2% HCHO) and polyaniline (78% for $ \mathrm{C H_{3} C O O H} $ and 12% for HCOOH), were applied as heterogeneous electrocatalysts using a $ \mathrm{C H_{3} O H / L i C l O_{4} / H^{+} / H_{2} O} $ electrolyte. 55,56 It was proposed that $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ adsorption occurs via hydrogen bonding interactions. Polydopamine (PDA), which contains significantly more hydrogen bonding sites per repeating unit, has shown selectivity for $ \mathrm{C_{1}} $ products (Fig. 2d). 54 It was proposed that these conductive polymers activate $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ sequentially at the carbonyl and amide groups. This catalyst exhibited CO FE over 80% and showed stability over 16 h in non-aqueous electrolytes. 54 In another example, the amine moieties of polyethylenimine was proposed to stabilize $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ RR intermediates via hydrogen bonding interactions and therefore promote $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ -to-HCOO- conversion on N-doped CNT (Fig. 2d). 57 Adsorbed $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ is reduced to $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ - at the N site, and stabilized via hydrogen bonding in polyethylenimine. It has been proposed that bonded $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ - is protonated and forms $ \mathrm{H C O O^{-}} $ . 57.

Graphene and CNTs have been actively studied as different types of metal-free carbon-based $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysts. Studies related to catalytic activity as a function of N-doping (pyridinic, graphitic, pyrrolic N) and B-doping have been carried out, and most have shown the production of CO and HCOO- to be favored (graphitic N-doped CNT: 90% for $ \mathrm{C O_{5 8}} $ , N-doped graphene: 73% for $ \mathrm{H C O O_{-5 9}} $ ). It has been reported that N-doped graphene quantum dots, which contain a higher

第 14 页

density of pyridinic N-edge sites, exhibited 31% $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{4}} $ FE.60 DFT calculations point to the importance of N-doped edges in converting $ \mathrm{CO_{2}} $ into $ \mathrm{C_{2}} $ products.61 Research in carbon-based materials should be more than cautious, because trace metals such as Ni, Fe, Mn, and Cu can be present as impurities in carbon-based materials such as graphite, graphene oxide, and CNTs. Specifically, Cu in graphene oxide has been reported to promote $ \mathrm{CH_{4}} $ formation in $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R}. $ 62 Furthermore, by taking advantage of the reduction of dissolved Cu ions in the electrolyte, $ \mathrm{CH_{4}} $ production can be optimized.62 This reveals that trace metals, even at low concentrations, can be effective in tuning the $ \mathrm{CO_{2} R R} $ activity of carbon-based materials.

Metal-free catalysts for $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ are appealing given the low-cost of such materials and the potential diversity of structure and reactivity available. However, challenges include the synthesis of well-defined, functionalized polymers suitable for structure-function studies to gain insight into the mechanism(s) of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . Features that would be of interest to explore in this context are the molecular weight, the dispersity, and the monomer content of the polymer. $ ^{63} $ This could be achieved, for example, by targeting bifunctional copolymers made of semiconducting blocks for electron delivery and blocks designed to activate $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ through weak secondary interactions.

Future directions and strategies

Here we turn to new approaches that rely on the molecular enhancement of heterogeneous catalysts to address challenges in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . Activity amplification and product selectivity increase are essential for practical applications. A significant aspect in the implementation of these molecular approaches is the need for systematic structure-function studies that provide insight into the nature of the active species and mechanism(s) of catalysis. These studies are essential for the rational improvement of the catalyst systems.

Second coordination sphere interactions. With inspiration from enzyme active sites, control of interactions of intermediates with groups in close proximity can increase activity and selectivity for $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R}. $ 64 Secondary coordination sphere interactions with motifs such as pendant amine donors, 65 hydrogenase mimics, 66 and hangman porphyrins, 67 have received attention for electrocatalyst. Designing pendant groups that can engage as hydrogen bonding and Lewis acid interactions is expected to have significant effects on catalysis (Fig. 4a). Lewis acids can help to shift the redox potential for $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ by promoting $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ binding and activation. 9 In Fig. 4b, solid-state structure (left, Protein data bank (PDB): 4UDX) and schematic representation (right) of the $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ -bound NiFe-CODH active site explain the role of different interactions in $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ activation: redox active metal, Lewis acidic metal, and hydrogen bonding with functionalized pendant groups. 64 The correct positioning of groups that can interact with intermediates in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ relative to the electrode surface is a significant challenge for this approach, but will be instrumental for reactivity.

Tandem catalysis. Coupling of multiple catalysts that perform successive steps in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ has been employed in molecular systems. $ ^{68} $ This approach can be used to perform $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ to CO at more positive potentials with a molecular catalyst, followed by subsequent reduction of CO to multicarbon products on a Cu electrode. Overall, such an approach provides access to a system that performs the reduction of $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ to $ \mathrm{C} \geq 2 $ species with lower overpotentials. $ ^{69} $ Alternatively, a molecular catalyst could be employed to perform orthogonal chemistry (Fig. 4c). For example, the catalytic dimerization or oligomerization of $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{4}} $ could result in a $ \mathrm{C_{4}} $ $ \mathrm{C_{6}} $ , or a statistical distribution of $ \mathrm{C_{2 n}} $ olefins, depending on the type of catalyst employed (Fig. 4d). $ ^{70} $ A Ni-based MOF has been applied for carbon upgrading from $ \mathrm{C_{2} H_{4}} $ to 1-butene $ (\mathrm{C_{4} H_{8}}) $ $ ^{71} $ Access to catalysts

for tandem chemistry that are compatible with electrochemistry on Cu may represent a significant opportunity.

Control of proton inventory. The chemistry of $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ in aqueous systems has the additional complication of non-redox reactivity due to the pH-dependent equilibrium among dissolved $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ $ \mathrm{H C O_{3-}} $ , and carbonate $ (\mathrm{C O_{32-}}) $ . Although a neutral pH electrolyte is advantageous to avoid carbonate formation, $ \mathrm{C} \geq 2 $ production on Cu electrodes is favored in micro-environments in which the pH is high $ ^{72} $ ; limits the concentration of protons at the electrode and translates to more C-C coupling chemistry. Therefore, in addition to electron transfer, the important aspect of controlling the selectivity profile of $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ RR is the proton transfer (Fig. 4e). $ ^{73,74} $ For example, DFT calculations have offered an explanation of pH in determining $ \mathrm{C_{1}} $ and $ \mathrm{C_{2}} $ product pathways at the well-defined Cu (111) surface (Fig. 4f). $ ^{72} $ In real catalysts, the electrode surface structure, as well as mass transport, will affect $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ RR, and computational insight needs to be accompanied by further experiment investigations. This approach has already been proven to be effective for controlling proton transfer in the oxygen reduction reaction with supported molecular Cu electrocatalysts with a hybrid bilayer membrane. $ ^{75} $ Analogously, a membrane-like layer generated by molecular additives on Cu could control the flow of protons and thereby improve $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ RR selectivity for $ \mathrm{C} \geq 2 $ products. A challenge here will be the development of appropriate preparation methodotologies to avoid blocking of active sites on the Cu catalyst.

shown to enhance $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ toward $ \mathrm{C}{\geq 2} $ products, while tert-butyl substitution increases $ \mathrm{H}{2} $ generation and decreases $ \mathrm{C}{\geq 2} $ products.26 This shows that the steric profile of the pyridinium additive can affect the $ \mathrm{C O{2} R R} $ product reaction pathway.26 Proposed mechanisms for the effect on selectivity include selective inhibition of sites primarily responsible for $ \mathrm{C H_{4}} $ formation, the activation of sites responsible for $ \mathrm{C}{\geq 2} $ formation, and inhibition of proton transfer (via a pH gradient). The development of a range of additives that result in low $ \mathrm{C{1}} $ production, as well as more detailed insights into the fundamental basis of such selectivity, remain areas of much interest.

Stabilization of electrode microstructure. It has been established that certain Cu crystal facets and morphologies favor $ \mathrm{C} \geq2 $ products. $ \mathrm{3} $ Surface reorganization during electrocatalysis can affect $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ selectivity. Molecular additives and coatings may serve as ligands to stabilize the surface of the electrode against severe reconstruction (Fig. 4i). $ \mathrm{2 7} $ When the Cu surface was functionalized with polydopamine, surface reorganization was prevented during $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ (Fig. 4j). Polydopamine led to highly stable $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ to $ \mathrm{C H_{4}} $ by Cu over 14 h without a decrease in current density, while $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ by bare Cu ceased within 3 h. $ \mathrm{7 6} $ Amine groups in polydopamine may assist in the protonation of $ ^{*} \mathrm{C O} $ at the Cu surface. Polymer-based metal surface modification thus can contribute not only to stability, but also to high selectivity toward the formation of specific $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ products. Furthermore, for the utilization of discrete Cu active sites displaying mononuclear, dinuclear Cu or oxo-bridged Cu in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ , the development of robust molecular catalysts is desirable for both practical applications and mechanistic studies.

Challenges

第 18 页

At present, most mechanistic studies regarding the role of molecules in $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ using molecularly-augmented heterogeneous catalysts are based on the binding energy between intermediates and the electrode surface, and rely on calculation. To deepen understanding of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ among molecular-augmented heterogeneous catalysts, fundamental studies of the electrochemical electrode/electrolyte interface, electron transfer kinetics, and mass transport of chemical species such as $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ (aq), $ \mathrm{H C O_{3}-}, \mathrm{C O_{32}-}, $ and OH- near the electrode, require experimental elucidation. 77,78 There is a lack of knowledge about the effect of surface coverage, the chemical state of molecules, the real structure of additive molecules during reaction, and the electroactive surface area of additive-modified electrodes. Investigating the effects of molecules on the $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ of heterogeneous catalysts will take into account electron-proton coupled/decoupled pathways, the surface structure of modified electrodes, the interfacial electric field, and transport limitations imposed by the surface molecular layer.

Given the complexity of these systems, gaining of fundamental insight into their function will likely require concerted multidisciplinary efforts including areas of catalysis, synthesis, spectroscopy, and theory. There exist challenges related to lifetime, activity, selectivity, and identity of the active sites. While the ability to tailor the micro-environment (the local environment near the metal active site) of hybrid catalyst structures may lead to improved selectivities and rates through carefully designed interactions, a major concern of such structures is that they can degrade/evolve such that the original rational design has been lost.79 For the stability of heterogenized molecular catalysts, aggregation, leaching, and demetallation must be prevented.13 Covalent grafting by small molecules between an organometallic complex and an electrode can enhance the stability of immobilized organometallic complexes.80 Research on heterogenized molecular catalyst stability will benefit from in-situ/operando spectroscopy. Especially, in-

第 19 页

situ/operando XAS provides a probe of oxidation states and the local coordination environment of the metal center in organometallic complex, and MOF, COFs heterogeneous catalysts during reaction. $ ^{13}C $ labeling experiments must be performed to ensure that the reduced carbon products are derived from $ \mathrm{C O_{2}} $ rather than via degradation of the catalyst or additives. Conductivity is another major challenge that needs to be addressed. Molecular-based approaches can only be effective if the electron from the conductive substrate can travel to the tailored active site.

Conclusion and outlook

This perspective has highlighted the potential of applying molecular strategies to enhance heterogeneous electrochemical $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ . These strategies can, at least in principle, offer precise control of catalyst active sites. The use of well-defined molecules for metal surface modification can control the binding energy of $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ intermediates, and thereby lead to product selectivity control by breaking scaling relations. A distinct advantage of molecular enhancement approaches is that structure-function relationships can be more precisely defined. Such relationships can provide mechanistic insights essential to rational catalyst improvement. Molecularly-enhanced heterogeneous $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ catalysts offer the potential to overcome major challenges in tunability and stability as a frontier opportunity for $ \mathrm{C O_{2} R R} $ electrocatalysis.
