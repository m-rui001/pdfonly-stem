Determination of charge carrier mobilities and trap site densities from SCLC measurements

The architecture is ITO/PEDOT:PSS (60 nm)/PTAA (40 nm)/Perovskite QD film (450-500 nm)/MoO $ {x} $ (2-3 nm)/Ag (100 nm) for hole only device and ITO/SnO $ {x} $ (50 nm)/Perovskite QD film (300-400 nm)/LiF (1-2 nm)/Al (140 nm) for electron only device.

Carrier mobilities values are calculated from the Child region according to Mott-Gurney's equation:

$$ \mu = \left(8 J _ {D} d ^ {3}\right) / \left(9 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V ^ {2}\right) $$

where $ \varepsilon $ is the permittivity. The value we used for mobility calculation is $ J_{D} $ is 0.03-0.07 $ A/cm^{-2} $ $ V=1.2 $ V, d=575 nm for hole devices and 400 nm for electron devices, and $ \varepsilon=4.8 $ for $ CsPbBr_{3} $ nanocrystals $ ^{1} $ . Data from multiple measurements were used for an average value listed in Supplementary Table 1.

As shown in Fig. 3h and Extended Data Fig. 6c, the I-V curve displays three linear regions: the Ohm's law region at low voltage, the trap-filled limit voltage $ \left( V_{TFL} \right) $ , and the Child's regime where all traps are filled. The trap densities for electron and hole can be calculated by the equation:

$$ n _ {t (e / h)} = 2 \varepsilon \varepsilon_ {0} V _ {T F L (e / h)} / \left(e d ^ {2}\right) $$

The values used and results are listed in Supplementary Table 4.

The photocurrent is proportional to the carrier densities and the mobilities. Knowing the carrier lifetime and the excitation density, the mobility values are estimated following the literature $ ^{2} $ as listed in Supplementary Table 1 shown above.

The photocurrent measured here is proportional to current densities, carrier mobilities and carrier lifetimes. The photocurrent-carrier mobility relationship is represented by:

$$ I = q G \left(\tau_ {(e / h)} \mu_ {(e / h)}\right) $$

where I is the photocurrent, q is the elemental charge, $ \tau_{(\mathrm{e / h})}, \mu_{(\mathrm{e / h})} $ are carrier lifetimes and mobilities of electron/hole, respectively. It is worth to note that the values in Supplementary Table 1 were calculated using the carrier lifetime evaluated from optical measurements without electric field. In the photoresistor, the photocurrent was measured under applied field (up to 40 $ \mathrm{V} $ across the electrode distance). Therefore, the real carrier lifetime should be longer, which will result in a smaller mobility value than listed in the table. Therefore, the electron mobility from photocurrent measurements is overestimated.

The hole lifetime, on the other hand, is limited by trapping even under electric field. This leads to a mobility value close to SCLC analyses.

While detailed analysis and discussion is added in the revised manuscript, we briefly discuss the trapping behaviour in our QD solids here. The Spiro and PCBM can almost extract all holes/electrons from the QD solids since PLQY of both covered QD solids dropped to $ \sim 0\% $ . The electron only device showed more than 10 times higher photocurrent under the same excitation density. The possible overestimated electron mobility could come from large absorption of 405 nm excitation light from Spiro layer.

Our bipolar shell strategy allows us to replace surface cation shells with any metal cations. $ \mathrm{P b}^{2+} $ is well known for forming electron deep traps in perovskite materials. Indeed, when the perovskite QD surface is lead-rich, the electron-only photocurrent was dramatically decreased. This consolidates our conclusion that the surface defects were very well passivated by bipolar shells. Additionally, the original QD film shows more than 100 times lower current. This is a result of both insulating long chain ligands and un-passivated traps.

LED model description:

We applied the analytical model to show the impact of carrier mobility on current-density relationships in LEDs $ ^{6,7} $ . The total current is contributed by three terms:

$$ I _ {t o t} = I _ {R} + I _ {s h u n t} + I _ {0} $$

The first term on the right side of equation (1) represents the current in the high bias region (bias $ > $ $ V_{\mathrm{on}} $ (turn-on voltage)) and eventually governed by series resistance effect. The term is given by $ ^{8} $ :

$$ I _ {R} = \frac {n V _ {T}}{R _ {S}} W \left(\frac {I _ {s a t} R _ {S}}{n V _ {T}} \exp \left(\frac {V}{n V _ {T}}\right)\right) $$

where W is the Lambert-W function, n is the ideality factor of the LED, V is the applied bias, $ V_{T} $ is the thermal voltage at room temperature (0.026 V), $ R_{S} $ is the series resistance and $ I_{sat} $ is the reverse bias current which comprised of contributions from both QD layer and PN junction. The $ I_{sat} $ is obtained using the simplified expression:

$$ I _ {s a t} = e A n _ {i} ^ {2} \frac {D}{N _ {x} L _ {x}} $$

where e is the elementary charge, A the area of the LED pixel, $ n_{i} $ is the intrinsic carrier concentration of the semiconductor, $ D=\mu V_{T} $ is the diffusivity, and $ N_{x}, $ $ L_{x} $ are the concentration of the carriers and diffusion length of carries. For perovskites, $ n_{i} $ is $ \sim10^{9} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} $ $ 9. $

The second and third terms on the right side of equation (1) represent: the shunt current $ \mathrm{I_{shunt}} $ caused by carrier mobility imbalance and injection inefficiency; and $ \mathrm{I}{0} $ is a constant contribution to the shunt current. $ \mathrm{I{shunt}} $ is given by:

$$ I _ {s h u n t} = \frac {V}{R _ {s h u n t}} $$

where $ R_{\mathrm{shunt}} $ is the shunt resistance of the diode.

Recombination zone width estimation

We estimated the recombination zone width using the following equation:

$$ W = \mu_ {e f f} \times F \times \tau_ {e - h} $$

where $ \mu_{\mathrm{e f f}} $ is the effective mobility of charge carriers. F and $ \tau_{\mathrm{e - h}} $ are the electric field intensity in LEDs and the carrier lifetime in QD solids. As shown in Supplementary Table 1, the average mobility is $ \sim $ 0.01-0.02 $ \mathrm{c m}^{2} \mathrm{V}^{-1} \mathrm{s}^{-1} $ . The device thickness is around 180 nm and the carrier lifetimes are taken from the PL lifetime measurement ( $ \sim $ 4 ns for 4 nm QD and $ \sim $ 7 ns for 7 nm QD). The recombination zone width is then calculated, and the values are $ \sim $ 71 nm for blue QD solid and 124 nm for green QD solid. Given the possible multi-carrier effect and disorders in the thick film, the actual recombination zone width should be smaller than the calculated values. Therefore, the optimized thickness values of QD solid layers are $ \sim $ 40 nm for blue QDs and 90 nm for green QDs. We have also observed more than 10 times larger current density values in the optimized devices.
