# Correlation of Energy Disorder and Open-circuit Voltage in Hybrid Perovskite Solar Cells

Yuchuan Shao, Yongbo Yuan and Jinsong Huang $ ^{*} $

Department of Mechanical and Materials Engineering, University of Nebraska-Lincoln, Lincoln, Nebraska 68588, USA.

Abstract

Organometal trihalide perovskites have been demonstrated as excellent light absorbers for high efficiency photovoltaic applications. Previous approaches to increasing the solar cell efficiency have focussed on optimisation of the grain morphology of perovskite thin films. Here, we show that the structural order of the electron-transport layers also has a significant impact on solar cell performance. We demonstrate that the power conversion efficiency of $ \mathrm{C H_{3} N H_{3} P b I_{3}} $ planar-heterojunction photovoltaic cells increases from 17.1% to 19.4% when the energy disorder in the fullerene electron-transport layer is reduced by a simple solvent annealing process. The increase in efficiency is the result of the enhancement in open-circuit voltage from 1.04 V to 1.13 V without sacrificing the short-circuit current and fill factor. These results shed light on the origin of open-circuit voltage in perovskite solar cells, and provide a new path to further increase their efficiency.

Introduction

Organometal trihalide perovskite materials have been attracting broad research interests for solar cell applications $ ^{1-9} $ due to many of their intriguing optical and electronic properties such as very large absorption coefficient $ ^{10} $ , large charge carrier mobility $ ^{11,12} $ long carrier recombination lifetime $ ^{12} $ and unique defect physics. $ ^{13} $ Benefiting from these excellent properties, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of perovskite photovoltaic devices has been dramatically improved from 3.8 $ \% $ $ ^{14} $ to a certified 20.1 $ \% $ $ ^{15} $ in less than five years of development. One origin of the high PCE of the perovskite solar cells is their relatively large ratio of the open circuit voltage $ (V_{OC}) $ to bandgap $ (E_{g}) $ of $ \sim 0.69. $ $ ^{16} $ Compared to typical excitonic organic solar cells with about 0.55 $ V_{OC}/E_{g} $ ratio $ ^{16} $ , perovskite solar cells enjoy much less photon energy loss. However, their $ V_{OC}/E_{g} $ ratio is still lower than that of the amorphous silicon and GaAs thin film solar cells of 0.80. $ ^{16} $ Giving the first-order charge-recombination rate in perovskite materials have been shown to be so weak due to the unusual defect physics $ ^{13} $ , there is still a large potential to further increase PCE of perovskite solar cells by boosting $ V_{OC} $ . This is even more urgent for planar heterojunction (PHJ) perovskite cells, which are very simple to be fabricated using the low-temperature solution process, but usually have a lower maximum $ V_{OC} $ than the “inverted” mesoporous-structure devices. $ ^{15,17-19} $ As a matter of fact, it has been demonstrated by both simulation and experimental results that a large degree of energy disorder of the photoactive layer would cause significant drop in $ V_{OC} $ of organic bulk heterojunction solar cells. $ ^{20,21} $ In the last few years of perovskite device research, most previous efforts have been focused on improving the crystallinity, or reducing the energy disorder, of the perovskite thin films. However, barely any attention has been paid to the organic charge transport materials, which inevitably introduce energy disorder into perovskite devices. The low carrier mobility of most

第 3 页

organic charge transport materials, such as $ 2,2^{\prime},7,7^{\prime} $ -tetrakis-(N,Ndi-p methoxyphenylamine)9,9 $ ^{\prime} $ -spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) $ ^{5,6,22} $ , Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) $ ^{19,24,25} $ , and poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)amine] (PTAA) $ ^{17,24} $ indicates they have large degree of energy disorder. The importance of the energy level alignment of the charge-transport layers with perovskite on the $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ of perovskite solar cells has been recognized recently, especially for the large bandgap perovskite solar cells $ ^{23,24} $ . Nevertheless, there is barely any study of the influence of energy disorder in these charge transport layers on the $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ of perovskite solar cells. In order to squeeze out all the potential of perovskite materials for photovoltaics application, it is desirable to have a profound understanding about the detrimental effect of energy disorder of the components other than the perovskite layer, and to find strategies to mitigate the energy disorder in perovskite solar cells.

In this manuscript, we report the influence of energy disorder in fullerene electron transport layer on the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ of the planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells. A simple solvent annealing method is found to mitigate the energy disorder by reducing material structural disorder, which enhances $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ of the $ \mathrm{C H_{3} N H_{3} P b I_{3}} $ PHJ device from 1.04 V to 1.13 V without decreasing the device short circuit current $ \left( \mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{S C}} \right) $ or fill factor (FF). The results highlight the importance of reducing energy disorder of charge carrier transporting layer.

Photovoltaic device structure and performance

The planar heterojunction perovskite solar cells in this study were structured as indium tin oxide (ITO) / PTAA / $ \mathrm{C H_{3} N H_{3} P b I_{3}} $ (550~600 nm) / PCBM (20 nm) / $ \mathrm{C_{60}} $ (20 nm) / 2,9-dimethyl-4,7-diphenyl-1,10-phenanthroline (BCP, 8 nm) / aluminum (Al, 100 nm), as shown in

第 4 页

Figure 1a. The $ \mathrm{M A P b I}{3} $ layers were formed by the interdiffusion of lead iodide $ (\mathrm{P b I}{2}) $ and a methyl ammonium iodide $ (\mathrm{C H}{3} \mathrm{N H}{3} \mathrm{I}, \mathrm{C H}{3} \mathrm{N H}{3}=\mathrm{M A}) $ stacking layer $ ^{2 5} $ followed by a solvent annealing process $ ^{1 0} $ . It forms smooth, compact perovskite films with average grain size much larger than the thin film thickness. $ ^{2 6} $ Then the PCBM layer was spun on top of the perovskite layer, and was thermally annealed at a temperature of $ 1 0 0 \mathrm{~}^\circ \mathrm{C} $ for one hour with or without the presence of dichlorobenzene (DCB) vapor. PCBM layer here acts as not only the electron transport layer, but also a passivation layer to reduce the surface and grain boundary trap density of the perovskite grains $ ^{1 8,2 7} $ . The devices were then finished by thermally evaporating $ \mathrm{C}_{6 0} $ (20 nm), BCP (8 nm), and aluminum (100 nm) layers in the sequential order.

Fig 1b illustrates the principle to enhance $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ of the PHJ perovskite devices by reducing the energy disorder in the electron-transporting layer. The electronic states of the organic PCBM thin films are subject to large energy disorder originated by their structural and chemical disorder, such as low crystallinity and random molecular orientation, interaction with neighboring molecules, impurities, and etc. Here we did not consider the energy disorder in the perovskite layer, because unlike the organic charge-transport materials, the perovskite layer fabricated by the low-temperature solution process still enjoys excellent crystallinity with very low-density trap states and low energy disorder $ ^{10} $ . As a result, perovskite materials have relatively sharp valence band (VB) and conduction band (CB) edges in the density-of-states diagram, which is consistent with the result of external-quantum-efficiency (EQE) measurement. The $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ in a photovoltaic device is determined by the quasi-Fermi level splitting of electrons and holes in the whole device under illumination, which is affected by the occupation of available electronic states by photogenerated charge carriers not only in the perovskite layer, but also in the charge transport layers. The band tail caused by the energy disorder of the PCBM layer that extends into

第 5 页

the forbidden gap introduces additional electronic states. The non-equilibrium photogenerated electrons tend to relax down to these states when they travel through the PCBM layer, which results in reduced quasi-Fermi level splitting and smaller $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ . From a carrier dynamics point of view, the charge transit time through PCBM layer is in the order of 100 ns calculated from its low mobility of $ 10^{-3} $ $ \mathrm{cm^{2}} $ $ V^{-1} $ $ s^{-1} $ $ ^{28} $ which is much longer than the reported electron relaxation time in PCBM layer in order of $ \sim100 $ ps. $ ^{29} $

It is noted that the energy disorder of solid molecules strongly depends on the film preparation process in addition to the intrinsic property of the molecular structure. Therefore, our strategy is to increase the crystallinity to mitigate the energy disorder of PCBM layer, as illustrated in Fig 1c, which narrows down the band tail electronic states and increases the $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ of the perovskite solar cells. To realize this scenario, a 1,2-Dichlorobenzene (DCB) vapor environment was introduced during the PCBM thermal annealing process. DCB has very good solubility to PCBM, but can barely dissolve the hybrid perovskites. The process is similar to the solvent annealing in polymer bulk heterojunction solar cell fabrication. $ ^{30,31} $ Therefore, DCB vapor treatment can improve the crystallinity and decrease energy disorder of PCBM layer without damaging the perovskite layer. In this work, the thermally annealed PCBM layers treated with and without DCB solvent vapor were inferred as "SA PCBM" and "TA PCBM", respectively.

Fig 2a shows the photocurrent curves of the optimized $ \mathrm{M A P b I}{3} $ devices with TA and SA PCBM measured under one simulated sun illumination. After decreasing the energy disorder of PCBM, the device PCE increased from 17.1 % to 19.4 %. The increasing of PCE here mainly comes from the $ \mathrm{V{O C}} $ enhancement from 1.04 V to 1.13 V, while $ \mathrm{J_{S C}} $ and FF remained to be $ \sim 22.6 $ mA $ \mathrm{c m}^{-2} $ and $ \sim 75.0\% $ , respectively. The series resistance for devices with TA and SA PCBM are

  1. 15 $ \Omega $ cm $ ^{2} $ and 3.80 $ \Omega $ cm $ ^{2} $ and the shunt resistance for devices with TA and SA PCBM are 2564 $ \Omega $ cm $ ^{2} $ and 2380 $ \Omega $ cm $ ^{2} $ , respectively. The photocurrents showed negligible hysteresis as shown in Supplementary Figure 1, as a result of the effective deactivation of surface and grain boundary charge traps by the double fullerene layers $ ^{27} $ . Figure 2b showed the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum and integrated $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ for the SA PCBM device. The integrated $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ of 22.3 mA $ \mathrm{cm}^{-2} $ is in good agreement with the $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ from J-V scanning. The steady-state measurements for $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ and PCE were shown in Fig. 2c, which confirmed the device performance parameters extracted from the photocurrent curves and verified the absence of photocurrent hysteresis in our devices. The statistics of $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ distribution shown in Fig 2d demonstrates the reliability and the repeatability of the $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ enhancement by the DCB vapor-treatment induced energy-disorder reduction in the PCBM layer.

Energy disorder characterization

To verify that DCB vapor treatment can enhance the crystallinity of PCBM, we conducted X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement of the spun PCBM films on glass substrate with and without DCB treatment. Figure 3a clearly show that a sharp diffraction peak at $ 2 0. 5^{\circ} $ appeared in the XRD spectrum of the SA PCBM thin film, while there is barely any diffraction peak in the TA PCBM film, which supports the conclusion of enhanced crystallization of PCBM by $ \mathrm{S A}^{3 2}. $ The absorption spectra of SA and TA PCBM were also measured. The slightly enhanced absorption also supports the better crystallinity in the ordered PCBM thin films. There is no obvious morphology difference between SA and TA PCBM on perovskite layer as shown in Supplementary Figure 2.

We also directly characterize the energy disorder in the whole device by measuring the distribution of electronic density of states (DOS) to confirm that the enhanced PCBM structure order can reduce energy disorder. The distribution of density of states is often described by a Gaussian shape in organic semiconductors $ ^{33} $

$$ g _ {n} \left(E - E _ {L}\right) = \frac {N _ {n}}{\sqrt {2 \pi} \sigma_ {n}} \exp \left[ - \frac {\left(E _ {L} - E\right) ^ {2}}{2 \sigma_ {n} ^ {2}} \right] $$

where $ N_{n} $ is the total density per unit volume, $ E_{L} $ is the energy center of the DOS, and $ \sigma_{n} $ is the disorder parameter which presents the broadening of the DOS. Here we investigated the variation of DOS distribution by extracting the chemical capacitance $ (C_{\mu}) $ from the impedance spectroscopy. It has been established by J. Bisquert et al that the chemical capacitance in this type of measurement is proportional to DOS, since $ C_{\mu} $ reflect the capability of a photovoltaic device to accept or release the additional charge carriers due to the shift of the quasi-Fermi level $ ^{34} $ . When the quasi-Fermi level move across these electronic states, the releasing of occupied charge is recorded as changing of chemical capacitance, which provides a perfect tool to study the DOS distribution in the device in this case. The measurement details can be found in the Supplementary Note 1. The fitting of the Gaussian DOS for perovskite solar cell devices with TA and SA PCBM films is shown in Fig 3a. The experiment data were well fitted by equation (1) for all the devices measured. The disorder parameter $ \sigma_{n} $ decreased from 136 meV to 90 meV after replacing the TA PCBM with SA counterpart. Since the perovskite layer is very crystalline, the measured energy disorder should be dominated by the contribution form the PCBM layer, and the measured $ \sigma_{n} $ variation should reflect the degree of disorder of PCBM layer by the different PCBM treatments. The direct observation of energy-disorder reduction in the device with SA PCBM well supported our proposed scenario. A $ \sigma_{n} $ of 73 meV has been reported in the pure PCBM devices according to disordered hopping transporting models $ ^{35} $ . The relatively larger

第 8 页

$ \sigma_{n} $ in our devices may origin from the additional electronic interactions between PCBM and the perovskite layer, or the disorder contribution of the thermally-evaporated $ C_{60} $ layer. We also measured the energy disorder of the perovskite solar cells with indene- $ C_{60} $ bisadduct (ICBA) layer, which was broadly applied in organic solar cells for a larger $ V_{OC} $ , because ICBA has a higher lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level than that of PCBM. Replacing PCBM by ICBA indeed increased the $ V_{OC} $ of the perovskite/fullerene hybrid Schottky junction devices. $ ^{19} $ In those devices, the perovskite layers were not continuous, and the direct contact of fullerenes with hole transport layer formed Schottky junction. $ ^{19} $ However, a smaller PCE of 12.8 % and $ V_{OC} $ of 0.97 V were obtained using ICBA (Supplementary Figure 3) $ ^{36} $ here. The unexpected results could be well explained by the much larger energy disorder of ICBA layer than PCBM when eventually reduced the quasi Fermi energy of electrons in ICBA.

Another method to study the influence of energy-disorder on $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ is to measure the density of photogenerated charges that is needed to produce a given quasi-Fermi level splitting. The combination of transient photovoltage spectroscopy (TPV) and transient photocurrent spectroscopy (TPC) method was established to determine the generated carrier concentration before they recombine. $ ^{20} $ This is also enabled by the much longer carrier recombination lifetime demonstrated in hybrid perovskite materials than the charge extraction time out of the device. $ ^{26,27} $ The details about the measurement of the TPV and TPC method are in the Supplementary Note 2. The measured results in Figure 3d clearly show that a larger carrier concentration of carriers is needed to fill the low energy levels in the device with TA PCBM to reach the same $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ than the device with SA PCBM, indicating the effectiveness of our solvent annealing method for energy disorder mitigation. In addition, at the same generated charge density of $ 3 \times 1 0^{1 7} \mathrm{c m}^{-3} $ , for instance, the devices with TA and SA PCBM have a $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ of 0.99 V and 1.07 V, respectively. This result is

第 9 页

also consistent with our scenario that the same charge generation density (or light intensity) could generate larger $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ in the devices with mitigated energy disorder.

Besides the degree of energy disorder, the charge carrier recombination dynamics in a solar cell can also impact the device $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ because the change of charge carrier recombination rate would cause the variation of equilibrium charge carrier concentration, the quasi-Fermi level splitting and thus the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ in the photovoltaic devices. However, in our study, the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ enhancement is not related to the change of charge recombination dynamics, because no evidence of the change of the recombination lifetime was found in the TPV measurements as shown in Fig 4a and 4b. The charge recombination lifetimes for devices with SA and TA PCBM are the same under any given illumination light intensity and charge carrier density. A simulation also supports the scenario as shown in Supplementary Note 3. Furthermore, the unchanged recombination rate reveals that the deeper electronic states induced by the energy disorder of TA PCBM do not act as recombination centers. Although the photogenerated electrons relax to the lower energy states, the electrons would not be trapped there which otherwise would cause reduced $ J_{\mathrm{S C}} $ and FF in devices with different PCBM treatments. The "cooled" electrons in the lower energy states can still efficiently transport out of PCBM layer by hopping to the cathode. Therefore, these lower energy electron states do not act as "traps" and have negligible influence on the charge extraction efficiency.

PCBM thickness dependent device performances

Finally, the PCBM thickness dependent device performances were examined as shown in Figure 5a and 5b. The thickness of TA and SA PCBM layer varied from 20 nm to 60 nm by

第 10 页

tuning the concentration of PCBM solution and spin-coating rate. The $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ of all the devices with SA PCBM is higher than that of the devices with TA PCBM regardless of the PCBM thickness, which demonstrates the effectiveness of the reduced energy disorder to enhance device $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ . As the PCBM thickness increased, the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ of devices with both SA and TA PCBM almost remain invariant while the FF reduced dramatically. The different variation trend of $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ and FF reflect that the change of series resistance of the electron transport layer, which was in accordance with the expectation since the PCBM thickness increase, only have now impact to device $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ . Thus, a thin electron transport layer is beneficial for reducing series resistance, prompting charge extraction and device performance, which is of significant importance for PCBM given its relatively low mobility.

Conclusion

We have revealed and highlighted the importance of energy disorder mitigation in electron transport layers on improving the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ in the perovskite solar cells. The enhancement of PCBM structural ordering effectively reduces the energy disorder, which can be deduced from the significantly decrease of DOS and disorder parameters. A recent independent theoretical work investigating energy disorder in fullerenes reported the same disorder parameter for ordered and disordered PCBM, which is in excellent agreement with our experimental results. $ ^{37} $ This technique presents an effective method to increase $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ without scarifying $ J_{\mathrm{S C}} $ or FF. Most importantly, a new direction has been pointed out to further increase the PCE of the perovskite solar cells by designing and application of new electron transport materials with even smaller energy disorder than SA PCBM. We speculate that a reduction of the energy disorder for hole transport layer would also have an impact to the $ V_{\mathrm{O C}} $ of perovskite solar cells. In addition,

selecting charge transporting materials carefully and optimizing the interfaces between the perovskite layer and the charge transporting layers can potentially decrease the interfacial energy disorder to obtain a larger open circuit voltage.

Methods

Device fabrication. The hole transporting layer, PTAA film was prepared by spin-coating 0.5 wt $ \% $ PTAA solution with 6,000 rpm, and the as prepared film thermally annealed at $ 110^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $ for 10 min. The $ \mathrm{M A P b I}{3} $ films were fabricated by solvent annealing induced interdiffusion method $ ^{27} $ . The film fabrication procedures are the same with previous studies, while $ \mathrm{P b I}{2} $ layer on PTAA should be spin-coated from $ 100^{\circ} \mathrm{C} $ pre-heated $ \mathrm{P b I}{2} $ DMF solution. To fabricate 500 nm thick $ \mathrm{M A P b I}{3} $ film, 650 mg ml-1 $ \mathrm{P b I}{2} $ DMF precursor were used with 75 mg ml-1 methylammonium iodide (MAI) 2-propanol precursor solution. After they were cooled down to room temperature, PCBM (dissolved into Dichlorobenzene (DCB), 2 wt %) was spun on top of the formed perovskite layers. After that, for PCBM treated with solvent annealing, the devices were put on top of a hot plate and covered by a glass petri dish. 10 $ \mu\mathrm{L} $ of DCB solvent was added at the edge of the petri dish during the thermal annealing process, so that the DCB vapor could go into the Petri dish and make contact with the PCBM layer. For control devices with thermal annealing PCBM, no DCB solvent was added during the thermal annealing process. The devices were finished by thermal evaporating $ \mathrm{C}{60} $ (20 nm), BCP (8 nm), and aluminum (100 nm) in the sequential order. The device working area was $ 8 \mathrm{~mm}^{2} $ , defined by the overlap of ITO substrate and Al cathode.

Device Characterization. Simulated AM 1.5G irradiation (100 mW/cm $ ^{2} $ ) was produced by a Xenon-lamp-based solar simulator (Oriel 67005, 150 W Solar Simulator) for current (I)-voltage (V) measurement. The light intensity was calibrated by a silicon (Si) diode (Hamamatsu S1133) equipped with a Schott visible-color glass-filtered (KG5 color-filtered). Keithley 2400 Source-Meter was used for I-V measurement. The scanning rate was $ 0.1 \mathrm{~V} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} $ . The steady-state $ V_{\mathrm{OC}} $ and $ J_{\mathrm{SC}} $ were measured by zero bias current and zero bias voltage versus time, respectively. The steady-state PCE was measured by monitoring current with $ 0.91 \mathrm{~V} $ bias voltage and then

第 13 页

producted the measured current with 0.91 V bias voltage to obtain the steady-state PCE. XRD measurements were performed with a Rigaku D/Max-B X-ray diffractometer with Bragg-Brentano parafocusing geometry, a diffracted beam monochromator, and a conventional cobalt target X-ray tube set to 40 kV and 30 mA. The single path absorption was measured using an Evolution 201 UV-Visible spectrometer (Thermo Scientific).
